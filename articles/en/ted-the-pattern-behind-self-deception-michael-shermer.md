# Content

- By default, humans are wired to believe — skepticism and scientific thinking require more effort and are harder to maintain.
- This tendency has evolutionary roots: ancestors who assumed a rustling in the bushes was a predator (rather than just the wind) were more likely to survive, even if they were wrong most of the time.
- The brain has two key hard-wired survival skills that drive belief in strange things: finding patterns and attributing agency.
- Dopamine plays a role in pattern recognition — it amplifies the perception of patterns. Cocaine raises dopamine levels (intensifying pattern-finding), while some antipsychotic medications lower them.
- These mental shortcuts that helped our ancestors survive are the same mechanisms that lead modern humans to believe in alien abductions, dowsing rods, and other unfounded ideas.
