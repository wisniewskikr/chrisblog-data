## 1. How rational an animal is the human being?

- Rationality is a set of cognitive tools that allow us to pursue goals under specific conditions.
- People are more rational when information is vivid and relevant to their lives.
- The difference between science and pseudoscience: science seeks evidence to falsify hypotheses, not to confirm them.
- The Monty Hall problem: three doors, one hiding a car and the others goats. When the host opens one door, you should switch your choice — the answer is: yes.
- This is a confusion of probability with prediction; prediction is a tendency to behave in a certain way — Monty gives us a clue.
- The conjunction rule: the probability of two events occurring together must be less than or equal to the probability of either one occurring separately.

## 2. Rationality and irrationality

- Rationality is the ability to use knowledge to achieve goals.
- Knowledge is a justified true belief.
- Two kinds of reason:
  - Theoretical reason: confirming the truth of ideas.
  - Practical reason: bringing about change in reality.
- Goals are set by emotions, and reason should pursue them.
- A single person's goals can be varied and often contradictory, e.g. the taste of cheesecake versus health.
- Ultimate motivations: survival and reproduction.
- Proximate motivations: hunger, love, fear, comfort, sex, power, status.
- Chronological conflicts: a smaller reward now versus a larger one later — depends on the nature of the later reward and the price of risk.
- Myopic conflicts: we make more objective decisions over the long term than the short term, e.g. choosing a healthy versus a fattening meal.
- Ulyssean self-control: shopping on a full stomach, instructing an employer to deduct savings contributions automatically.
- Libertarian paternalism: the state ties us to the mast, but we can untie ourselves.
- Sometimes ignorance is rational, e.g. not knowing the ending of a film, or the placebo effect.
- Rational incapacity: sometimes it is better to be unable to do something, e.g. being unable to lower a price at a market because the boss has forbidden it.
- Rational irrationality: e.g. "I won't look for someone else, because I irrationally love you."
- Taboos: a refusal to analyse certain scenarios, e.g. selling a child or one's kidneys.
- Morality depends on rationality at a given moment.
- The foundations of morality: self-interest and social instinct combined with impartiality — the interchangeability of perspectives.

## 3. Logic and critical thinking

- The schema of reasoning: premises + rules = conclusions.
- Types of argument:
  - Valid: correct rules but false premises.
  - Sound: correct rules and correct premises.

## 4. Probability and randomness

- A common error: confusing a non-random pattern with a non-random process.

## A man should be willing, when others are also willing, to lay down his right to all things and be contented with so much liberty against other men as he would allow other men against himself.

- The quote describes the principle of reciprocity: a willingness to limit one's own freedom to the same degree that one expects of others.
