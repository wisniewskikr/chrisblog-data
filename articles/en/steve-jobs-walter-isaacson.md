# Content

## 1. Childhood. Abandoned and chosen

- Father Paul Jobs, mother Clara Hagopian; after World War II Paul bet his friends he could find a wife within 2 weeks – and won.
- Clara had an ectopic pregnancy and could not have children.
- Steven Paul Jobs was adopted from a Syrian man and an American woman; later they also adopted a girl named Patty.
- He grew up in Mountain View and helped his father work on cars.
- Eichler homes – affordable and solid – became a design inspiration for Jobs.
- They lived surrounded by modern defence-industry companies; Terman, dean of Stanford, established a technology park in the area.
- An integrated circuit is made of transistors built from semiconductors; germanium was used first, then silicon.
- Moore's Law: the number of transistors doubles every two years.
- Silicon Valley stretches from San Francisco through Palo Alto to San Jose.
- Jobs was bored at school, worked after classes, and experimented with drugs.

## 2. The odd couple. Two Steves

- Jobs attended McCollum's electronics class for one year; there he met Steven Wozniak, five years his senior.
- Wozniak's father was an engineer; Wozniak designed computers, spent a year at the University of Colorado, then transferred to De Anza, and later to Berkeley.
- Jobs and Wozniak were brought together by a shared fascination with electronics and music.
- Together they created the Blue Box, which enabled free long-distance calls; they sold 100 units.

## 3. Rejection. Turn on, tune in ...

- Jobs began dating Chrisann Brennan; they moved into a cabin together.
- He enrolled at Reed College, a private liberal-arts school, where he became involved in Zen Buddhism and vegetarianism.
- Friedman became Jobs's guru and taught him charisma.
- Jobs dropped out but was allowed to attend classes as a non-enrolled auditor; he walked barefoot and lived in a dormitory room.

## 4. Atari and India. Zen and the art of game design

- After dropping out, Jobs went to work at Atari; because he did not bathe he was assigned to night shifts.
- Atari paid for his trip to India, where he solved a technical problem; once there he contracted dysentery and spent 7 months in the country.
- After returning he went back to Atari; Wozniak was working nearby at HP.
- Together they built a Breakout game using fewer than 50 integrated circuits in 4 days; Jobs did not share the bonus with Wozniak.

## 5. Apple I. Turn on, boot up, jack in ...

- Jobs meditated in the morning, attended physics lectures at Stanford at noon, and worked at Atari in the evening.
- The first personal computer, the Altair, ran on an Intel 8080 processor.
- At the Homebrew Computer Club the idea of a personal computer occurred to Wozniak; he built it after hours at HP; Gates also attended the club.
- On 29 June 1975, for the first time someone pressed keys and letters appeared on a screen.
- Jobs persuaded Wozniak to start a company; they sold their possessions and raised starting capital of $1,300.
- Jobs came up with the name Apple while on an apple diet and returning from an orchard.
- Ron Wayne, a friend of Jobs, received a 10% stake and was to arbitrate disputes between the partners.
- On 1 April 1976 they founded Apple as a partnership with a split of 45%, 45%, and 10%; expenses above $100 required the approval of at least two partners.
- Wayne withdrew a few days later, fearing another business failure; in 2010 his stake would have been worth $2.6 billion.
- Terrel placed the first order for 50 boards at $500 each – this was the Apple I, assembled in Jobs's garage.

## 6. Apple II. Dawn of a new era

- At the first personal computer festival in Atlantic City, Jobs understood that a computer should be a fully integrated whole.
- Manock designed the casing for $1,500; Rod invented a switching power supply.
- Markkula helped write the business plan and contributed $250,000; each of the three partners held 26% of the shares.
- On 3 January 1977 Apple Computer Co. was incorporated; the company's three pillars: empathy, focus, and impute (people judge a book by its cover).
- McKenna handled marketing; at the trade show in San Francisco they collected 300 orders.
- They moved from the garage to an office and hired Mike Scott to manage Jobs.

## 7. Chrisann and Lisa. The one they abandoned ...

- Jobs had a daughter with Chrisann Brennan – Lisa Nicole.
- After a paternity test the court ordered him to pay child support.

## 8. Xerox and Lisa. Graphical user interfaces

- Apple II sales: 2,500 units in 1977; 210,000 units in 1981.
- Apple III was a failure – Jobs designed the casing and refused to allow changes, so the boards were crammed too tightly.
- Jobs exchanged some Apple shares for access to Xerox PARC technologies: GUI, bitmaps, and the Smalltalk language.
- Xerox PARC solutions were used in the Lisa project; a mouse was developed; Atkinson was the main programmer.
- Because Jobs interfered everywhere, Scott and Markkula stripped him of executive authority over the Lisa project.

## 9. Going public. Famous and rich

- In 1980 Apple went public, achieving a valuation of nearly $2 billion.
- Jobs was disloyal to colleagues; Wozniak was extremely loyal and sold his shares to those who had deserved them.

## 10. The birth of the Mac. You say you want a revolution

- The Mac takes its name from Raskin's favourite apple variety; Raskin was secretly working on the project; a confrontation with Jobs arose because Raskin wanted to use a low-powered processor.
- Wozniak crashed during a take-off attempt in a small plane, suffered partial amnesia, and returned to finish his degree at Berkeley.

## 11. The reality distortion field. Playing by his own rules

- Jobs deceived himself, which made him convincing to others, and he was able to find weak spots in his interlocutors.
- He regarded himself as an artist and inspired others to think the same way.

## 12. Design. Real artists simplify

- Sony's design philosophy: black, industrial.
- Bauhaus design philosophy: "God is in the details", "less is more".
- Jobs explained to the engineers what it meant for the Macintosh to be user-friendly.
- Jobs was intensely focused on fonts.
- The Macintosh was developed by a team of 45 people.

## 13. Building the Mac. The journey is the reward

- In 1981 IBM released its personal computer.
- The Mac also competed against the Lisa.
- The Lisa was released but failed after two years.

## 14. Here comes Sculley – the Pepsi Challenge

- For Mac marketing Jobs hired Sculley, who had previously worked at Pepsi.
- Marketing costs were built into the price of the Mac, causing it to rise significantly.

## 15. Launch. A dent in the universe

- IBM launched earlier and thereby gained a dominant market position.
- For the Mac launch they created what is considered the greatest advertisement ever made, directed by Ridley Scott – the director of Blade Runner.
- The Mac officially launched on 24 January 1984.

## 16. Gates and Jobs. When orbits intersect

- Bill Gates was born in 1955, the same year as Jobs; his father was a lawyer and his mother a civic activist; he attended the best private school in the area.
- In high school Gates wrote programmes, including one for his school; he went to Harvard but dropped out to found a software company.
- He started by writing BASIC for the Altair, then came DOS, and later Windows, based on the Mac's GUI.
- Gates's company wrote Excel and BASIC for the Mac.

## 17. Icarus. What goes up must come down

- Mac sales began to fall – it was impressive but underpowered and slow: too little RAM, no hard drive, no fan.
- In 1985 an advertisement insulting to managers was aired (featuring the lemming motif), very grim in tone.
- Jobs began quarrelling with Sculley; the board removed him from his role as head of the Mac division.

## 18. NeXT. Prometheus unbound

- Jobs founded a new company producing more powerful computers for universities; he took 5 key Apple employees with him, enraging the company.
- Perot invested in NeXT.
- NeXT computers were very expensive and very late to market.

## 19. Pixar. Technology meets art

- In 1986 Jobs bought Pixar – the computer division of George Lucas's film studio – for $10 million, acquiring a 70% stake.
- The hardware, software, and animated content divisions all ran at a loss.
- In 1988 the short animated film Tin Toy won an Academy Award.

## 20. Regular guy. Love is just a four-letter word

- Jobs was romantically involved with: Barbara Jasinski, Joan Baez, Jennifer Egan, Tina Redse, and Laurene Powell.
- He made contact with his biological family: his mother Joanne Simpson and his sister Mona Simpson.
- He dined at a restaurant run by his biological father, Abdulfattah Jandali, and even met him without knowing who he was.
- He gradually developed a better relationship with his daughter Lisa Brennan.
- In 1991 he married Laurene Powell; they have three children: Reed, Erin, and Eve.

## 21. Toy Story. Buzz and Woody to the rescue

- Toy Story was a joint production of Disney and Pixar.
- After its release Pixar went public, achieving a valuation of $1.2 billion.

## 22. The second coming. And what rough beast, its hour come round at last ...

- Apple considered two operating systems: NeXT and Be.
- Apple and NeXT merged.

## 23. The restaurant. Because he who is down today ...

- Jobs forced Amelio out as CEO and also compelled board members to resign.
- He ended the war with Microsoft: Gates's company continued to produce Word and Excel for the Mac and invested in Apple.

## 24. Think different. Jobs as i-CEO

- Jobs introduced the famous advertising slogan "Think different".
- He reduced the product line to four products: for professional and ordinary users, in desktop and portable versions.

## 25. Design principles. The studio of Jobs and Ive

- Jonathan Ive was the head of the design team.
- At Apple, engineering is subordinate to design; at most other companies it is the other way around.

## 26. iMac. Hello (again)

- The iMac consisted only of a monitor integrated with the computer, a keyboard, and a mouse; it was translucent; it launched in 1998.
- Jobs ordered the CD tray removed, which meant the computer lost the ability to burn CDs – despite being warned about this.

## Chapter 27. Chief executive officer. After all these years - still quite a character

- Jobs and Tim Cook together created just-in-time supply chains.
- Jobs owned about 100 turtlenecks that he wore regularly.
- As interim CEO, Jobs earned $2.50.
- Jobs received a Gulfstream V jet.
- Jobs became CEO, dropping the "interim" prefix.

## Chapter 28. Apple stores. Genius bars and sandstone from Siena

- Jobs wanted to have his own chain of stores.
- He built a mock-up store near the Apple campus and spent a lot of time there; the floors were laid with stone from Florence.
- The store openings proved to be a success.

## Chapter 29. The digital hub. From iTunes to the iPod

- A new idea emerged: the computer should serve as a hub for multiple peripheral devices.
- Key programs and devices of this period: iMovie, iTunes, iPod.

## Chapter 30. The iTunes store. The pied piper

- Jobs created the iTunes Store, where a song could be purchased for 99 cents.
- Jobs created the iPod and iTunes for Windows.
- Apple released the iPod Mini and iPod Shuffle.

## Chapter 31. Music man. The soundtrack of his life

- Bob Dylan appeared in an Apple advertisement; now it was artists who sought out such collaborations.
- For U2, Apple created a special U2 iPod.

## Chapter 32. Pixar's friends... and enemies

- A rivalry over insect films: Pixar released "A Bug's Life" while DreamWorks released "Antz".
- Pixar's next film was "Toy Story II".
- Jobs designed Pixar's new headquarters with a single atrium in the centre; it also featured a Love Bar.
- Subsequent films included "Monsters, Inc.", "Finding Nemo", "The Incredibles", and "Cars".
- Disney bought Pixar for 7 billion dollars; Jobs was also to receive 7% of Disney's shares.

## Chapter 33. The Mac of the 21st century. The distinctiveness of Apple

- Apple switched from Motorola processors to Intel processors.
- Jobs was accused of backdating stock options.

## Chapter 34. Round one. Memento mori

- Jobs believed his cancer had developed during an exhausting year when he was running both Pixar and Apple.
- The cancer was detected early during a kidney examination, but Jobs treated himself naturally for 9 months; he eventually underwent surgery, but metastases appeared.
- Jobs delivered a commencement speech at Stanford.

## Chapter 35. iPhone. Three revolutionary designs in one

- Apple began work on multi-touch technology and acquired FingerWorks, a company already working on it.
- Gorilla Glass was developed, followed by Godzilla Glass.

## Chapter 36. Round two. The return of cancer

- In 2008 Jobs's cancer returned and he lost a great deal of weight.
- Jobs registered on transplant waiting lists in two different states – this is not illegal, but requires wealth (the need to reach a hospital within 8 hours, hence a private jet).
- Jobs received a transplant.

## Chapter 37. iPad. The post-PC era

- The iPad used ARM processors instead of Intel.
- The iPad launched on the market.
- Applications were available in the App Store.
- The iBook Store launched with books sold under an agency model: publishers set their own prices and Apple takes 30%.
- The iTunes Store maintained a low, fixed price of 99 cents per song.

## Chapter 38. New battles... and echoes of old ones

- Apple accused Google of using their solutions in Android.
- Antennagate: in the iPhone 4, surrounding the phone with metal caused connectivity problems – affecting 1 in 100 devices.

## Chapter 39. To the end of the world. The cloud, the spaceship, and beyond

- A magnetic case was created for the iPad 2.
- Jobs was designing his own yacht.
- Apple released iCloud – a service that synchronises everything through the cloud.
- Apple purchased land from HP and designed a new headquarters for the company.

## Chapter 40. Round three. Struggles at dusk

- Jobs wanted to live to see his son Reed finish high school.
- Jobs met with Obama several times.
- In 2011 the cancer returned; Jobs underwent therapy with targeted drugs.
- Jobs resigned as CEO of Apple.

## Chapter 41. Legacy. The greatest paradise of invention

- Jobs adhered to a hero–bozo philosophy.
- He was capable of very intense focus – this produced both good and bad results.
- He was brutally honest.
- He possessed magical leaps of imagination.
- He sought the intersection of the humanities and the sciences.

## Epilogue

- Jobs died on 5 October 2011.
