# Content

## I. The phenomenon of high sensitivity

### 1. Masculinity in a time of change

- Women experience depression more often; men more often die by suicide.
- Men die earlier than women due to their lifestyle – data from monastic life serves as evidence.
- Flexibility toward current masculine norms can be beneficial.
- Showing emotions – except for anger – is stigmatized for men.

## 2. Understanding high sensitivity

- Humans have an innate temperament – this is visible even in infants.
- Temperament forms the foundation of personality.
- Introverts are directed inward (so-called dandelion children), extroverts outward (so-called orchid children).
- High sensitivity applies to both negative and positive events; sensitive children develop better than extroverts in a favorable environment.
- High sensitivity is a temperament trait, while introversion and extroversion are personality styles that can be developed.
- 30% of highly sensitive people are extroverts.

## 3. Recognizing high sensitivity

- Characteristics of high sensitivity:
  - Depth of processing
  - Overstimulation
  - Emotional intensity
  - Sensory sensitivity

## 4. Distinguishing high sensitivity

- Acceptance is key – it is the starting point for change.
- Highly sensitive men must pay particular attention to self-soothing and emotional regulation.

## II. Living (well) with high sensitivity

### 5. Strategies for coping with arousal

- It is important to be able to regulate emotions: identify them, understand their cause, observe without judging.
- Basic emotions: joy, surprise, sadness, anger, contempt, disgust.
- Emotions manifest on four levels: thoughts, feelings, body, behavior.

## 6. Mindfulness and acceptance

- Mindfulness means consciously, without criticism or judgment, paying full attention to what is happening around and within us – in body, heart, and mind.

## 7. Relaxation and imagination

- Feelings affect the body, and the body affects feelings.
- Relaxation techniques: bath, massage, walk.
- Imagination can also be used by picturing a calm place or a trusted person.
- Other techniques: body scan, progressive muscle relaxation, imagining a safe place.

## 8. Relationship with oneself

- There is a clear link between mental health and self-compassion.
- Three human emotional systems:
  - Threat system – active when we face danger
  - Drive system – active when we desire something
  - Soothing system – active when the other two systems are not driving us

## 9. Self-esteem

- Self-confidence means evaluating oneself positively, being aware of one's own worth, and believing in one's abilities.
- Pillars of self-esteem:
  - Self-acceptance
  - Self-confidence
  - Social competence
  - Social network
