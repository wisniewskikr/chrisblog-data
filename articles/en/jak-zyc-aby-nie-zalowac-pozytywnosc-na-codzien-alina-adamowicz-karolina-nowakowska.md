# Content

## 1. Happiness as a choice. Positivity to start with
- We learn to complete tasks – that is the path to efficiency – but we do not learn emotional intelligence, which is the path to fulfilment.
- Happiness is a state of mind that comes and goes; it is both possible and necessary to work on it.
- Areas to work on: attitude to life, thoughts, control of emotions, the way we interpret reality, not worrying about things beyond our control, lifestyle.
- The brain is plastic, which is why we can build happiness through our own efforts.
- Research shows that 40% of happiness depends on you.
- We are genetically and socially motivated to notice the bad more than the good.
- Mental hygiene: at the end of the day it is worth looking back and noting what went well.
- Happiness should not be confused with pleasure or social pressure.
- BDNF – a protein that restores balance.
- Every hour of physical activity extends life by an average of 2–5 hours.

## 2. Loving egoism. The art of self-acceptance
- Self-worth: the conviction that you are good enough just as you are – it concerns who you are.
- Self-confidence: faith in yourself in specific situations – it concerns what you can do.
- Egoism: a life attitude characterised by care for one's own well-being.

## 3. What can be changed, and what cannot?
- Only half of personality traits are hereditary.
- We often confuse sacrifice with love, and a sense of duty with identity.
- Research shows that 90% of the things we fear will never happen.
- Supportive habits: speaking well of yourself, hugging yourself, praising yourself, practising gratitude, engaging in physical activity (e.g. dancing).

## 4. Balance in life
- In order to improve something, you first need to know what the current state is.

## 5. Negative positivity. When pretend happiness causes harm
- Negative positivity is the pressure to think positively at all costs and not to admit sadness, anger or regret.
- True strength does not lie in pretending everything is fine, but in telling the truth.
- Avoiding emotions is avoiding life itself – not courage, but disconnection.
- Psychological resilience lies not in ignoring difficult states, but in the ability to experience and process them.

## 6. How to bring change into your life?
- Heraclitus of Ephesus: nothing is more constant than change.
- Change should be introduced courageously, in small steps and consistently.
- Change consists of six stages: 1. Precontemplation – lack of awareness of the problem; 2. Contemplation – considering change; 3. Preparation – decision and planning; 4. Action – actually implementing change; 5. Maintenance – consolidating change; 6. Resolution.
- Kaizen – the Japanese method of small steps: take small steps, dedicate only a few minutes a day, give yourself small rewards, solve small problems.
- Three key aspects of change: coaching (a 3–5 year perspective), consistency and commitment in action, a supportive community.

## 7. Life without regret. The courage to be yourself
- When you live to gain others' acceptance, you begin to lose yourself.
- Courage means: perseverance when no one sees your effort, honesty with yourself, acting against the trend, saying "no" when others say "yes".

## 8. Those who laugh, live. Even if sometimes it is laughter through tears
- Laughter has a real impact on emotions, relationships and the body.

## 9. Mindfulness
- Relationships are very important, and the key to them is honesty and openness – you cannot build a relationship while wearing a mask.
- Being active in relationships means focusing on what you like and overlooking what you do not.
- When caring for relationships, you should start by taking care of yourself.

## 10. Programme yourself for a better life
- Positivity is the choice to notice not only what hurts, but also what supports you.
- A positive person does not suppress difficulties, but is also able to see light and a wealth of possible solutions.

## Summary
- To feel peace and fulfilment, you need to experience positive states three times more often than negative ones – that is how we are biologically programmed.
