# Content

## Introduction

- In wealthy societies basic needs are already met – we are driven by what we want, and that requires making choices.
- Once basic needs are satisfied, the brain turns toward new experiences, challenges, relationships, and creative expression.
- It is worth having a consciously planned way of being.
- Understanding the 10 basic human drives is key.
- The drives are divided into baseline and expansive.

## 1. From darkness into light

- The internal charge has two dimensions: quality (positive or negative) and intensity (high or low).
- Ideally, one's internal charge should be positive and very strong.
- Three types of life: limited life, comfortable life, fully charged life.
- Limited life: a person is imprisoned by their own barriers and limitations; the world is seen as threatening and dangerous; escape is possible through crisis or a conscious decision.
- Comfortable life: a person is stuck in a rut; the world feels barren and stagnant; the person seeks a sense of belonging and satisfaction.
- Fully charged life: a person strives to realize their potential; the world is magical and full of meaning; the person craves challenges, savors the journey, and constantly looks for opportunities to change and grow.
- Traits of a charger:
  - Alert and focused on the present moment.
  - Future-oriented.
  - Seeks challenges.
  - Genuinely interested in others and feels an authentic, close bond with those around them.
  - Self-reliant.
  - Strives for creative self-expression.
  - Gives meaning and significance to what they do.

## Part I. The five baseline drives

### 2. The drive for control

- The golden mean is key – neither too little nor too much control.
- Activator 1 – Control your attitude and character: most life events are random; we can only control our reaction to them; research confirms the advantage of optimism; an optimist is not a dreamer but someone who believes in the possibility of action and acts.
- Activator 2 – Control what is new: it is worth strategically introducing novelty and new experiences to avoid boredom and depression; six ways: breaking from routine every 90 days, visiting a café or restaurant, attending exhibitions and events, adventures during travel, expanding one's social circle, learning and developing new skills.
- Activator 3 – Control work and professional life: two main problems are the lack of a sense of ownership (it is worth being involved in a project from start to finish) and distraction from emails and messages.

## 3. The drive for competence

- There is a feedback loop between competence and self-confidence.
- Competence is the ability to understand the world, master it, and act within it.
- Competence level determines: what you pay attention to, what tasks you take on, how much you give of yourself, how persistent and flexible you are, whether you lead or follow others.
- Activator 1 – Assess and consciously manage your motivation to learn: first establish why you want to learn and what could prevent you from achieving it; intrinsic motivation is more effective than extrinsic.
- Activator 2 – Take on real, concrete challenges, aim for success, and seek coaching support.
- Activator 3 – Integrate success into your identity.

## 4. The drive for congruence

- Congruence means consistency; a person feels bad when inconsistent in thoughts and behavior.
- Activator 1 – Set new standards for yourself: the past is often used as an excuse rather than an explanation; self-image is a self-fulfilling prophecy, so focus on the future; it helps to identify three words describing inner traits and three describing outer traits.
- Activator 2 – Calibrate your mood sensor: congruence also concerns feeling; a power plant does not have energy – it generates it; similarly, a person does not wait for a mood but generates it.
- Activator 3 – Keep your word and follow every matter through to completion; words can wound or inspire.

## 5. The drive for care for oneself and others

- Care is linked to mirror neurons – we feel what others feel.
- What blocks care: excessive anxiety and nervousness, and insufficient attention.
- When anxiety is too high, the brain shuts down emotions as a defense mechanism.
- Activator 1 – Take care of yourself: get enough sleep, eat well, and exercise.
- Activator 2 – Be more sensitive and tender; allow others to care for themselves.
- Activator 3 – Be more attentive and attuned to others.

## 6. The drive for connection with others

- Social life is very important; making a good connection with someone triggers dopamine.
- There is often a conflict between personal interests and group interests.
- Activator 1 – Define and design your ideal relationships: four types – family, friends, romantic/sexual partners, and colleagues.
- Activator 2 – Practice positive projection: expect the best from others and notice the best in them; the optimal ratio of praise to criticism is 5:1.
- Activator 3 – Seek and maintain close relationships with people who stimulate your growth; it is good to have 10 true friends.

## Part II. The five expansive drives

### 7. Expansive drives – introduction

- Baseline drives concern stability and security; they are oriented toward daily life.
- Expansive drives concern change; they are future-oriented.
- It is important to manage events, understand the world, and learn new things.

## 8. The drive for change

- The golden mean: too little change leads to boredom, too much leads to chaos.
- Change requires more brain activity than baseline drives – vision and composure are needed.
- Change at the societal level is easier than at the individual level.
- Activator 1 – Make changes focused on gains, not losses: we fear the consequences of change, the inconvenience during the process, and disappointment; it is important to shift thinking from negative to positive; change happens regardless.
- Activator 2 – Be clear about your goals and be ambitious: few people know exactly what they want; it is worth starting with analysis, being determined, and having a grand vision that motivates you every morning.
- Activator 3 – Make real choices: establish what you want, but also what you do not want.

## 9. The drive for challenge

- A challenge leads to maximum use of one's potential.
- A goal is another thing to do – reaching somewhere you have usually already been.
- A challenge requires great effort and leads to a new place.
- Main problems: an overflow of mundane tasks and procrastination.
- Activator 1 – Choose the most demanding challenges that will give you the greatest satisfaction: traits of good challenges – they require attention and focus, they push beyond your limits and promote growth, they produce measurable results that can be tracked in real time, they are time-bound with a clear goal, and they allow you to share experiences with others.
- Activator 2 – Focus on the journey toward completing a task and do not fear criticism: problems include unmet expectations and fear of others' reactions; the solution is to focus on the process; criticism is rare – about 7 times in a lifetime – while praise is far more frequent.
- Activator 3 – Set yourself thirty-day monthly challenges: they can be social (e.g., better communication with others) or for the benefit of others; a group of wise, determined people can change the world – and it has always happened that way.

## 10. The drive for creative expression

- Each of us can express our individuality through creativity.
- Creative workers are becoming increasingly important.
- Activator 1 – Increase the level of creative expression in all areas of life: everything we do is an expression of our creativity – the look of our home, our workplace, our relationships with a partner and friends, time for ourselves, and our contribution to the world.
- Activator 2 – Observe people's behaviour and design: creative people are not solitary geniuses but sociable individuals who develop their creativity by observing and getting to know others. The more often we go out into the world and interact with others, the faster and more effectively our brain regenerates.
- Activator 3 – Be more creative and share your work with others: creativity is an idea plus action (e.g. writing a book is not just thinking about it but writing it page by page). Creativity is not a character trait but a self-imposed discipline – not a flash of genius but long, painstaking work. Your own voice is not what you find, but what you share with others.

## 11. The drive to contribute (to the development of the world)

- Churchill: "We make a living by what we get; we make a life by what we give."
- When we make a personal contribution to the development of the world, we feel a deep sense of meaning and purpose.
- There are two kinds of giving: giving something of yourself, and giving someone something.
- Activator 1 – Dedicate yourself and apply yourself to what you do (and be proud of it): doing something 100% is already a contribution to the world; it is also worth remembering what we have already done so far.
- Activator 2 – Give others something of yourself (and have a deep sense of meaningfully contributing to a worthy cause): these must be endeavours we consider important and significant; the choice of action is as important as the choice of cause. Five factors: using our talents and creativity and seeing the results of those actions; being a teacher to others; observing the direct social effects of our actions.
- Activator 3 – Be a teacher, guide, and mentor to others: this compels us to be a better person.

## 12. The drive for higher awareness

- We want to feel in control while also surrendering to a higher power.
- Activator 1 – Maintain full awareness: awareness means being alive and alert; higher awareness means better control of one's thoughts. We should be aware of: our thoughts, emotions, behaviour, other people, and our own progress. Key questions: "What will I focus my thoughts on right now?" "How will I feel right now?"
- Activator 2 – Rise to a higher level of awareness: focus on the present moment. An important question: "On a scale of 1 to 10, how present am I right now?" Judging and evaluating is good, but not when we want to live in the present moment.
- Activator 3 – Live in wonder: be grateful for what you have.

## Summary

- Above all, we must stop thinking that what is difficult or complicated is bad.
