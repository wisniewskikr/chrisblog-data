# Content

## Evolution has equipped us with the ability to draw boundaries. It makes it easier to navigate the world. But we can never be sure we have drawn the boundaries correctly.

- Every boundary is a decision, and life is made up of decisions.
- We navigate a grey world using black-and-white navigation.
- We systematize instead of creating hierarchies; we divide instead of integrating; we look for differences instead of similarities.
- The golden triad: Fight–Flight, Us–Them, Good–Evil.

## 1. The instinct for categorization

- The ability to categorize enables us to navigate the world.
- Children divide things into categories; as we gain experience, we make ever more precise distinctions.
- Goals of categorization: predictability and minimizing doubt.
- We classify the world on three levels: superordinate (most important in everyday life, different for amateurs and experts), basic, and subordinate.

## 2. Misfortunes come in pairs

- The sorites paradox: how many grains make a heap?
- Just noticeable difference: the minimum value of change that a person can perceive; expressed by the formula ΔB / B = K, where B is the quantity before the change, ΔB is the change, and K is the constant of sensory difference.
- Plato was wrong: it is not us sitting with our backs to the entrance seeing only the shadow of an ideal world — it is the world that is blurry, and we try to systematize it.
- When changes are smaller than the just noticeable difference, we may not detect them (e.g. obesity, climate warming).

## 3. When categories clash

- We strive to extract as much information as possible at the cost of as little energy as possible.

## 4. The dark side of black and white

- The tyranny of choice: the more alternatives, the harder it is to decide.
- Seven basic emotions: joy, surprise, sadness, anger, disgust, fear, contempt.
- The human brain can hold seven items in short-term memory.
- Personality structure (Big Five): openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism.
- National cultural dimensions (Hofstede): power distance, uncertainty avoidance, collectivism and individualism, masculinity and femininity, long-term orientation, indulgence.
- Compulsive hoarders: a categorization disorder characterized by hyperselectivity — more categories but fewer items in each, making it harder to discard things.
- Stereotypes: the opposite — hyposelectivity, fewer categories, black-and-white thinking (e.g. every Muslim is a terrorist).

## 5. Adjusting the visor

- If you attach too much importance to something, everything else fades into the background.
- Excessive focus can turn into an addiction.

## 6. The complexity of simplicity

- Need for cognitive closure: we want to know everything.
- Need for complexity: deep reflection on the connections between phenomena.

## 7. At the other end of the rainbow

## 8. The game of perspectives

- Language is becoming increasingly sharp, with a lack of good intermediate expressions.
- The way we speak is the way we see.
- Instead of changing the world, one can change the way the world is seen.
- The word "only" can completely change the meaning of a sentence.

## 9. Ask and you shall not go astray

- 6 principles of persuasion: reciprocity (a sense of debt for something received), scarcity (the less there is of something, the more we want it), authority (we comply with those we consider competent), consistency (we strive for coherence in our behavior), liking (we fulfill the wishes of those we like), social proof (we model ourselves on others, especially when uncertain).
- 5 pillars of persuasion: simplicity (the brain prefers simple information), subjective benefit (we prefer advantageous behaviors), novelty (sudden and unexpected events attract attention), confidence (the brain likes certainty about being right), empathy (we respond favorably to people we identify with).

## 10. Supersuasion

- Identifying with a group exerts a profound influence on how we interpret events.
- Three dials: Us–Them, Fight–Flight, Good–Evil.

## 11. Persuasion under cover — the secret art of getting your way

- Reveal the sorites paradox: where is the boundary?
- Once you find the right categories, ensure they align with the pillars of persuasion.
- Use metaphors.

## 12. Shifting boundaries

- Anonymity amplifies aggression.
- Our tribespeople are right, strangers are not — regardless of the facts.

## Afterword. The wisdom of radicals

- We are all radicals: either we make a decision or we do not.
- The world is not black and white.
