## Introduction

Seven basic emotions:
- Fear
- Anger
- Sadness
- Joy
- Disgust
- Surprise
- Contempt

## 1. What are emotions?

- Emotions are quick, direct reactions to stimuli.
- They are subjective: the same thing may please one person and not another (e.g. public speaking).
- They can be triggered by reality or by thoughts alone.
- They serve purposes: they prompt avoidance or pursuit of situations, prioritize tasks, and regulate behaviour and social communication.
- Emotion regulation involves amplifying or suppressing emotions in order to cope with a new situation.
- Emotional self-control means suppressing the outward expression of emotions — in order to maintain inner consistency or meet others' expectations.

## 2. The role of the amygdala and the prefrontal cortex

- The amygdala captures signals, evaluates them as positive or negative, assigns them importance (when important enough, they become an emotion), and stores emotional memories.
- The amygdala is fully developed at birth, whereas the hippocampus is not — which is why we remember little from early childhood, yet feelings are strongly shaped during that period.
- The prefrontal cortex filters emotions.
- The sympathetic nervous system acts as the "accelerator": it activates the fight-or-flight response.
- The parasympathetic nervous system acts as the "brake": it calms the body down.

## 3. How our emotions are shaped

- Experiencing emotions begins in the womb — a foetus can feel fear, joy, and other emotions.
- After birth, the feelings of the child and caregiver become synchronised.
- From month 2: anger, sadness, intentional smiling.
- From month 4: surprise and joy.
- From month 8: shame, fear of strangers.
- From age 2: observing others' emotions and responding to them.
- Between ages 4 and 6: guilt, feelings of inferiority, empathy.
- Playing out traumatic experiences helps a child process them.

## 4. Emotional chaos

- Children should not be discouraged from crying, as they will never learn to regulate their emotions.
- Dysfunctional families are characterised by chaos and inconsistency: the same event may provoke different reactions.
- It is impossible to toughen a child into stopping feeling — paradoxically, when this is attempted, the child later struggles with emotions.

## 5. Emotions have power

- Asking "why?" makes us feel powerless.
- Asking "what can I do about this?" directs us towards action.
- Relaxation techniques: diaphragmatic breathing, saying words containing the "r" sound, chanting "am" or "om", massage, looking up at the sky.

## 7. How to support someone?

- Emotional support: when someone needs to talk things through.
- Rational support: when someone needs advice.
- What to avoid when offering support: making comparisons, minimising the importance of the problem, changing the subject too quickly, offering hasty or easy solutions.
