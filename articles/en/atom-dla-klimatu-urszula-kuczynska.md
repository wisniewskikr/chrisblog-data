# Content

## 1. It's time to stop being afraid. A conversation with Dr. Bjorn Peters

- Basic energy needs: electricity and heat for manufacturing plants.
- Renewable energy risks driving away industry; to make it economically viable, North Africa and Asia would need to be connected to the grid.
- Pumped-storage power plants serve to store energy, not produce it – storage capacity amounts to only 6 hours.
- Organisation: Nuclear Pride.

## 2. Anna Vero in Atomgrad. A conversation with Dr. Anna Veronika Wandetland from the Herder Institute

- Poland abandoned the half-finished Żarnowiec nuclear power plant – the decision was made by Tadeusz Mazowiecki.
- Nuclear energy needs to be demystified and properly explained to the public.
- Three Mile Island: the largest nuclear accident in the USA, classified at level 5 – effects were felt outside the plant.
- During the Three Mile Island accident, the film The China Syndrome was showing in cinemas.
- The world's first electricity-producing reactor was a Russian facility in the city of Obninsk near Moscow.

## 3. I will be the beginning of this change. A conversation with Britta Augustin from the German branch of Mothers for Nuclear

- Organisation: FOTA4Climate.

## 4. The illusion of Ents with nerds. Eco-modernism the Polish way

- Greenpeace's failed experiment in the village of Dharnai.

## 5. Specialists in the impossible

- Onkalo: a nuclear waste repository in Finland; the name means "Hidden Place".

## 6. Almost the youngest one in the reactor

- The experimental reactor Maria operates in Otwock.

## 7. A piece of Magda in Różan

- Two reactors operated in Otwock in succession: first Ewa, then Maria.
- During the Chernobyl accident, people were given iodine to saturate the thyroid and prevent it from absorbing radioactive iodine – this turned out to be unnecessary.
- The National Radioactive Waste Repository is located in Różan in Mazovia.

## 8. One for the price of two. The story of the power plant that was never built

- Poland planned to build nuclear power plants at three locations: Żarnowiec, Klempicz near Wronki, and Kujawy.
- Żarnowiec power plant: construction was 40% complete; $770 million was lost, additional penalties amounted to $440 million – while the estimated cost of completion was only $300 million.
- The decision to abandon Żarnowiec was made by Tadeusz Syryjczyk, who was associated with the mining community.

## 9. The failure Greenpeace keeps quiet about

- Greenpeace blocked a ship in Gdynia carrying coking coal from Mozambique – this type of coal does not occur in Europe and is needed for panel production.
- Village of Dharnai: Greenpeace electrified it with solar panels, but power was quickly depleted and extremely expensive; the village was soon connected to the regular grid.
- Emissions in perspective: India has 17.7% of the world's population and accounts for 6.4% of emissions; Poland – 0.5% of the population and 1% of emissions.

## 10. Tohoku – fear itself is the most terrifying

- In 2011 the Tohoku earthquake triggered a tsunami.
- The Fukushima nuclear plant in Japan reached level 7 – the second after Chernobyl.
- The Linear No-Threshold model (LNT) holds that every dose of radiation is potentially dangerous – as if 30 jumps from 1 metre were equivalent to a jump from 30 metres.

## 11. On a fire in a bucket

- The nuclear power plant in Ostrovets, Belarus is financed by Russia for geopolitical purposes; two incidents were recorded: a fire in a bucket and an electrical short circuit.
- Closing the Ignalina nuclear power plant was Lithuania's condition for joining the EU.
- Lithuania planned to build a new nuclear plant in Visaginas as part of a multi-country consortium, but Poland withdrew.
- Poland imported 10% of its energy in 2020.

## 12. Ukraine decided not to be afraid

- Reactor No. 4 at Chernobyl exploded in 1986, but the remaining reactors continued operating until 2000.
- In France, 72% of electricity comes from nuclear power.
- Ukraine operates 15 nuclear reactors.

## 13. En marche – full steam ahead

- In 1973, during the Yom Kippur War, Egypt and Syria attacked Israel in retaliation for the occupation of the Golan Heights and Sinai during the Six-Day War.
- When Israel began to prevail, Saudi Arabia cut production and imposed an embargo on the USA and Europe, which Europe felt acutely.
- In 1974 France announced a shift to nuclear energy – under Prime Minister Messmer.
- Energy carbon intensity: France – 50 g CO₂/kWh, Germany – 370 g CO₂/kWh, USA – 390 g CO₂/kWh.
- Lifecycle climate impact: nuclear – 12 g CO₂/kWh, wind – 12 g CO₂/kWh, solar PV panels – 48 g CO₂/kWh.

## 14. Do you know where our electricity comes from?

- In China, 70% of electricity comes from coal.
- The largest energy-related disaster did not happen at Chernobyl but at Banqiao: in 1979 heavy rains breached a dam, drowning 30,000 people, and another 150,000 died from hunger and thirst.

## 15. This is how it's done

- Russia's Rosatom is the global leader in nuclear energy.
- Russia operates a floating nuclear power plant, the Akademik Lomonosov.
- All nuclear waste produced in the USA over the years would fill 30% of the National Stadium.
- Nuclear waste can be reprocessed.
