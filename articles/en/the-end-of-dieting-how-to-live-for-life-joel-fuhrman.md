# Content

## Introduction

- Health = nutritional value of food / calories contained in that food — this is a measure of the nutritional density of the diet
- Macronutrients (carbohydrates, fat, protein) provide energy
- Micronutrients are vitamins, minerals, fiber, and phytocompounds found in plants
- The WHO indicates that a healthy BMI is 25; studies on teenagers show their BMI falls between 18 and 22, averaging around 20
- It is the quality of food, not quantity, that determines body weight and mental, physical, and emotional condition
- The best form of prevention is self-control

## Part I

### 1. Toxic hunger

- The standard Western diet is toxic, and unhealthy food is addictive
- Addiction causes tolerance to build up — more and more of a substance is needed to feel the same pleasure
- The discomfort felt when withdrawing from an addictive substance is called withdrawal syndrome
- The digestive cycle has two phases: anabolic (eating, digesting, and absorbing nutrients; active detoxification is slowed or stopped) and catabolic (detoxification begins)
- The first step is to introduce large amounts of micronutrient-rich foods into the diet to crowd out unhealthy food
- Overcoming bad habits requires conscious effort and time to develop good ones
- Self-esteem must be built up: through physical exercise, job satisfaction, helping others, or acquiring new skills; lack of self-belief weakens willpower
- Health consists of three elements: diet, exercise, and a positive attitude

## 2. Debunking dietary myths

- Selective perception: people perceive facts through the lens of their own views and preferences, rationalizing distortions
- Three facts about health: (1) vegetables, legumes, seeds, nuts, and fruit are the key to good health; (2) excessive consumption of meat and animal products contributes to disease; (3) eliminating refined carbohydrates from the diet results in significant weight loss and improved overall health
- The maximum recommended amount of meat in the diet is 5%; meat increases the risk of cancer, diabetes, and heart disease
- White flour raises blood glucose levels just like pure sugar — white bread and white pasta should be avoided
- Olive oil is one of the most fattening products available to humans
- Wheat itself is not particularly bad — the problem is that it is excessively processed and milled too finely, which raises its glycemic index
- A good diet: (1) meets the body's macronutrient needs (fat, carbohydrates, protein); (2) provides sufficient micronutrients (vitamins, minerals, phytocompounds) — the latter contain no calories
- A vegan source of DHA and EPA is an algae-based supplement; the recommended intake is about 200 mg of DHA+EPA per day, including at least 100 mg of DHA

## 3. The end of dieting

- Weight fluctuations (cycles of losing and regaining weight) are very harmful
- There are two types of fat: visceral (surrounds organs) and subcutaneous (found just under the skin)
- Excess weight is caused not by the amount of food consumed, but by its type — eat healthy foods that digest slowly
- Prepare healthy meals in advance and always have something healthy in the fridge

## Part Two

### 4. The power of whole foods

- The source from which we obtain calories matters greatly
- Macronutrients (fat, protein, carbohydrates, water) provide calories
- Vitamins, minerals, certain types of fiber, and phytocompounds supply chemicals essential to health
- Vitamins are divided into water-soluble (B vitamins and vitamin C) and fat-soluble (A, D, E, K)
- Water-soluble vitamins must be consumed regularly because the body cannot store them; fat-soluble vitamins are stored, so they can be taken less frequently and in larger amounts
- Vitamins are best supplied through natural plant foods: broccoli, spring onions, tomatoes
- Phytochemicals are essential for the normal functioning of the immune system
- G-BOMBS are the foods providing the greatest immune system support: **G**reens, **B**eans, **O**nions, **M**ushrooms, **B**erries, **S**eeds
- The most valuable seeds: chia, flaxseed, hemp, and sesame

## 5. The nutritarian school of eating

- The nutritarian approach means increasing the proportion of nutrient-dense foods in the diet and limiting low-value ones
- Eat as many plants with a high ANDI score as possible, while also keeping the diet varied
- Cell energy comes from glucose, which is derived from carbohydrates; insulin (produced in the pancreas) transports glucose to the cells
- Too much glucose leads to excess insulin production, which can result in obesity, type 2 diabetes, and certain cancers
- Unrefined carbohydrates (good): legumes, whole grains, fresh fruit and vegetables — they contain elements that slow sugar absorption
- Refined carbohydrates (bad): sugar, white bread, sweetened breakfast cereals, white potatoes — stripped of fiber that slows sugar absorption
- High-protein foods such as meat also raise blood insulin levels
- A high glycemic load is considered to be values of 20 and above — examples include brown rice, millet, and even sweet potatoes
- It is not an excess of carbohydrates that harms health, but their poor quality
- Preferred sources of healthy carbohydrates are legumes: chickpeas, black beans, red and white beans, split peas, lentils, soybeans
- IGF-1 is an important growth factor, but elevated levels promote the development of cancerous diseases; IGF-1 levels are determined by the amount of animal protein consumed
- The body can defend itself against excess iron from plant sources, but not against iron from animal sources
- Protein can be obtained from plant foods: leafy green vegetables, legumes, nuts, seeds
- Egg consumption is more harmful than commonly believed, particularly for people who are overweight, pre-diabetic, or diabetic
- Salmon is one of the most contaminated foods we eat — it contains 10 times more toxins than commercial beef

## 6. The eating plan

- Principles of the nutritarian diet:
  1. Eat a large salad as a main dish every day
  2. Eat at least half a cup, ideally one cup, of legumes every day
  3. Eat a double portion of steamed green vegetables every day
  4. Include nuts, seeds, and pits in the daily diet
  5. Eat mushrooms and allium vegetables every day
  6. Eat three portions of fresh fruit daily
- Weekly meat limit: 300 g for women, 450 g for men
- Practical tips:
  1. Eggplant promotes weight loss
  2. Eat more weight-loss-friendly foods
  3. Replace fatty sauces and dips with a low-calorie sauce or hummus
  4. Have steamed green vegetables for dinner every evening
  5. Use agar — a vegan substitute for gelatin
  6. Replace salt and sugar with other spices and condiments
  7. Use legumes as the main source of starch
  8. Never treat nuts, seeds, and pits as snacks — incorporate them into meals
  9. Buy seasonal fruit and vegetables in larger quantities and freeze them for winter
  10. Take up gardening and start growing some of your own food
  11. Switch to healthy, homemade desserts
  12. Drink green smoothies for breakfast
- The inner lining of metal cans very often contains the toxic compound BPA

## Supplements

- Synthetic folic acid: avoid — it works completely differently from the natural folic acid found in green plants
- Beta-carotene (provitamin A): avoid supplementation — studies have shown that synthetic beta-carotene acts differently from the natural form found in food
- Vitamin A: probably the most dangerous supplement — definitely avoid
- Vitamin E: avoid
- Selenium: avoid
- Copper and iron: supplement only when a deficiency has been confirmed
- Vitamin B12: essential
- Vitamin D: supplement when necessary
- Zinc: low doses of 10–15 mg
- Iodine: 150 micrograms per day
