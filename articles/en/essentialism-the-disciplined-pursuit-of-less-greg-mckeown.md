# Content

## 1. The essentialist
- Less means better.
- Almost everything around us is noise – only a few things truly matter.
- If you don't set your own priorities in life, someone else will do it for you.
- Steps: review and evaluate, eliminate, execute.

## I. Essence

### 2. Choose
- Options are a thing. Choice is an action.
- Helplessness can be learned.
- Choice is the foundation of essentialism.

## 3. Discern
- The principle: less but better.
- There is no full correlation between effort and result.
- Pareto's law: 20% of effort produces 80% of results.
- The essentialist believes that almost everything is unimportant.

## 4. Make a trade-off
- The essentialist asks: in what do I want to achieve the greatest success?

## II. Exploration

Essentialists make time for: space, listening, play, sleep, and selection.

### 5. Escape
- We need space to distinguish important things from trivial ones.
- During the day you must carve out time to think.

## 6. Look
- The best people not only see individual factors, but understand how those factors work together and what they cause.
- Don't focus on details – look at the bigger picture.
- It is worth listening to what is not being said.
- During a discussion it is worth asking yourself: what question are you trying to answer?

## 7. Play
- The essentialist knows that play is very important.
- Play is an activity valued for the pure pleasure of doing it.
- Play improves health, relationships, and education, and is an antidote to stress.

## 8. Sleep
- Sleep protects resources.

## 9. Select
- If you cannot say yes with full conviction, say no.
- Give a task 100 points and consider it worth doing only if it scores 90.
- The essentialist accepts only the top 10% of opportunities.
- The essentialist asks: is this exactly what I am looking for?

## III. Elimination

### 10. Clarify
- Above all, you must know your goal.
- Clarity of purpose predicts how well people perform at work.
- Lack of a clear goal weakens motivation and reduces cooperation.
- When a company lacks goals, people either play political games because they don't know what to do, or they define their own goals and the company stops moving in one direction.

## 11. Dare
- The word "no" said in a good cause can change the course of history.
- The most important thing is to ensure that the most important things remain the most important things.
- We have a biologically ingrained desire not to disappoint members of our group.
- People respect and admire those who are able to say no.
- You can decline politely without using the word "no".
- Refusing often shifts the weight from popularity to respect.

## 12. Uncommit
- The sunk-cost effect: once we have invested money in something, we don't want to stop the venture.
- The essentialist has no problem cutting losses.
- The endowment effect: we undervalue things we don't own and overvalue things we do. Antidote – pretend you don't own it yet.
- It is good to seek an impartial opinion from someone who is not emotionally attached to the thing or project.
- The status-quo bias: doing the same thing as always.
- The reverse pilot concept: we give up an activity for a period of time and observe the consequences.

## 13. Edit
- An editor is just as important as a director – they remove all unimportant scenes from films to leave only the most important ones and highlight the essence.
- The Latin root of the word "decide" – *cis* or *cid* – literally means "to kill" or "to cut".
- It is sometimes harder to do less than more. Hence the saying: "I'm sorry, but if I had had more time, I would have written a shorter letter".
- An editor not only cuts, but sometimes also gently corrects.

## 14. Set limits
- Boundaries are being slyly moved – they must be guarded.
- If we don't set our own limits, there won't be any, or they will be set by someone else in a completely random way.
- It is best to set limits well in advance so that you don't have to refuse later.

## IV. Execution

### 15. Create a buffer
- A buffer is something that prevents important things from being damaged – the essentialist creates a buffer for things that matter, e.g. saves money for a rainy day or leaves early for important meetings.
- The essentialist prepares carefully and well in advance.
- Planning fallacy: people tend to underestimate the time needed to complete a task. The solution is to add a 50% buffer.

## 16. Subtract
- You should systematically identify and remove bottlenecks in your life.
- The goal of theoretical work is truth; the goal of practical work is action.
- The essentialist achieves more by removing obstacles rather than doing more.
- The goal: do less, but better.

## 17. Progress
- Take pleasure in small progress, but on the right things.
- It is best to start with small steps and then gradually build momentum.
- Start small and reward yourself for results.

## 18. The genius of routine
- The essentialist designs a routine that makes important things almost effortless.
- Through repetition, synapses are formed that are easy to activate.
- Always focus on the hardest things first.

## 19. Focus
- Ask yourself: what is important right now?
- Don't spend too much time on things outside your control – i.e. the past and the future.
- A person can do two things at once, but cannot focus on two things at once.
- A Chinese saying: in work, do what brings you joy; in family life, be fully present.

## 20. Be
- You can be a non-essentialist who applies essentialism for a moment, or you can be an essentialist who doesn't apply essentialism for a moment.
- The Dalai Lama said: simplicity is extremely important for happiness.

## Appendix. The essence of leadership
- Conveying the right information to the right people at the right time.
- Clarity means success.
