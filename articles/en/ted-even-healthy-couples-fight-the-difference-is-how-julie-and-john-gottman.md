# Content

- What matters in a relationship is not *whether* a couple fights, but *how* they fight.
- There are three types of couples: those who avoid conflict, those who engage with conflict and then seek resolution, and those who openly express conflict.
- The ratio of positive to negative interactions must be at least 5 to 1 for a relationship to stay healthy.
- The four most damaging negative patterns are: 1. Criticism; 2. Contempt; 3. Defensiveness; 4. Stonewalling (withdrawing from the conversation).
- When things escalate, asking for a break is a healthy and effective strategy.
- A constructive way to raise an issue is to speak first about your own feelings, then describe the situation, and finally express a positive need — for example: "I feel tired from cooking every day, so could you take me out to dinner?"
- Most conflicts in relationships are not fully solvable — what matters is how the couple *manages* the conflict, not whether they resolve it.
- The goal of fighting should be to understand your partner, not to win.
