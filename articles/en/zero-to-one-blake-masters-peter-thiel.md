# Content

## 1. Challenges posed by the future

- Interview question: is there something important that very few people in the world would agree with you on?
- The future: it will be different from the present, yet rooted in today's world
- Two kinds of progress: horizontal and vertical
- Horizontal progress: copying what works, e.g. globalisation
- Vertical progress: doing what no one has done yet, e.g. new technologies
- New technologies most often originate in startups
- A startup is a small group of people – as opposed to a lone genius or a large bureaucracy

## 2. Let's pretend it's 1999

- The dotcom stock market crash of the 1990s
- Thiel founded PayPal, which initially transferred money from Palm Pilot to Palm Pilot
- E-mail spread and people began sending money that way
- PayPal paid users $10 for signing up and $10 for referring a new user
- Four principles that emerged after the crash:
  - Grow incrementally
  - Companies must be lean and flexible
  - Grow by responding to competition
  - Focus on the product, not on sales

## 3. All happy companies are different

- Perfect competition: supply equals demand
- Monopoly: a company sells at the prices it dictates
- Companies in perfect competition lie by defining their market as smaller than it really is, e.g. English breakfasts in San Francisco
- Monopolists lie by defining their market as a combination of other markets, to conceal their dominant position, e.g. Google and search
- A monopoly, unlike perfect competition, can also be guided by values other than profit
- Monopolies are the engine of progress

## 4. The ideology of competition

- People fight because they differ from one another
- One should not focus on competition and conflict
- Do not fight for honour just to win – there must be a goal
- PayPal merged with X.com

## 5. The last-mover advantage

- Cash flows – projected profits – are what matter
- A monopoly has: proprietary technology, network effects, economies of scale, and a brand
- Proprietary technology: you are at least 10 times better than the competition
- Network effect: a product gains value when everyone uses it
- Economies of scale: the larger the company, the lower the unit costs
- Brand: a monopoly by definition owns one dominant brand
- Avoid competition as much as possible

## 6. You are not a lottery ticket

- Is success a matter of luck or skill?
- Students should achieve perfection at something in order to become a one-person monopoly

## 7. Follow the money

- The Pareto principle — 80/20 — is a power law
- The timing of a decision matters – some moments are better than others
- The place of a decision matters – some places are better than others
- Diversify a portfolio, but with as few positions as possible

## 8. Secrets

- People's goals can be easy, difficult, or impossible to achieve
- To be happy, one must have difficult goals

## 9. Foundations

- The foundations of a company are very important – changing them later is difficult
- Choosing a co-founder is like entering a marriage
- A company has three elements: ownership, control, and management
- The board should have 3 to 5 members
- The less a company pays its CEO, the better it tends to function
- Equity stakes should be kept secret, as disclosure generates conflicts
- Openness to new ideas is important

## 10. The mafia mechanism

- A company should be a place that brings together well-matched people
- A job candidate should be convinced by two things: the company's mission and the quality of the team

## 11. Will they come if you build it?

- People underestimate sales
- Salespeople change how a product is perceived without changing its substance
- The domain of salespeople is persuasion, not sincerity
- Distribution is as important as the product
- Five sales models:
  - Complex sales: an expensive product sold by the CEO, above $1 million
  - Personal sales: carried out by a team, from $10,000 to $100,000
  - Dead zone: a threshold at which salespeople are simultaneously necessary and unprofitable
  - Advertising and marketing
  - Viral marketing: the product encourages users to persuade friends to use it as well
- A company without a PR strategy will not earn recognition

## 12. Man and machine

- Machines should complement humans
- At PayPal, combining the "Igor" algorithm with human judgment made it possible to detect fraudsters effectively

## 13. Seeing green

- Technology: should be 10 times better than existing solutions
- Timing: is this a good moment? Does the right technology exist or will it soon?
- Monopoly: what is the market and can it be dominated?
- People: the right team
- Distribution: is there an effective channel for reaching customers?
- Long time horizon
- Secret: does the company rest on an undiscovered truth?

## 14. The founder's paradox

- Founders are very often eccentric – simultaneously outsiders and assimilated into society
- Four scenarios for the future: alternating periods of progress and decline (a sine wave), steady progress leading to a state similar to the present, a massive catastrophe, and massive technological progress
