# Content

## Introduction

- Medicine before 1930 was based on three P's: placebo, palliative (painkillers), and purgative.
- Therapeutic nihilism was introduced: doctors began doing nothing and simply observing.
- Based on those observations, treatments were developed, e.g. bloodletting when the heart was overloaded.

## Law one. A strong intuition is worth more than a weak test result

- Medicine is a game of probabilities: you choose a theory and then use tests to increase or decrease its likelihood.
- Intuition as "prior knowledge" allows many possibilities to be eliminated.
- The problem lies in false-positive and false-negative results.
- History tends to repeat itself – the past is the best guide to the future.
- If you feed garbage into an equation, you get garbage out.

## Law two. Normals teach us the rules – but outliers teach us the laws

- The history of astronomy as an illustration:
  - Ptolemy: Earth at the centre.
  - Copernicus: heliocentrism.
  - Brahe: Earth at the centre, the Sun orbiting the Earth, other planets orbiting the Sun – everything agreed except Mars, which exhibited retrograde motion.
  - Kepler, Brahe's assistant, discovered elliptical motion.
- It is precisely the outliers – such as the retrograde motion of Mars or anomalies in Mercury's motion – that open the way to the discovery of laws.
- In medicine the equivalent is the "anecdotal case": when a treatment works for only one person, it is worth asking why.

## Law three. Every perfect medical experiment is accompanied by human bias

- In tumours, sometimes 3 genes are altered and sometimes 120.
- In medicine, bias is particularly strong because of hope and patients' expectations.
