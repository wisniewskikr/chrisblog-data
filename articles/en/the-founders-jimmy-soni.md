# Content

## History:
- If anything is done on the internet today, there is a good chance that PayPal alumni were involved.
- PayPal is the result of a merger between x.com and Confinity.
- They worked together for two years.
- They sold to eBay for $1.5 billion.

## I. Sicilian defense

### 1. Pieces of the puzzle
- On 26 April 1986 the Chernobyl disaster occurred.
- Max Levchin was a child living 150 kilometers away; his mother was a physicist who worked with computers – he helped her, and as a reward he was allowed to play on them.
- In 1991 the family fled to the USA.
- Levchin was admitted to the University of Illinois, joined the ACM group, and met Luke Nosek and Scott Banister.
- Together they created SponsorNet, an advertising application.
- They looked up to Andreessen, who attended the same university and later created the Mosaic browser and Netscape.
- Max worked for the military and then founded a company called NetMeridian.
- In 1998 he moved to California.

## 2. Looking for support
- Peter Thiel followed a conventional path through the end of his studies: top grades, law school at Stanford, a clerkship at an appellate court.
- He did not get a Supreme Court clerkship, which shook him deeply.
- He became a trader at Credit Suisse, then moved to California and founded the hedge fund Thiel Capital, hiring Ken Howery.
- Thiel met Nosek and Banister, and later Levchin.
- Together they founded Fieldlink (mobile security): Thiel as investor, Levchin as CTO, Powers as CEO.

## 3. The right questions
- Musk studied at Queen's University in Canada.
- He took a job at Scotiabank because of Nicholson; he left with a negative view of banks after they lost billions by being afraid to buy bonds.
- He transferred to the University of Pennsylvania, did internships in California, and then deferred his doctorate at Stanford.
- He began writing vector maps.
- Musk and Kimbal founded Zip2; after its sale, Musk had $21 million.

## 4. Winning is what matters to me
- Banking services were outdated.
- Musk met Fricker (through Nicholson) and Payne; Ed Ho also joined.
- Musk invested $12.5 million in x.com.
- Musk argued with Fricker and eventually fired him.

## 5. Beamers
- Colleagues from university worked at Fieldlink: Levchin, Nosek, Pan, and Simons.
- They renamed the company Confinity.
- They came up with the idea of sending money between PalmPilots using infrared.

## 6. Cooked
- Confinity and x.com were neighbors at 394 University Avenue in Palo Alto.
- At Buck's bar they transferred $4,500,000 via PalmPilots.
- A professional firm chose the name PayPal.
- The idea of sending money through the PayPal.com website was born.

## 7. The money argument
- Moritz joined x.com with $5 million.
- X.com launched on Thanksgiving 1999.

## II. The bad messenger

### 8. Just start
- X.com began acquiring many customers.
- PayPal also began acquiring many customers; Sacks made sure the focus stayed on user acquisition via email.
- Both companies paid bonuses for opening an account.
- Omidyar created eBay.
- Harris was hired as CEO of x.com.

## 9. The widget war
- In early 2000 the companies competed fiercely, although they had known each other from sharing the same building.
- Network effect: every new phone increases the value of existing phones on the network.
- Metcalfe's Law: the value of a network grows proportionally to the square of its size.
- A meeting took place between Harris and Musk and Thiel and Levchin.
- A 50/50 split was agreed upon; Musk objected, but Harris said he would leave.

## 10. The crash
- On the way to a meeting with Confinity, Musk crashed his McLaren upon seeing Thiel.
- After the merger, the combined company received $100 million in funding – just before the dot-com crash.

## 11. The Nut House coup
- PayPal had customer service problems.
- A customer service center was set up in Omaha.
- Harris withdrew the referral bonus system.
- A conspiracy to remove Harris was hatched at the Nut House.

## 12. New order
- Musk became CEO and banned meetings.
- Credit cards charged high transaction fees, so it was decided to link PayPal to bank accounts.
- Authentication was done using the amounts of cents sent to an account (e.g., 17 and 14 cents).
- The X-Click button was invented: users placed it on their websites and it redirected to PayPal.
- A Premium option was introduced, charging 1.9% but offering additional features.
- The company moved to a new headquarters.

## 13. The sword
- Botha, a student from South Africa, was hired and built a financial model in Excel.
- Musk insisted on building PayPal 2 on Windows.

## 14. The Ides of March
- In January 2000 Musk married Justine.
- During their honeymoon a conspiracy against Musk was formed, concerning the name X-PayPal and the V2 project.
- Musk was removed from his position.
- Musk nearly died from malaria and meningitis; his child also died.
- Eight years later he launched his first rocket into space.

## III. Twin towers

### 15. Igor
- Thiel became CEO.
- Optical image recognition to fight bots – CAPTCHA – was implemented.
- Work focused on transaction patterns rather than individual fraudsters; the IGOR system was invented.

## 16. Use the force!
- A rule was created: if you run a business, you must switch to a business account.
- A $500 limit was introduced – above that, a business account or a bank link was required.

## 17. Caught in the act
- Gorshkov and Ivanov were hackers from Chelyabinsk; the FBI caught them.

## 18. Guerrilla warfare
- eBay together with Visa offered free payments.
- eBay introduced a Buy It Now button redirecting to eBay Payments, which worked poorly.

## 19. World domination
- PayPal began thinking about international expansion and within a year was available in 26 countries.
- PayPal decided to serve online casinos.

## 20. Blinded
- PayPal decided to go public.
- The September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks occurred.

## 21. The outlaws
- 1/3 of transactions took place outside eBay.
- CertCo filed a lawsuit – the period before an IPO is particularly favorable for such action.
- Negotiations with eBay were artificially drawn out to keep them quiet before the IPO.
- A threat of a ban on operations in Louisiana emerged due to a lack of a banking license.
- The IPO took place on 15 February 2002; the share price rose from $13 to $18.
- Thiel and Levchin became millionaires; Musk made $100 million.

## 22. The T-shirt battle
- PayPal T-shirts were given out at an eBay event.
- eBay reached a deal with PayPal once Meg and Peter were no longer part of the negotiations.

## Aftermath
- After the eBay acquisition, bureaucracy increased.
- At the time of its IPO PayPal had 20 million users; by 2010 that number had exceeded 100 million.
- After 13 years PayPal was spun off from eBay and went public for the second time.
- PayPal employees went on to create YouTube, Yelp, LinkedIn, SpaceX, and Tesla.
