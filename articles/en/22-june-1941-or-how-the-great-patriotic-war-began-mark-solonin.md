# Content

## I. The squandered war

- In the Soviet Union, fronts were always formed from military districts before the outbreak of a conflict.
- The Soviet Union was preparing an invasion of Europe on 19 or 20 June.
- The elite 1st Tank Division was moved to Finland.
- Germany had a Panzer Group; Russia had a Mechanised Corps.
- The Mechanised Corps consisted of: 2 tank divisions, 1 mechanised division, an independent motorcycle regiment, and special sub-units.
- One division consisted of 4 regiments.
- The Russian Corps outnumbered the German Group in tanks by 2–3 times, but the Group was larger in overall strength by 2–3 times.
- On 22 June 1941 the German army attacked, while the best Russian divisions were marching towards Finland.
- On 25 June 1941 Russia attacked Finland in accordance with a pre-existing plan.
- On 23 June 1941 the authorities arrested the commander of the Finnish front, Meretskov.
- Stalin completely withdrew from public life for several days.
- The Finns defended themselves and even recovered the territories they had held before the war.
- Had the Russians voluntarily ceded those territories: Finland would have remained neutral, the defence of Leningrad would not have been necessary, and troops could have been shifted to the western front.

## II. The swamp

- Boldin's Mechanised Cavalry Group was excellently equipped and hidden in the forests near Białystok – the Germans did not know about it.
- It was meant to cut off the rear of the German army advancing into Russia and was defended only by infantry.
- It theoretically suffered defeat because: saboteurs had cut the cables and there was no communication, aviation had destroyed the supply depots and there was no fuel, and a letter to Minsk requesting ammunition never arrived.

## III. Seven don't beat one

- Documents indicate that an attack on Germany had been planned in advance.
- The Russian superiority was sevenfold.
- A KV tank knocked out 22 enemy tanks in direct combat, took 156 direct hits, and returned to its battalion.
