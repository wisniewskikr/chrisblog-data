# Content

## Introduction
- There are no atheists in foxholes.
- We need hope, and reality often fails to provide it – that is when illusions come in handy.
- Functionality is placed above reality.
- The brain and eyes filter out 99% of information.

## Part I. Everyday life

### 1. Empty words
- Empty words are like air in tyres – they smooth out certain bumps.
- It is a social programme for fitting into one's surroundings.
- We are unable to accept the truth.
- We value feelings and loyalty more than truth.

## 2. Everything will be fine
- Lies provoke disgust when they serve to gain credit at others' expense, and admiration when they help us do what is best.
- A safety margin: we want to gain as much as possible without losing the sense that we are good people.
- Lying is an adaptive response.
- Optimists live better and longer, so lying is not always bad.

## 3. The theatre of healing
- In France, Mesmer and his magnetic machine did not work, but the placebo effect was discovered.
- The placebo effect has also been confirmed in knee surgery.

## 4. The invisible hand
- Price works like a placebo effect.
- The brain can transform our expectations into reality.
- A brand is another example of a placebo.
- The Greeks divided thinking into logos and mythos.

## Ii. The search for meaning

### 5. The heart has its reasons
- Love serves to protect the interests of genes.
- *Gates of Heaven*: a film about a pet cemetery.
- Naive realism: anyone driving slower than us is an idiot, anyone driving faster is a madman.
- Sick people see reality more clearly than healthy people, and that is why they are sadder.

## 6. Predictive reasoning
- We readily agree with people to whom we are emotionally attached.
- The happiest couples are those who see each other most through rose-tinted glasses and do not notice the qualities of potential partners.
- The right hemisphere handles reality, the left – dreams; patients with a damaged left hemisphere know what is wrong with them, while those with a damaged right hemisphere are blithely unaware of it.
- The brain does not analyse an exact image but a model – this is less costly, yet it makes it harder to notice something unexpected, like a gorilla at a basketball game.

## 7. Somewhere over the rainbow
- Cognitive dissonance: when two ideas clash in our minds, we pile on additional facts to get rid of the discomfort.

## Iii. The tribe

### 8. Walking through fire
- The belief in immunity to bullets caused attackers to stop appearing.
- Rituals help people cope with a threatening and unpredictable world – they operate on a psychological level and sometimes cross into the real one.
- Hazing is organised in places where inter-group bonds matter – those bonds grow stronger as a result.
- Benefits of ritual: strengthening inter-group bonds and providing a sense of comfort.

## 9. Something worth dying for
- A nation is an imagined community.
- Myths initially provoke laughter, but after a few generations they become a foundation.
- Advantages of myths: they guarantee stability; disadvantages: lack of flexibility.
- The absence of myths and rituals is a privilege – enjoyed when everything is provided for.

## 10. The great illusion
- Terror management theory: people understand that the world is a bad place and try to manage this through religion.
- Ways of cheating death: longevity, resurrection, reincarnation, living on as a memory.

## Epilogue
- When people lack a sense of real control, they try to achieve it mentally – hence conspiracy theories.
- Paradoxically, it is sometimes easier to open up to logic when emotional needs are met.
