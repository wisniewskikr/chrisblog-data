# Content

## I. The fundamentals

### 1. The surprising power of atomic habits
- An improvement of just 1% compounds over a lifetime into a large change.
- The results of small good and bad habits are not visible right away, which is why motivation to maintain or abandon them is lacking.
- Net worth is a delayed result of financial habits; body weight – of eating habits; knowledge – of learning habits; clutter – of cleaning habits.
- Goals are about outcomes; processes are about systems.
- Problems should be solved at the level of systems, not outcomes; outcomes are only temporary solutions.
- Small changes remain invisible until a certain threshold is crossed.

## 2. How habits shape identity (and vice versa)
- The problem with change is that we often try to change the wrong thing.
- Three layers of behaviour change:
  - Changing outcomes (e.g. losing weight, publishing a book) – what you get.
  - Changing processes (habits and systems, e.g. a training plan) – what you do.
  - Changing identity (beliefs, worldview, self-image) – what you believe.
- Behaviour that is inconsistent with identity will not last.
- Focus on identity and make habits part of your identity.
- Habits embody identity.
- Two steps: 1. Decide who you want to be; 2. Prove it with small wins.

## 3. Four easy steps to building better habits
- A habit is a behaviour repeated often enough to become automatic.
- The brain can handle only one task at a time – habits solve this.
- Habits provide freedom: financial, health-related, etc.
- The habit loop: cue, craving, response, and reward.
  - Cue: noticing the reward (food, water, sex, money, etc.).
  - Craving: the desire to change a state; thoughts, feelings, and emotions transform the cue into a craving.
  - Response: the habit in the form of a thought or action; receiving the reward.
  - Reward: delivers satisfaction or teaches.
- Problem phase: cue and craving; solution phase: response and reward.
- How to build a good habit: 1. Cue – make it obvious; 2. Craving – make it attractive; 3. Response – make it easy; 4. Reward – make it satisfying.
- How to break a bad habit: 1. Cue – make it invisible; 2. Craving – make it unattractive; 3. Response – make it difficult; 4. Reward – make it unsatisfying.

## The first law. Make it obvious

### 4. The man who didn't look good
- Before you can effectively build new habits, you must take stock of your existing ones.
- Pointing and calling habits reduces errors and brings unconscious behaviour to the level of consciousness.
- Habits are not inherently good or bad, but they become so over the long term.
- The first step to changing bad habits is identifying them; it helps to say a bad habit aloud.

## 5. The best way to build a new habit
- Implementation intention: a plan made in advance specifying when and where a given action will be performed; it increases the likelihood of doing it more than threefold.
- The most common types of cues for habits are time and place.
- Formula: "I will (behaviour) at (time) in (location)".
- The Diderot effect: the tendency to make purchases on the back of a previous purchase – Diderot, creator of the French encyclopaedia, after buying an elegant robe, began redecorating his entire apartment to match it.
- Habit stacking: a specific form of implementation intention that links a new habit to an existing one instead of to a fixed time and place.
- Formula: "After (current habit), I will (new habit)".

## 6. Motivation is overrated – environment often matters more
- Environment is the invisible hand shaping human behaviour.
- The more accessible a product or service is, the more likely you are to try it.
- Visual cues strongly influence behaviour.
- If you want to establish a habit, make the cues associated with it prominent in your environment.
- Think of your environment as a space filled with relationships – a sofa can be a place for reading or a place for rest.
- It is easier to build new habits in a new environment.

## 7. The secret of self-control
- People without addictions spend less time in environments that expose them to those addictions.
- A habit can be broken, but it cannot be forgotten.

## The second law. Make it attractive

### 8. How to create an irresistible habit
- Supernormal stimuli are an amplified version of reality – junk food is an example: the brain prizes salt, sugar, and fat.
- Products with high dynamic contrast (e.g. crunchy combined with soft) encourage continued consumption even when full.
- Without dopamine there is no craving; dopamine is released both when experiencing pleasure and when anticipating it.
- The brain has more circuitry devoted to wanting than to liking.
- Link actions you want to take with actions you should take (e.g. riding a stationary bike while watching Netflix).
- Premack's principle: more probable behaviours will reinforce less probable ones.

## 9. The role of family and friends in shaping habits
- We do not choose our earliest habits – we imitate them.
- Social norms are the invisible rules that guide our behaviour every day.
- We imitate the behaviours of three groups: 1. The close, 2. The many, 3. The powerful.
- Surround yourself with people who have the habits you want to develop.
- When we don't know what to do, we look to the group for answers; we often prefer to be wrong with the group than right alone.
- People aspire to status because it is a smart strategy; once they fit in, they look for ways to stand out.

## 10. How to find and remove the causes of bad habits
- A craving is a specific manifestation of a deeper underlying motive (e.g. wanting food stems from the need to survive).
- There are many ways to satisfy the same underlying motive.
- Life is prediction: we constantly try to work out how to behave based on what we already know.
- The cause of a habit is the prediction that precedes it; a craving is a felt sense of deficiency.
- Emotions allow us to treat things as good, bad, or neutral.
- It is worth reframing habits to highlight the benefits they bring.

## The third law. Make it easy

### 11. Walk slowly, but never backward
- There is a difference between being in motion and taking action; being in motion can mean planning for a lifetime and is often a way to avoid failure.
- The condition for establishing a habit is repetition, not perfection.
- Long-term potentiation: each repetition strengthens the neural signal.
- Hebb's rule: neurons that fire together wire together.
- Automaticity is the ability to act without having to think.
- Habits form based on frequency, not on the duration of action.

## 12. The law of least effort
- Agriculture is easier to practise on an east–west axis (Europe, Asia) than on a north–south axis (the Americas, Africa) because the climate is more uniform along that axis.
- Our true default strategy is laziness and the desire for comfort.
- The law of least effort: when choosing between similar options, we naturally gravitate toward the one requiring less work.
- The more convenient a habit, the greater the chance of continuing it consistently.
- Designing your environment is one of the most effective ways to make habits more convenient.
- Japanese companies searched their production processes for all sources of friction and eliminated them (gaining by subtracting).
- Resetting a room: tidying it after the last activity and setting it up for the next one.
- Reduce the friction associated with good behaviours; increase the friction associated with bad ones.

## 13. How to stop procrastinating using the two-minute rule
- Habits are like highway on-ramps: they lead you down a fixed path and before you know it you are speeding toward the next behaviour.
- Continuing what you are already doing feels easier than starting something entirely different.
- The decisive moment: the moment of hesitation between one behaviour and another.
- The difference between a good day and a bad day often comes down to a few good choices made at decisive moments.
- When forming a new habit, it should not take you more than two minutes.
- To improve a habit, you first need to establish it.
- Habits often last only a few moments but shape behaviour for the minutes or hours that follow.

## 14. How to make good habits irresistible and bad habits impossible
- Commitment device (automatic commitment technique): making a choice in the present that determines your actions in the future.
- The simplest way to break a bad habit is to make it impractical.

### Law four. Make it satisfying

## 15. The fundamental rule of behavior change
- We are more likely to repeat a behavior if it is associated with pleasant sensations.
- Animals live in an environment of immediate gratification; humans live in one of delayed gratification.
- Homo sapiens has existed for 200–300 thousand years, agriculture for only about 10,000 years, and the environment of delayed gratification for about 500 years.
- The result is a time inconsistency: we value the present more than the future.
- Bad habits: the immediate effect is pleasant, but the ultimate outcome is unpleasant.
- Good habits: the immediate effect is not pleasant, but the final result is satisfying.
- We pay the cost of good habits in the present; we pay the cost of bad habits in the future.
- The brain prioritizes the present, which privileges bad habits.
- What is immediately rewarded is repeated; what is immediately punished is avoided.
- A method: after a good behavior, transfer money to a separate account dedicated to a treat for yourself.

## 16. How to maintain good habits every day
- It is useful to have a visual measure of progress, e.g. moving marbles from one jar to another after each set of exercises.
- A habit tracker: a simple notebook in which you record your progress.
- An effective mantra: don't break the chain.
- The most powerful motivator is progress.
- The "never miss twice" rule: if you slip up, return to the habit as soon as possible – nobody is perfect.
- One mistake is an accident; two mistakes are the beginning of a new (bad) habit.
- The first rule of compounding: never interrupt unnecessarily.
- Bad workouts are the most important ones – they preserve the gains accumulated during previous, successful attempts.
- Goodhart's Law: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

## 17. An accountability partner can change everything
- People avoid punishment that is large and swift.
- A habit contract is a written commitment to suffer a specified penalty for breaking the agreement.
- You need to find one or two people to serve as accountability partners who will sign the contract.
- The awareness of being monitored motivates and reduces procrastination, since delaying is linked to an immediate penalty.

### Advanced methods: how to go from being merely good to being truly great

## 18. The truth about talent (when genes matter and when they don't)
- People differ in their predispositions; the art lies in aligning aspirations with competencies.
- Genes influence personality, which is a set of traits that remain consistent across different situations.
- Five behavioral spectrums: openness to experience (curiosity vs. caution), conscientiousness (organization vs. spontaneity), extraversion (openness vs. withdrawal), agreeableness (warmth vs. indifference), neuroticism (anxiety vs. calm).
- The explore–exploit trade-off: every new activity should be preceded by a period of exploration.
- If you are winning – exploit; if you are losing – explore.
- If you have plenty of time – explore; if you have little time – exploit.
- You can circumvent others' genetic advantages by combining several fields in which you are sufficiently good.
- Choose behaviors suited to your character and skills.

## 19. The Goldilocks rule: how to stay motivated in life and work
- Choose tasks at the edge of your abilities – tasks that are too easy bore you, tasks that are too hard discourage you.
- This is the Goldilocks rule.
- Success is achieved by those who can endure the boredom of daily training.
- Machiavelli: people crave novelty so much that those who are doing well desire change just as much as those who are doing poorly.
- What distinguishes a professional from an amateur is showing up when the work is tedious or exhausting.
- You have to fall in love with boredom.
- The greatest threat to success is not failure, but boredom.
- The brain has System 1 (fast and unconscious) and System 2 (slow and conscious); the state of flow occurs exactly between these two systems.

## 20. The downsides of building good habits
- The advantage of habits is the ability to do things without thinking.
- The disadvantage of habits is becoming accustomed to doing things in a fixed way and not noticing small errors.
- Mastery = habits + deliberate practice.
- The solution is a system of reflection and review.
- Strive to improve by at least 1% each year.
- During reflection and review, answer three questions: 1. What went well? 2. What did not go so well? 3. What did I learn?
- The integrity report covers: 1. What core values guide my life? 2. Am I currently living and working in accordance with my principles? 3. How can I raise the bar in the future?
- Reflection and review must be done at the right time and with the right frequency – too frequent and detailed is like looking at yourself too closely in a mirror; too infrequent is like not looking in the mirror at all.
- The more sacred an idea is to us, the more forcefully we will defend it from criticism – the same can happen with a habit.
- It is worth protecting yourself from over-identifying with a specific aspect of your identity.
- A person is born gentle and soft, and dies hard and rigid – hardness and rigidity belong to the domain of death, while gentleness and softness accompany life.

## Summary: the secret to lasting results
- The paradox: a single coin does not make you rich, but by adding coin after coin, at some point you must admit that you are rich.
- Changing one habit will not make you a master, but adding habit after habit can change your life.
- Happiness means the absence of desire – there is no need to feel any differently.
- Behaviors arise from desire, not intelligence; desire leads to action.
- Emotions are the primary mode of human operation; only afterward does System 2 – logical action – come into play.
- Self-control is difficult because it requires eliminating desire rather than satisfying it.
- Seneca: he is not poor who has little, but he who always craves more.
- Feelings appear before action (craving) and after action (reward); the flow is: cue – craving (feeling) – response – reward (feeling).
- Desire initiates, pleasure sustains.
