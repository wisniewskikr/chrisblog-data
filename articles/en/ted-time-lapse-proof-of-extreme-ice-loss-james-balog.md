# Content

- Photographer James Balog founded the Extreme Ice Survey, deploying a network of time-lapse cameras to document glaciers around the world.
- The cameras capture glaciers receding at an alarming rate, providing striking visual evidence of climate change.
- Naturally occurring CO₂ levels in the atmosphere peak at around 280 ppm; by 2009, concentrations had already reached 385 ppm.
- The dramatic rise in CO₂ is closely linked to the accelerating loss of glacial ice observed in the footage.
- Time-lapse photography makes the invisible visible — transforming decades of slow glacial change into undeniable, real-time imagery.
