# Content

## Introduction

- "Here and now" molecules: serotonin, oxytocin, endorphins (natural morphine), and endocannabinoids (natural marijuana).
- The molecule of anticipation: dopamine – oriented toward the future.

## Chapter I. Love

- Dopamine was discovered in 1957 by Montagu.
- It is produced by one in every 2 million brain cells.
- Dopamine is a response to the unexpected – to possibility and anticipation.
- Reward prediction error: the brain is programmed to crave the unexpected.
- Dopamine = Expected Reward − Actual Reward.
- Passion is ignited by dreams of a potential world and extinguished by confrontation with reality.
- The brain divides the world into the peripersonal zone (what is within reach) and the extrapersonal zone (everything else, requiring effort and time).
- Dopamine deceives – it knows no finish line and will never deliver full satisfaction.
- Stages of love: passionate (driven by dopamine, lasting about 1–1.5 years), then companionate (driven by "here and now" molecules: oxytocin in women, vasopressin in men).
- Maturity is the ability to transition from passionate to companionate love.
- Testosterone is the molecule of sexual drive; in women its level peaks on day 13/14 of the cycle – when they are most fertile.
- Sex is love in fast-forward: desire and arousal (dopamine), contact and orgasm ("here and now" molecules).

## Chapter 2. Drugs

- According to Aristotle, the ultimate goal is happiness.
- The ventral tegmental area produces dopamine, which activates the nucleus accumbens, generating motivation for survival and reproduction.
- The goal of dopamine: accumulate as much as possible with an eye to the future.
- Desire is a response to encountered things – an image, not possession.
- Desire and satisfaction involve two different brain areas, which is why we are often dissatisfied with what we craved.
- Ways to avoid disappointment: a continued flow of dopamine, limiting dopamine, or strengthening the transition from dopamine to "here and now" molecules.
- Drugs hit precisely the desire circuit and spread like a tumor.
- Natural rewards (food, sex) have a feedback circuit that says "enough"; drugs bypass it and bypass the surprise circuits.
- The potency of a drug increases with the speed of its action.
- Over time, drugs lose their power – the experience shifts from a high to merely escaping a low.
- Availability matters greatly; raising taxes and regulating where and when substances can be used are effective measures.
- Desire is powerful; satisfaction is weak and hard to achieve.
- Prolonged and persistent stimulation of dopamine provokes a violent reaction.
- Teenagers have incompletely developed frontal lobes (maturing around age 25), which is why they are reckless and impulsive.
- 25% is the optimal reward frequency: that is how many "treasures" should be found in boxes to sustain motivation.

## Chapter III. Dominance

- Dopamine operates through two circuits: the desire circuit (excitement and enthusiasm) and the control circuit (logical thinking).
- Dopamine influences whether we feel motivated to make an effort at all.
- Self-efficacy affects perseverance – we must believe in our abilities.
- A dominant or submissive stance becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy.
- Agentic relationships are oriented toward mutual benefit; affiliative relationships find satisfaction in the interaction itself.
- Too much control dopamine creates an addiction to achievement and prevents fulfillment from "here and now" molecules.
- Too little control dopamine manifests as ADHD: difficulty focusing and sustaining attention.
- Emotions are experiences of "here and now" molecules; high dopamine levels make people emotionally cold.
- The trolley dilemma: "here and now" molecules prevent pushing someone with one's own hands; dopamine allows writing a program that kills that person.
- Willpower works like a muscle – it gets depleted.
- Types of addiction therapy: motivational enhancement (kindling the desire to improve one's life), cognitive-behavioral therapy (removing triggers), the 12-step program (community, emotional support, and sense of accountability).

## Chapter 4. Creativity and Madness

- Madness and genius are linked by dopamine.
- Salience describes the degree to which something is important, vivid (e.g., a clown), or valuable (10,000 more than 10).
- A diseased brain assigns salience to meaningless situations; a healthy brain can suppress irrelevant things.
- Modeling means ignoring what is irrelevant in order to trace many scenarios – the fit of models to reality is crucial; weaknesses include insufficient information, false assumptions, and difficulty with abstract thinking.
- Creativity is the breaking of models – thinking outside the pattern.
- Dreams resemble abstract thinking.
- Artists and scientists resemble each other thanks to dopamine.

## Chapter 5. Politics

- Liberals tend to have higher dopamine levels; conservatives tend to have lower levels.
- IQ is the ability to build models (dopamine); day-to-day decisions rely on "here and now" molecules.
- Most decisions cannot be made in a purely rational way – due to too little or too much data.
- A single gene can have different variations called alleles.
- Conservatives are characterized by empathy, altruism, and monogamy.
- Loss causes a large drop in dopamine and amplifies the amygdala; gain produces only a small rise.
- Perceived threat makes people more conservative.
- In a 100-dollar split game, the average accepted offer is 30%; conservatives often accept less to avoid offending the proposer, while liberals demand more in the name of social justice.

## Chapter 6. Progress

- Human migration timeline: 100,000 years ago – emigration from Africa; 75,000 – Asia; 46,000 – Australia; 42,000 – Europe; 30,000 – North America.
- Dopamine drives the desire for exploration and adventure and reduces stress.
- The dopamine transporter draws dopamine back into the cell; when it is defective, dopamine stimulates repeatedly – this is how bipolar disorder arises.
- Between 1901 and 1913, the USA received 42% of Nobel Prizes, many from immigrants; 52% of start-ups were founded by immigrants.
- Dopamine influences all human behavior – it is the conductor, not the orchestra; it gives the signal to begin.
- The cost of raising a child in the USA: $254,000.
- Artificial intelligence and virtual reality may threaten the survival of humanity.

## Chapter 7. Harmony

- A person can be paralyzed by excessive worry about the future as well as by excessive indulgence in the present moment.
- Mastery: dopamine reaches its goal and yields to "here and now" molecules without a struggle, giving a sense of control.
- Locus of control: internal (everything depends on you) or external (everything depends on fate).
- Directing attention to reality and contact with nature brings relaxation.
- Humans are not multitaskers – attempting multitasking takes longer and produces more errors.
- A wandering mind is an unhappy mind.
- Creativity blends dopamine with "here and now" molecules – painting, woodworking, cooking, and sport all help.
- The happiest construction workers create something with their own hands and work in a cohesive team.
