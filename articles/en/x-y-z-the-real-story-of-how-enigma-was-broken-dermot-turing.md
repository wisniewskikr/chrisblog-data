# Content

## 1. Nulle part

- Maksymilian Ciężki: member of the War Council in Poznań, had lung problems after World War I, had radiotelegraph training.
- Antoni Palluth: radio electronics engineer from Poznań.
- The Curzon Line was created by an émigré from Poland – Lewis Namier, born in Wola Okrzejska, the same place as Sienkiewicz.
- Jan Kowalewski took up deciphering by chance – his predecessor had left for his sister's wedding. As an engineer, he entrusted the task to mathematicians rather than linguists and psychologists.

## 2. The king's entrance

- Kowalewski was sent to Tokyo to help the Japanese with their ciphers.
- Franciszek Pokorny took his place.
- Palluth left for civilian life and founded a company producing radios.
- The Poles acquired an Enigma for commercial purposes.
- Gwido Langer joined the radio intelligence service.
- Bertrand was a member of French intelligence.
- Baron von König became a French intelligence agent – Rex.
- Rex recruited Schmidt, who needed money for his affairs; Schmidt's brother headed the German cipher section, and Schmidt himself was employed there.

## 3. Mighty quills

- Marian Rejewski went to study in Göttingen but did not like it.
- The Bureau moved from Poznań to Warsaw.
- Permutations: inverse operations do not yield the original values.
- Palluth's factory produced military equipment and a copy of Enigma.

## 4. Scarlet flowers

- Hitler came to power.
- The French passed data to the British.

## 5. How did they …

- The first six letters of a message indicated the Enigma setting.
- In 40% of cases the letters were repeated – so-called females.
- A device called the sexmachine was used to exploit them.
- A cryptographic bombe was created; one bombe was needed for each rotor combination, so soon 60 were required.
- In 1939 the Germans introduced improvements, but the SD continued transmitting with the old cipher for a time – allowing the Poles to discover the wiring.
- From January 1939 ten plugboard cables were used, rendering the bombes useless.
- Knox was the Englishman responsible for breaking ciphers in Britain.
- On the eve of the war the Poles shared the secret of Enigma with Britain.
- The British hired Turing, a specialist in machines.

## 6. The dreadful pile

- Operation Gleiwitz started World War II.
- Turing built a new bombe drawing on the Poles' experience.
- Polish cryptanalysts made their way to France.

## 7. The mirror cracked from side to side

- Germany conquered France through neutral Belgium and the Netherlands.

## 8. Into three parts

- The Poles were evacuated from France to Algiers.
- Alan Turing proposed to Joan Clarke, but he was gay and the engagement was broken off.

## 9. The mysterious man from Enigma

- Germany attacked Russia.
- The Poles broke Russian ciphers and passed them to the British.
- Japan attacked the United States.
- The ship Lamoricière sank due to poor-quality coal; Polish cryptanalysts were on board.
- An Enigma machine and codebooks were captured from U-559.

## 10. Hide and seek

- The Germans detected the cryptanalysts' operations in France and they were forced to flee.
- Some of the Poles were captured.

## 11. The last game

- Rex began to talk and betrayed Schmidt.
