# Content

## 1. Thinking about the universe

- The nearest star – Proxima Centauri – is 4 light-years away from us.

## 2. Our evolving picture of the universe

- Aristotle deduced that the Earth is a sphere based on the shape of the shadow during lunar eclipses and the way ships disappear over the horizon.
- Ancient observers noticed that 5 celestial bodies behave differently from the other stars: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn.
- Ptolemy created a concentric model of the universe.
- Copernicus proposed that the Sun is stationary and the Earth moves.
- Galileo discovered the moons of Jupiter.
- Kepler noticed that planets orbit in ellipses, not circles.
- Newton discovered gravity – an invisible force of attraction.

## 3. The nature of a scientific theory

- Physics currently rests on two theories: general relativity (large distances) and quantum mechanics (small distances).
- A quantum theory of gravity is meant to unite both theories.

## 4. Newton's universe

- Aristotle claimed that the natural state of a body is rest, and that objects fall at different speeds depending on their weight.
- Galileo proved that objects fall at the same speed regardless of weight; the law of gravity and the law of acceleration cancel each other out.
- Newton formulated the following principles:
  - When no forces act on a body, it moves at constant velocity.
  - The greater the mass, the greater the gravity.
  - The greater the mass, the smaller the acceleration.
  - The farther apart objects are, the weaker the gravity between them.

## 5. The principle of relativity

- Rømer discovered in 1676 that the moons of Jupiter disappear at different frequencies depending on whether Jupiter is closer to or farther from Earth – light needs more or less time to travel accordingly.
- The speed of light is 300,000 km/s.
- Maxwell unified electric and magnetic forces into the electromagnetic field.
- Einstein showed that there is no absolute time – time is linked to space, forming spacetime.
- The equivalence of mass and energy: E = mc²; anything with mass moves at a speed less than the speed of light.

## 6. Curved space

- Einstein argued that gravity is not a force like others but a consequence of the curvature of spacetime, and that it acts instantaneously.
- Mercury rotates by 1 degree every 10,000 years.
- In 1919, an expedition confirmed the bending of light by the Sun.
- Time passes more slowly near a massive body.

## 7. The expanding universe

- Our galaxy is the Milky Way – it rotates once every few hundred million years.
- In 1924, Hubble discovered many galaxies by using the absolute luminosities of certain types of stars, assuming they are the same everywhere.
- From light, one can determine the temperature and chemical composition of an object.
- Hubble discovered that the universe is expanding.
- Earth's escape velocity is 11 km/s.
- Friedmann theoretically predicted the expansion of the universe, which Hubble later confirmed empirically. Friedmann's three models:
  - The universe will begin to contract.
  - The universe will expand forever.
  - The universe will come to a standstill.
- Penzias and Wilson confirmed the expansion of the universe by detecting microwave background radiation interference.
- We know that dark matter exists, but we cannot observe it directly.

## 8. The Big Bang, black holes, and the evolution of the universe

- The Big Bang occurred approximately 13.7 billion years ago.
- Temperature is a measure of the energy or speed of particles.
- The word "atom" comes from Greek and means "indivisible".
- Atoms are made up of electrons, protons (made of quarks), and neutrons (made of quarks).
- For every particle there is a corresponding antiparticle.
- Photons are massless carriers of light energy.
- Neutrinos are emitted by the Sun and pass through matter with no effect.
- The Sun has enough fuel for approximately 5 billion more years.
- A black hole is a star so massive that its escape velocity exceeds the speed of light.
- A supernova is the collapse of a very massive star; the Chinese observed one in 1054 – despite being 5,000 light-years away, it was visible in daylight and bright enough to read by at night.
- The Sun formed about 5 billion years ago and is a second- or third-generation star.
- The oxygen on Earth originated from the first organisms that converted hydrogen sulfide into oxygen.

## 9. Quantum gravity

- Laplace proposed a completely deterministic model of the universe.
- In 1900, Max Planck suggested that electromagnetic waves are emitted in packets – photons.
- In 1926, Heisenberg formulated the uncertainty principle: one can predict the position or the speed of a particle, but not both at the same time.
- Quantum mechanics describes many possible outcomes and their probabilities; interference involves the amplification or cancellation of waves.
- In 1913, Bohr discovered that electrons can orbit only in certain allowed orbits.

## 10. Wormholes and time travel

- Time travel into the future is possible by travelling in a spacecraft at a speed close to the speed of light.
- Travel back in time might be enabled by wormholes.

## 11. The forces of nature and the unification of physics

- The four fundamental interactions:
  - Gravity: based on gravitons, acts over large distances, is attractive in nature.
  - Electromagnetic: acts on electrons and quarks; repels like charges, attracts opposite charges; based on virtual photons. When an electron moves to an orbit closer to the nucleus, a real photon is emitted; when a photon strikes an atom, the electron moves to an orbit farther from the nucleus.
  - Weak nuclear force: responsible for the decay of atomic nuclei, i.e. radioactivity.
  - Strong nuclear force: binds quarks within protons and neutrons, and also binds protons and neutrons within atomic nuclei; based on gluons.
- The Grand Unified Theory (GUT) aims to unite these interactions.
- Strings in string theory are either open (with ends) or closed (without ends).
- String theory somewhat resembles a plumbing system.
- The weak anthropic principle: we exist because the conditions ideal for our existence came about; two or four spatial dimensions create unstable conditions.
