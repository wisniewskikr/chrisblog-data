# Content

- **Confirmation bias** is the tendency to seek out evidence that confirms what we already believe. A common example: confusing weather with climate — weather is the mood, climate is the personality.
- **The Dunning-Kruger effect** refers to the tendency to overestimate our own knowledge or competence in areas where we have limited expertise.
- **Cognitive dissonance** occurs when new information contradicts our existing beliefs, causing discomfort that can lead us to reject the new information rather than update our views.
- Misinformation and fake news amplify all three biases, making it harder to reason clearly about science.
- **What you can do:**
  1. Take an inventory of your own biases — recognise they exist in all of us.
  2. Critically evaluate your sources of information.
