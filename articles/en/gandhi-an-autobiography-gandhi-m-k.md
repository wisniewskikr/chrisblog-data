# Content

## Part I.

### 1. Birth and parentage
- Father Kaba Gandhi was prime minister of the government in Porbandar
- Father was married four times
- Gandhi was the youngest child
- Mother was very religious
- Born in Porbandar in 1869

## 2. Childhood
- He was not a particularly bright pupil

## 3. Child marriage
- At the age of 13 he was engaged three times
- At the age of 13 he already had a wife; marrying several children at once was an economy for the family

## 4. Playing the husband
- He was jealous and forbade his wife to go out
- They quarrelled over this
- His wife was uneducated
- There was a custom whereby a child-wife spent a great deal of time at her parents' home

## 5. At high school
- Because of the marriage he lost a year of schooling, and one brother dropped out altogether
- He received prizes and scholarships, but mainly through good fortune
- He made up the lost year

## 6. A tragedy
- A reformer cannot be on intimate terms with the person he wishes to reform
- He was a Vaishnava and did not eat meat
- A friend persuaded him that he should become stronger

## 7. A tragedy (continued)
- For a year he ate meat

## 8. Thefts and atonement
- He smoked
- He stole a little gold from his brother

## 9. My father's death and my double shame
- He nursed his father
- His father died
- He had a child who died after 3 days

## 10. A little about religion
- He became acquainted with various religions

## 11. Preparations for going to England
- He passed his entrance examinations in India but was not satisfied
- He decided to study in England
- He took three vows: not to drink wine, not to eat meat, not to be with women

## 12. Cast out
- He left behind his wife and their infant child of a few months
- He was excommunicated by his caste for going to England
- On 4 September he sailed from Bombay in the company of a lawyer

## 13. At last in London
- During the voyage he was very shy and spoke to nobody
- In London he stayed first in a hotel with a friendly lawyer, then moved into a rented room

## 14. My choice
- For the first month he learned English customs at the home of a family friend
- He was pressed to eat meat but managed to resist; he eventually found a vegetarian restaurant, having gone hungry before that

## 15. Playing the English gentleman
- For 3 months he tried to be a gentleman: dancing, French, violin, etc.

## 16. Changes
- He recorded every expenditure
- He studied Latin

## 17. My experiments with diet
- He experimented with vegetarianism

## 18. Shyness is my shield
- Shyness can be helpful, as one rarely says anything without thinking first

## 19. The canker of untruth
- At first he did not say that he was married

## 20. Getting acquainted with religions
- He became acquainted with various religions

## 21. Nirbal ke bala rama
- Prayer helped him

## 22. Narayan Hemchandra
- A writer who did not care about his appearance

## 23. The World Exhibition
- In 1890 he went to Paris for 7 days for the exhibition and the opening of the Eiffel Tower

## 24. The nomination – and what next?
- Three years of study
- He passed his examinations

## 25. My helplessness
- He feared that his knowledge was very limited

## Part II

### 1. Raychandbhai
- He arrived in India
- He learned that his mother had died
- Raychandbhai was a jeweller with an extraordinary memory, but his main aim was the deepening of religious subjects

## 2. How I began my life
- He reconciled himself with his caste
- He went to Bombay for 4–5 months to observe lawyers in action

## 3. The first case
- During his first case he froze and did not utter a word

## 4. The first shock
- He returned to Rajkot, where he opened a law office
- His brother asked him for help because he knew a certain official; Gandhi was shown the door and promised himself he would never do such a thing again

## 5. Preparations for going to South Africa
- Gandhi was despondent; he saw much corruption and did not want to take part in it
- His brother's friends asked whether he would go to South Africa to help with a case
- He agreed

## 6. Arrival in Natal
- Another child was born to him
- He travelled by ship

## 7. Some of my experiences
- He landed in Durban, Natal
- The court forbade him to wear his turban in court, but he refused to remove it and was thrown out

## 8. On the way to Pretoria
- On the way he was thrown out of a first-class carriage at a station

## 9. Further hardships
- He reached Charlestown
- He then travelled by coach to Johannesburg, but the coachman took his inside seat and made him sit outside
- He then made his way to Pretoria

## 10. The first day in Pretoria
- It turned out there was no work for him

## 11. Meetings with Christians
- He held discussions with Christians

## 12. In search of contact with Indians
- He convened a meeting of Indians on the subject of trade
- He learned about the social, economic, and commercial conditions of every Indian in Pretoria

## 13. What it means to be a "coolie"
- Indians were not allowed out after 9 o'clock nor to walk on the pavement

## 14. Preparing for the trial
- He prepared for the case on behalf of Dada Abdulla
- He persuaded the parties to go to arbitration, and Dada won
- The debt was repaid in instalments

## 15. Religious ferment
- He attended a Christian convention

## 16. Man proposes, God disposes
- There were plans to deprive Indians of the right to elect representatives to parliament
- Gandhi committed himself to staying

## 17. Settling in Natal
- They lost, but finally began to speak with one voice
- He performed public work for free
- 20 merchants paid annual retainers for legal advice

## 18. No room for coloured people
- In Natal a coloured lawyer was unwanted
- The affair gave him publicity
- He was admitted to the bar by the Supreme Court

## 19. The Natal Indian Congress
- They founded the Natal Indian Congress
- They collected funds paid annually in advance
- Members educated themselves and distributed pamphlets

## 20. Balasundaram
- A European beat an indentured Indian
- Gandhi transferred his indenture to another employer
- He gained wide recognition as a result

## 21. The three-pound tax
- Indians had to pay 25 pounds if they wished to remain after their indenture
- The fee was reduced to 3 pounds per person

## 22. Comparative study of religions
- He studied various religions

## 23. Running my own household
- He kept a household befitting his position

## 24. Homeward
- He returned by ship to India for six months to bring his family back

## 25. In India
- He wrote the "Green Pamphlet" about the conditions of Indians in South Africa
- He served on a committee aimed at improving sanitary conditions to prevent an epidemic

## 26. Two passions
- He helped to care for the sick

## 27. The meeting in Bombay
- He took part in a public meeting in Bombay; because of his weak voice a friend read the speech on his behalf

## 28. Poona and Madras
- He travelled around the country recruiting supporters

## 29. Return at once!
- He returned to Africa with his wife, two sons, and the son of his widowed sister

## Part III

### 1. The rumblings of the storm
- During the sea voyage they were caught in a storm

## 2. The storm
- Under the pretext of quarantine the passengers were not allowed to disembark

## 3. The ordeal
- When Gandhi came ashore he was attacked by a mob – beaten and jostled
- He escaped from his friend's house in disguise

## 4. After the storm
- They bought a property and covered their running costs from the rent; however, Gandhi considered this a mistake – a foundation should rest on members' contributions, and their absence means loss of support

## 5. Bringing up children
- Gandhi educated his children at home

## 6. In the spirit of service
- He worked in a hospital for two hours a day

## 7. Brahmacharya – I
- He decided to take a vow of chastity because his wife and children were an obstacle to his public work

## 8. Brahmacharya – II
- Fasting helps in living a celibate life

## 9. A life of simplicity
- Gandhi began to economise; he washed his own collars

## 10. The Boer War
- He took part in the Boer War on the British side as a stretcher-bearer

## 11. Sanitary reform and famine relief
- He demanded that Indians maintain cleanliness in order to prevent an epidemic

## 12. Return to India
- Before leaving he received gifts, which he deposited in a bank for the benefit of the community

## 13. Back in India
- Gandhi attended the Congress, which met for only 3 days a year

## 14. The official and the guardian
- At the Congress he sorted correspondence

## 15. At the Congress
- He moved a resolution on South Africa at the Congress

## 16. At Lord Curzon's reception
- At the receptions maharajas were required to wear their jewels

## 17. A month with Gokhale – I
- He met many different people

## 18. A month with Gokhale – II
- In Calcutta he saw goats being sacrificed to Kali

## 19. A month with Gokhale – III
- He made a journey to Burma

## 20. In Benares
- He travelled across all of India by third-class train

## 21. Should I settle in Bombay?
- He moved to Bombay

## 22. Faith put to the test
- His son fell ill and he had to decide whether to give up vegetarianism in order to help him

## 23. Back to South Africa
- He left his wife in India and went to South Africa to meet Chamberlain

## IV.

### 1. Love's labour lost
- Chamberlain implied in a veiled way that the law of the sword prevailed

## 2. Autocrats of Asia
- Gandhi was asked to leave the Transvaal

## 3. The swallowed insult
- Gandhi began his legal career in Johannesburg

## 4. The invigorating spirit of sacrifice
- He decided to bring his family to Africa

## 5. The results of self-examination
- He renounced his property and his brother broke off relations with him for many years

## 6. A sacrifice for vegetarianism
- He lent 1,000 pounds for a vegetarian restaurant and never received repayment

## 7. Treatment by water and earth
- He tried to limit the use of medicines

## 8. A warning
- For health reasons he drank goat's milk for a time

## 9. Clashes with the authorities
- He brought about the conviction of two Asian officials

## 10. A pious memory and penance
- He made no distinction between people on the basis of faith or origin

## 11. Close contact with Europeans
- Europeans lived in his home

## 12. European contacts (continued)
- He employed stenographers and was not disappointed in them

## 13. Indian Opinion
- He published a journal in which he described his own transformation and the fate of Indians in South Africa

## 14. Coolie locations or ghettos?
- He helped Indians who were to be evicted from the location

## 15. The plague – I
- They cared for plague victims

## 16. The plague – II
- The sick were moved to a lazaretto; two patients treated by the water-and-earth method recovered

## 17. The location in flames
- Indians were moved to a camp for several days and the location was burned down

## 18. The enchanting spell of a book
- He was deeply impressed by Ruskin's book "Unto This Last".

## 19. The Phoenix settlement
- The publication of "Indian Opinion" was moved to the Phoenix settlement.

## 20. The first night
- On the first night the engine would not start and they had to operate the press by hand.

## 21. A Pole joins the work
- He returned to Johannesburg and hired two lawyers, including a Pole.

## 22. Whom God watches over
- He persuaded the Pole to marry and the families moved in together.

## 23. A glance at family life
- He had no time to give his sons a proper general education.

## 24. The Zulu rebellion
- The Zulu uprising broke out and Gandhi enlisted in the army as a stretcher-bearer.

## 25. Inner unrest
- After the war he took the vow of brahmacharya (self-dedication).

## 26. The birth of Satyagraha
- It is a newly coined word combining truth and steadfastness; in English it is translated as passive resistance.

## 27. Some more dietary experiments
- He adhered to his diet even more strictly.

## 28. Kasturba's courage
- His wife fell ill but refused to drink meat broth in order to recover.

## 29. Domestic Satyagraha
- His wife fell ill, but a diet free of salt and legumes helped her feel well again.

## 30. The path to self-restraint
- He concluded that fasting should neither be overestimated nor underestimated.

## 31. Fasting
- Fasting is beneficial for health and for mastering one's desires.

## 32. Headmaster
- He founded the Tolstoy Farm and taught children there at school from time to time.
- They neglected neither physical exercise nor craft training; the children also got plenty of movement through garden work.

## 33. Imparting general knowledge
- He taught the children general knowledge and languages.

## 34. Spiritual training
- He also educated the children spiritually; since they came from different religions (Christianity, Islam, Hinduism), he had them study their own sacred scriptures.

## 35. Weeds
- There were also difficult boys on the farm and he was urged to get rid of them so his sons would not mix with them; he refused, as it might have bred a sense of superiority in his sons.

## 36. Fasting as penance
- When two of his pupils misbehaved, he did not punish them but undertook a fast himself as punishment; he believed the teacher is responsible for the deeds of his pupils.

## 37. To meet Gokhale
- He was summoned to London; in the meantime the First World War broke out.

## 38. My part in the war
- He held that one should support Great Britain; he regarded the mistreatment of Indians as the mistakes of officials and believed that during the war one should not press one's grievances — only after it ended.

## 39. The moral issue
- He decided to take part in the war as a stretcher-bearer.
- A person who renounces evil cannot renounce it completely — even drinking water or walking causes harm to microorganisms; what matters is that one does not wish to do so and does it only to the minimum degree.

## 40. Satyagraha in its nature
- A dispute arose with a colonel who was abusing his authority over Indians.

## 41. Gokhale's compassion
- He refused to restrict his diet for health reasons.

## 42. Treatment of pleurisy
- He kept falling ill and decided to return to India.

## 43. Return home
- He returned to Bombay after 10 years.

## 44. Some thoughts on the legal profession
- During his practice in South Africa an error was discovered; the question arose whether to inform the court — Gandhi was in favour of doing so and the client agreed.

## 45. Was it a rash step?
- The judges accepted his arguments and did not dismiss the case.

## 46. Clients become co-workers
- Clients knew that he only took clean cases.

## 47. How I saved a client
- His friend was caught smuggling; Gandhi advised him to meet with the prosecutor and the chief customs officer and accept the penalty, thus avoiding prison.

## Part V.

### 1. First experiences
- Other arrivals from Phoenix came with him.

## 2. With Gokhale in Pune
- The governor asked him not to take any action without prior consultation.

### 3. Was it a threat?
- He introduced Satyagraha in India; first the tariff was abolished in one of the provinces.

## 4. Santiniketan
- At the settlement they dispensed with cooks and prepared food themselves.

## 5. The hardships of third-class passengers
- Travelling third class in India is very difficult.

## 6. Whether to join the association
- Gokhale died.
- He did not formally join the association but was linked to it in spirit.

## 7. Kumbha Mela
- He went to Haridwar for the Kumbha Mela festival; people began to recognise him and ask for his blessing.

## 8. Lakshman Jhula
- He visited Lakshman Jhula — a bridge over the Ganges.

## 9. The founding of the ashram
- He founded an ashram near Ahmedabad called Satyagraha Ashram.

## 10. Between the hammer and the anvil
- They admitted a Pariah family to the ashram and faced hardships as a result.

## 11. Abolition of the indentured labour system
- He travelled throughout India advocating unity.
- He helped bring about the abolition of indentured labour.

## 12. The indigo stain
- In Champaran, farmers were forced to plant indigo on one-third of their fields; he went there to see the situation for himself.

## 13. Noble countrymen from Bihar
- He estimated the work could take two years and that they might go to prison.

## 14. Face to face with ahimsa
- He applied passive resistance and appeared before the court.

## 15. The case is dropped
- The court dropped the case and ordered him to assist.

## 16. Methods of work
- They rented a house for work and raised funds.
- Villagers began to come forward in large numbers.

## 17. Companions
- He began running schools.
- He began teaching the villagers hygiene.

## 18. Studying village life
- Assistance was given to the villagers.

## 19. When the governor is good
- The lieutenant-governor proposed forming a committee, which decided in favour of the villagers.

## 20. Contact with workers
- He began helping workers and advised them to strike.

## 21. A brief look at the ashram
- The ashram was established near Ahmedabad; it was a wasteland; they intended to engage in weaving.

## 22. Fasting
- To support the strikers he undertook a fast and after 3 weeks an agreement was reached.

## 23. Satyagraha campaign in Kheda
- Kheda suffered a crop failure; a suspension of land tax was requested; the government refused and a strike began.

## 24. The onion thief
- The most important element of protest is dignity and showing goodwill towards opponents.
- Onions were gathered in defiance of the government's ban; some participants were imprisoned.

## 25. The end of the Satyagraha campaign in Kheda
- The campaign ended with only the wealthy being required to pay the tax.

## 26. Striving for unity
- He took part in the war conference in Delhi.

## 27. Recruitment campaign
- He agreed to support conscription.
- For the first time he addressed a gathering in Hindi.
- People were reluctant to join the army.

## 28. On the threshold of death
- He had a minor illness and instead of fasting he ate, which led to a serious illness.

## 29. The Rowlatt Act and my blunder
- He organised a campaign against the act.

## 30. A magnificent spectacle
- On 6 April 1919, at his request, all of India observed hartal — a strict fast.

## 31. An unforgettable week – I
- In Delhi the news of a later date did not arrive and resistance broke out earlier; police fired on the gathered crowd.
- Gandhi was in Bombay, where the procession drew very large crowds.
- The next act of resistance was to target the Salt Act, which prohibited people from producing their own salt.
- Another proposal concerned the sale of banned literature.
- He was turned back on his way to Delhi and returned to Bombay.
- In Bombay riots broke out and were brutally suppressed by the police.

## 32. An unforgettable week – II
- He calmed the crowd and urged it to cease violence.

## 33. The Himalayan blunder
- He concluded that he had made a mistake: society must first be educated, and only then should passive resistance be launched.

## 34. Navajivan and Young India
- He began publishing these journals and explained the principles of passive resistance in them.

## 35. In the Punjab
- He went to the Punjab, where a state of emergency had been declared.
- He refused to take part in the Hunter Commission, which was to investigate cases of the enforcement of the state of emergency.
- They set up their own commission to show what the government was capable of.

## 36. The Khilafat and the question of cow protection
- He took part in a conference of Muslims and Hindus in Delhi.
- He spoke about the refusal to cooperate with the British government should it betray the Muslims on the question of the Khilafat.

## 37. The Congress in Amritsar
- During the Congress the Viceroy proposed minor reforms; the majority wanted to reject them, Gandhi wanted to support them, which threatened a split; ultimately a compromise was adopted.

## 38. My debut in Congress
- At the Congress he dealt with the question of raising funds for a memorial to the massacre.
- He was engaged in drafting resolutions.

## 39. The birth of Khadi
- He wanted to weave cloth himself but could not find anyone who knew how to use a spinning wheel.

## 40. At last found
- A spinning wheel was eventually found and, after solving many other problems, they began producing their own cloth.

## 41. An instructive dialogue
- He had a conversation with a factory owner; they concluded that they were not in each other's way.

## 42. The wave rises!
- Non-cooperation was agreed upon on account of the Khilafat question.

## 43. In Nagpur
- At the Congress the committee's resolutions were confirmed.

## 44. Farewell
- Gandhi bids farewell to the reader.
