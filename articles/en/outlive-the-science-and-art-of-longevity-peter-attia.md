# Content

## Part I

### 1. The long game

- There are fast deaths and slow deaths.
- The Four Horsemen: heart disease, cancer, neurodegenerative diseases, diabetes.

## 2. Medicine 3.0

- Risk should not be avoided but worked through.
- Eras of medicine:
- Medicine 1.0: Hippocrates and 2,000 years after his death – disease is part of nature, not a punishment.
- Medicine 2.0: mid-19th century and germ theory; 1628 and Sir Francis Bacon's scientific method – hypothesis and testing; lifespan improved through sanitation and antibiotics.
- Medicine 3.0: (1) prevention, not treatment; (2) the patient is an individual; (3) risk assessment; (4) focus on quality of life.

## 3. Objective, strategy, tactics

- Sun Tzu: "Strategy without tactics is the slowest route to victory. Tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat."
- Hyperbolic discounting: choosing an immediate reward over a potential future gain.
- Strategy vs tactics – the Ali vs Foreman fight: strategy before the ring, tactics in the ring.
- Three areas of health decline: (1) diminished cognitive abilities (memory, association); (2) deterioration of physical function; (3) worsening emotional health.
- Medicine 3.0 tactics: exercise, nutrition, sleep, emotional health, medications.

## Part II

### 4. Centenarians

- Jeanne Calment lived to 122.
- One centenarian per 3,333 people.
- Genes account for 20–30% of lifespan.
- Women live longer than men at a ratio of 4:1, but usually in worse health.
- APOE gene: variant e2 extends life, variant e4 shortens it and increases the risk of Alzheimer's.
- Another relevant gene is FOXO3.

## 5. Eat less, live longer?

- Rapamycin was discovered on Easter Island (Rapa Nui) – a drug that aids organ transplant acceptance, discovered by Sehgal; natives left the sick near the volcano.
- Rapamycin slows cell growth and division and affects mTOR – a growth sensor in many organisms.
- mTOR: with abundant food – growth/reproduction mode; with scarce food – cleanup mode (autophagy).
- Rapamycin also suppresses immune processes, but high intermittent doses prove beneficial.
- Luigi Cornaro (an Italian nobleman) followed a diet of 350 grams of food per day, which resolved his ailments; he described it in the treatise "Discourses on the Sober Life."
- AMPK acts like a fuel gauge in a car – activated by fasting or exercise; effects: (1) creation of new mitochondria (which produce ATP); (2) glucose production in the liver; (3) blocking mTOR activity.
- Autophagy: "self-eating" – the cell clears out worn-out components.
- Metformin: a diabetes drug that appears to reduce cancer incidence.

## 6. The crisis of abundance

- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease affects 1 in 4 people – more fat enters the liver than is excreted; reversible, with the next stage being cirrhosis.
- Metabolism is the process of breaking down nutrients into smaller molecules the body can handle.
- Subcutaneous fat is beneficial because it stores excess glucose (like a bathtub); different people have different-sized "bathtubs."
- When there is too much fat, it begins to accumulate in unwanted places.
- Insulin resistance: insulin (a pump) allows more glucose (air) to be pushed into the cell (a balloon).
- Fructose is very harmful: it creates uric acid (which the body cannot dispose of), causes gout, and tricks the body by lowering ATP levels – making us feel hungry (this does not happen with glucose).

## 7. The ticker

- 1 in 3 people who have a heart attack dies.
- Cholesterol is essential to life: it builds cell membranes, hormones, bile acids, etc.; 20% is produced by the liver, the rest by body cells.
- Cholesterol is transported in the blood by LDL and HDL – small transport "submarines"; LDL carries more lipids, HDL carries more proteins.
- Dietary cholesterol can be excreted; the misconception about its harm arose because studies were done on rabbits and chickens, which lack this ability.
- The endothelium is a "fence" on the vessel wall – damaged chemically (smoking) or mechanically (high blood pressure).
- When LDL gets trapped behind the endothelium, it oxidises and attracts more LDL particles.
- The endothelium calls in monocytes, which become macrophages; when they consume too much, they become "foam cells," forming a fatty streak and then an atherosclerotic plaque; a rupture can lead to stroke or heart attack.
- Recommended: monitor apoB instead of LDL-C, and take the Lp(a) test.
- Medications: statins.

## 8. The runaway cell

- Three strategies against cancer: (1) prevention – still largely ineffective; (2) better therapies; (3) early detection.
- Features of cancer: (1) it does not stop multiplying when it should; (2) it spreads; (3) it has a large appetite for glucose; (4) it does not use oxygen – less ATP energy, but more molecules for reproduction.
- Immunotherapy: any method that attempts to boost the efficiency of the immune system.
- Early detection means fewer mutations and a smaller tumour burden.
- Cancer detection methods: (1) radiographic (MRI); (2) visualisation (colonoscopy); (3) genetic (liquid biopsy).

## 9. Chasing memory

- Alzheimer's and Parkinson's are forms of neurodegeneration.
- The e4 allele of the APOE gene is associated with a high probability of Alzheimer's.
- Alzheimer's was previously considered a normal aging process – today it is treated as a disease.
- Women are more susceptible to Alzheimer's than men at a ratio of 2:1.
- Helpful factors: endurance exercise, strength training (the stronger the person, the lower the risk), sleep, dental flossing, and dry saunas (four 20-minute sessions per week).

## Part III

### 10. Tactical thinking

- Strategic domains: (1) physical activity; (2) diet; (3) sleep; (4) emotional health; (5) supplements.

## 11. Exercise

- VO2 max: average is about 40 ml/kg/min, in professionals about 60 ml/kg/min.
- The concept of the "centenarian decathlon" – identifying which activities you want to perform at age 100 and training with that goal in mind.

## 12. The basics of training

- Types of training: (1) cardio (type 1 muscle fibres); (2) strength (type 2 fibres); (3) balance.
- Cardio zones from 1 (walking) to 5 (sprinting); optimal is zone 2 – conversation possible but difficult; recommended 4 sessions of 45 minutes per week.
- Mitochondria: low activity level – fat burning; high level – glucose burning.
- VO2 max training: 4 minutes fast, 4 minutes slow, repeated 4–6 times; once a week.
- Hunting analogy: 95% steady effort and 5% fast effort.
- An 80-year-old man has about 40% less muscle than a 25-year-old; we lose: muscle mass → strength (2–3× faster than muscle) → power (force × speed, 2–3× faster than strength).
- In old age it is very hard to build muscle but very easy to lose it.
- BMD (bone mineral density): after age 60, mortality within one year of a fracture is 15–30%.
- Rucking: carrying a weighted pack on the back as a form of training.
- Grip strength is important.

## 13. The gospel of stability

- Injuries are the greatest health threat; it is better to train smart than hard.
- Endurance and strength are built on stability.
- DNS: a movement learning method modelled on how babies learn to move.
- Stability: 2× per week for one hour, on other days 10–15 minutes.
- Stability starts with breathing; three breathing patterns: (1) Mr Puffed-Up – breathes with the upper chest; (2) The Sad Guy – compressed, leaning forward; (3) The Yogi – mobile and flexible.
- Next element is the feet: "foot yoga" exercise (lifting the toes), standing on the four "corners" of the foot.
- Three spinal regions: lumbar, thoracic, cervical.
- Exercises: cat-cow and CARs for the shoulders.

## 14. Nutrition 3.0

- Dunning-Kruger effect: lack of knowledge combined with high confidence.
- The author's approach is not a "diet" but nutritional biochemistry.
- Four principles of healthy nutrition: (1) the right number of calories; (2) sufficient protein and fat; (3) vitamins and minerals; (4) avoiding pathogens and toxins.
- Types of nutritional research: (1) epidemiological – observes large groups, cannot distinguish correlation from causation, prone to the "healthy user bias"; (2) clinical.
- Distinction: efficacy (result under ideal conditions) vs effectiveness (result under real-world conditions).

## 16. Awakening

- Sleep is very important.

## 17. Work in progress

- Categories of trauma:
  1. Abuse (physical, sexual, emotional, etc.)
  2. Neglect
  3. Abandonment
  4. Enmeshment (blurring of boundaries between a child and an adult)
  5. Witnessing tragic events
- Trauma is the experience of a moment of perceived helplessness.
- There is no clear boundary between trauma and adversity.
- Adaptations to trauma:
  1. Addiction
  2. Codependency (excessive reliance on someone)
  3. Habit (anger, rage)
  4. Attachment disorders (relationship problems)
- Medicine 2.0 struggles with emotional health.
- Reframing: placing oneself in someone else's position.
- Breaking the chain: negative stimulus → negative emotions → negative thoughts → negative action.
- Key issues:
  1. Emotional regulation (regaining control)
  2. Stress tolerance
  3. Interpersonal effectiveness (how we show others our needs and feelings)
  4. Self-management (taking care of oneself)
- Mindfulness: creating space between an emotion and an action.
- Doing the opposite of what one feels like doing — changing behaviour changes mood.
