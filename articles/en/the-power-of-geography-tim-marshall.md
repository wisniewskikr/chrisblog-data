## Introduction

## Chapter I. Australia

- Australia lies between the USA and China and must constantly monitor their moves.

## Chapter II. Iran

- Iran is surrounded by mountains with deserts in the interior – it is very difficult to conquer.

### History

- After World War I, power was seized by an officer of the Persian Cossack Brigade – Reza Khan, crowned as Reza Shah Pahlavi.
- After World War II, his son Mohammad Reza Pahlavi came to power.
- Mohammad Mosaddegh, a nationalist, became prime minister and targeted British refineries in particular.
- The CIA restored the Shah to power; he used the secret police SAVAK.
- In 1979 the Shah was overthrown by Ayatollah Khomeini.
- War broke out with Saddam Hussein's Iraq.
- After Khomeini's death, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei took his place.
- In 1997 the moderate Khatami became president.
- In 2005 Khatami was replaced by Ahmadinejad.
- In 2013 Rouhani became president.

## Chapter III. Saudi Arabia

- In the 18th century Muhammad ibn Saud developed the oasis of Ad-Diriyya.
- Theologian Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab swore allegiance to Saud: the Sauds handle governance, the Wahhabis handle religion.
- Saud's eldest son married Wahhab's daughter.
- They gained control over Arabia, but the Ottomans destroyed the First Saudi State.
- Turki ibn Abdullah rebuilt the state, but it was then taken by the Rashidis, and the Sauds fled to Kuwait.
- Ibn Saud made his way to Najd, killed the governor, and became ruler; he recovered Saudi Arabia and concluded a treaty with the British.
- After his father's death, Saud took power but squandered the money; he was replaced by his half-brother Faisal, and then by Khalid.
- In 1979 rebels seized the Grand Mosque in Mecca; after it was retaken, radical religious teaching began to spread.
- Fahd inherited power.
- In 1990 Iraq attacked Kuwait and Saudi Arabia asked the USA for help.
- Of the 19 hijackers in the September 11 attacks, 15 were from Saudi Arabia.
- Muhammad ibn Salman (MBS) became ruler.
- The Lebanese prime minister was forced to resign during a visit to Riyadh.
- In 2018 Jamal Khashoggi was murdered at the consulate in Istanbul.

## Chapter IV. United Kingdom

- 43 AD: Roman invasion; Wales and Scotland held out.
- About 300 years later came the influx of Anglo-Saxons.
- Edgar the Peaceful divided the country into counties.
- 1066: William the Conqueror and the Battle of Hastings.
- 1215: King John and Magna Carta.
- Henry VIII Tudor had 6 wives and caused the break with the Church.
- His daughter Elizabeth I Tudor strengthened the Protestant faith and had her cousin, Mary Queen of Scots, executed.
- The final unification of England, Wales, and Scotland.
- The Scots lost up to one-fifth of their wealth in the failed Darien expedition and therefore united with England; the funds could then be directed towards development rather than conflict.

## V. Greece

- Athens was of the greatest importance.
- War with Persia and the Battle of Thermopylae.
- The Peloponnesian Wars between Athens and Sparta.
- Greece was unified by Philip II of Macedon and his son Alexander the Great; Macedonia had arable land that Greece lacked.
- Greece came under Byzantine rule.
- Toward the end of World War I it joined the Allies.
- During World War II it was occupied by Germany; a partisan movement developed.
- It remains in conflict with Turkey.

## VI. Turkey

- The Turks originate from Mongolia.
- Atatürk modernised the state after World War I.
- Erdoğan aims to make Turkey a great power.

## VIII. Ethiopia

- The country is rich in lakes and mountains; a rift valley with water runs through the centre, dividing the country into two parts.
- The main export is coffee.
- Legend has it that the Queen of Sheba visited King Solomon and bore him a son, Menelik.
- Legend holds that the Ark of the Covenant is kept in the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion near the city of Axum.
- The Kingdom of Axum.
- In 1855 Emperor Tewodros II began to arm the military.
- Ethiopia defeated Egypt and Italy, maintaining its independence.
- Emperor Haile Selassie I lost to Fascist Italy, but the British allowed him to return to the throne.
- Mengistu carried out a coup and established communist rule.
- Meles Zenawi then led the government, followed by Abiy Ahmed Ali.
- The murder of singer Hachalu Hundessa sparked widespread riots.
- Ethiopia is building a dam on the Nile, which affects Egypt.

## IX. Spain

Regions seeking separation:
- The Basque Country
- Catalonia

### History

- Conquered by Rome, then by the Visigoths.
- After the collapse of the Visigoth state, one of the leaders asked Muslims for help; they stayed on the peninsula.
- Charles Martel defeated the Muslims at Tours.
- The Catholic Monarchs – Isabella and Ferdinand – defeated the Muslims and then ordered all non-believers to convert.
- Discovery of America.
- The English defeated the Spanish at Calais and Spain ceased to be a naval power.
- At Trafalgar the British defeated the Franco-Spanish fleet.
- Franco seized power.
- Juan Carlos I Bourbon then took the throne.

## X. Space

- Konstantin Tsiolkovsky – a Russian who developed many theories of space travel.
- The Artemis Accords were concluded without Russia and China.

### Areas

- Terra
- Near-Earth space: satellites
- Lunar space
- Solar space

- There are 5 Lagrange points around Earth where the gravitational fields of Earth and the Moon cancel each other out – no fuel is needed there; they could become a source of conflict.
