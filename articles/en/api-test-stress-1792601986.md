# Content
- Stress itself may not be harmful — it is the *belief* that stress is bad for you that makes it dangerous.
- When you view your body's stress response (racing heart, faster breathing) as helpful, your physical stress response actually becomes healthier.
- Reframing stress as your body preparing you for a challenge can shift it from a threat response to a courage response.
- A major study tracking 30,000 adults found that high stress only increased the risk of dying among those who believed stress was harmful.
- Stress triggers the release of oxytocin, a hormone that motivates you to seek support and strengthens social connections.
- The oxytocin released during stress is cardioprotective — it helps the heart recover and reduces the harmful physical effects of stress.
- One of the most effective ways to reduce stress is to care for and connect with others; helping others can make you more resilient.
- McGonigal's core message: chasing a meaningful life will create stress, but trusting yourself to handle it — and leaning on others — makes you healthier.
