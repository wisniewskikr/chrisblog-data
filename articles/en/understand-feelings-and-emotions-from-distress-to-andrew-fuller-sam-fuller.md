# Content

## Introduction

- External senses: touch, smell, taste, sight, hearing, haptic (one's own body in space)
- Internal senses: emotions (felt by everyone) and feelings (not necessarily felt by everyone)
- Feelings are like unexpected guests at a dinner party – strange combinations, e.g. your worst enemy falling into a ravine in your new car
- Feelings are an archaic form of communication, faster than words
- Two types of people (who often marry each other): maximisers and minimisers
- Feelings demand attention, but that does not mean we should give in to them
- Feelings are an ancient language that helps us understand others and ourselves

## Acceptance

- Relying on oneself rather than others
- Acceptance is not passivity but awareness – you disagree, yet you accept it
- We have different selves and we should allow them to communicate

## Ambition

- Risk that after achieving a goal we feel not satisfaction but the desire for the next one
- Risk of neglecting other interests
- Two kinds of ambition: the ladder and the tapestry

## Apathy and boredom

- Every 7 years a person undergoes change
- What to do: get closer to nature, change something, get involved in something

## Pain and suffering

- Sit with the pain and examine it
- Suffering develops compassion – make use of that
- The pain after love is like withdrawing from an addiction

## Greed and acquisitiveness

- Greed is the desire to have more than others

## Curiosity

- Focus on questions, not on answers

## Déjà vu

## Anger

- Two types: hot heads and cold hearts
- Anger should neither be vented nor suppressed, but redirected
- The question "What can I do for you?" often calms an angry person

## Pity

- Pity means listening closely to other people's problems
- It is useful when it leads to action

## Love

- Two most important feelings: fear (pain) and love
- Four types of partners: the supporter, the leader (the follower), the muse, a younger version of oneself

## Hatred

- When we love, we want someone exclusively; when we hate, we want company

## Uncertainty

- Long-term uncertainty is unpleasant, but short-term uncertainty is thrilling
- The main function of the brain is prediction, i.e. overcoming uncertainty

## Disgust

- Disgust is meant to protect us
- We can feel disgust towards people when we treat them as members of a group rather than as individuals

## Obsession

- Unwanted, intrusive thoughts

## Rejection

- People who are terrified of rejection lose their sense of self

## Courage

- Courage is acting in the right way

## Self-confidence

- Self-confidence is recognising the qualities we possess
- Do not fall into the trap of avoidance

## Feelings of worthlessness and self-disgust

## Suspicion

- Suspicion is believing something is happening without being certain
- Suspicions arise quickly; trust builds slowly
- Binary thinking is fast but not always accurate

## Admiration and awe

- Awe is fleeting and serves as a gateway to gratitude

## Contempt

- Contempt is the intellectual version of disgust

## Failure

- Failure is placing a full stop where there should be a comma
- Statistically it is easier to do something badly than well
- Failure is an important part of learning

## Desire and longing

- Desire is not lasting
- Two things truly worth desiring: more time and health

## Forgiveness

- If we never forgave, we would have no friends

## Disappointment

- Things did not turn out the way we wanted

## Bitterness

- We hand matters over to external forces and become blind to good

## Loneliness

- The thought that nobody needs us

## Sadness

- Feeling unhappy, a lowering of mood
- Happiness requires effort; sadness comes on its own
- Ancient Greece and the only truly true statement: all this shall pass

## Peace of mind

- Peace of mind is a state of stability and composure – a calm presence
- Do not be swept away by the eight winds: success, fame, praise, pleasure, failure, decline, disgrace, pain
- Three zones: the zone of worry, the zone of resilience (the longer we stay here the better), and the zone of avoidance

## Fear

- Fear is like fire: when you control it, it warms you; when you do not, it burns you

## Happiness

- Happiness is a capricious friend who disappears quickly
- Happiness is adapting to the present moment
- Happiness is not only passive relaxation but also an absorbing challenge; flow: the bar set neither too high nor too low

## Identity and belonging

- A sense of belonging is the most powerful antidote to evil

## Trauma

- Trauma is a high level of stress and anxiety resulting from a past event
- Trauma causes one's mental age and emotional age to stop advancing
- To resolve it, one must find its source

## Resentment

- Resentment is the feeling of having been treated badly

## Addiction

- Addiction is a desperate entanglement

## Recognition

- Recognition is appreciating something

## Sensitivity

- Sensitivity is behaving in an authentic and genuine way

## Shame

- Shame is a signal that our balance has been disturbed

## Busyness and hurry

- The belief that the present moment is merely a prelude to another, better one
- Three types of employees: cats (loyal to a place), dogs (loyal to people), rats (loyal to themselves)

## Worrying and anxiety

- Worry consists of concerns and fears related to the current or future situation

## Enthusiasm

- Moments when we are brimming with energy
- The rule of threes: 3 hours for yourself per week, 3 days for yourself per month, 3 weeks for yourself per year

## Surprise

- An alarm signal that something important is happening
- Agatha Christie once disappeared for 11 days

## Trust

- Trust is an unbroken faith in something

## Envy

- Envy is a feeling of unhappiness caused by someone else's success or possessions

## Jealousy

- Jealousy is a negative feeling towards others who compete for the same resource

## Embarrassment

- Embarrassment is shame and social anxiety

## Betrayal

- Betrayal is a feeling of heartlessness and being awakened to it

## Regret

- We are wiser after the event

## Grief

- Grief is the price we pay for love

## Kindness

- Kindness is caring about others
- The opposite of kindness is heartlessness

## How not to go mad in a crazy world
