## 1. Why marriage

- Marriage requires both luck and work.
- Either one alone is not enough.

## 3. Marriage and families

- Distinguishing between wrongs: wrongs to the soul – fight hard; wrongs to sensitivity – let go.

## 4. The impact of children on marriage

- After having children, one must actively work on the marriage.
- Regular dates: a babysitter or family you will reciprocate for.
- Designated time for two, e.g. Saturday for sex, Sunday morning for a walk.
- Talk about everything, but not about the children.

## 5. Everyday married life

- Stay mindful on a daily basis.
- Do not attach too little importance to your spouse's feelings.
- It is not always worth telling the truth: negative feelings can be sincere but fleeting, and spoken words cannot be taken back.
- Marriage needs a balance between what is shared and what remains private.
- The romance of the ordinary: love is not only what you have at the beginning, but a constant, voluntary act.

## 6. Sibling rivalry in marriage

- Spouses often have their own territories, e.g. she listens to confidences, he can fix everything.
- Sometimes – consciously or not – they encroach on each other's territories.

## 7. Marital sex

- Types of sex: tender/passionate, playful/serious, conventional/perverse, hot/cold, quick/unhurried.
- Good sex requires enthusiasm and imagination.

## 8. War, peace, and carrying on

- Before a quarrel, consider whether it is necessary: will it solve the current problem, prevent a future one, or serve as a needed release of emotions.
- Never completely lose control of yourself.
- Remember that you will not always feel the way you do right now.
- Accept responsibility if the fault is on your side.
- Do not practice psychiatry on your spouse.
- Turn the matter into a joke if possible.
- Do not wait too long to have the quarrel.
- Know in advance what you want to get out of the quarrel.
- Do not say "If you loved me…".
- Stick to the point and to the present moment.
- Do not attack the Achilles' heel: something your spouse confided in trust.
- Do not exaggerate your grievances or deceive.
- Do not invoke authorities.
- Not only speak, but also listen.
- Respect not only facts but also feelings.
- Accept the lack of agreement on some issues.
- After a quarrel, stop the silent treatment.
- There need not be winners and losers – there can be a compromise.
- If you lose: admit that you caused damage and caused harm.

## 9. The divorce option

- A therapist's question: what is your spouse afraid of, and does your behaviour sharpen or ease that fear.
- Childhood problems: we may do to our spouse what was done to us, or what we would have liked to do to our parents.
- Unconscious expectations that are hard to meet: the spouse should be like one's parents or completely different from them, should fill the heart with love, ensure one never feels lonely, find the missing part of oneself.

## 10. Subsequent marriages

- Marriage breakdown rates: first marriages – 40%, subsequent marriages – 60%.
