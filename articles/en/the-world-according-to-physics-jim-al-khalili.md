# Content

## Introduction

- Doing physics means searching for explanations.
- To begin that search, one must first ask the right questions – that is the domain of philosophers.

## 1. Wonder at understanding

- Modern science has replaced storytelling.
- Types of sciences:
  - Physics: interaction between theory and experiment.
  - Mathematics: mainly logic and deduction.
  - Geology, ethology, psychology: observational sciences, data collection.
- In 2012 the Higgs boson was discovered using the Large Hadron Collider.

## 2. Scale

- Progress in physics was made possible in part by the telescope and the microscope.
- On 7 January 1610 Galileo observed the moons of Jupiter.
- In 1665 Robert Hooke and Antonie van Leeuwenhoek studied the world using the microscope.
- The Big Bang occurred 13.8242 billion years ago.
- Gravity acts on time: the stronger it is, the more slowly time passes.
- That is why time in satellites is deliberately slowed down – otherwise a navigation error of 10 kilometres per day would arise.
- Basic concepts of physics:
  - Universality: a theory works everywhere.
  - Symmetry: a property of a system remains the same when something else changes.
  - Reductionism: the whole is nothing more than the sum of its parts.

## 3. Space and time

- Space does not exist independently of time, and it is light that connects them.
- The speed of light is constant – it is time that changes.
- A body falls to earth because it seeks the place where time passes most slowly – it tries to age more slowly.
- Matter and energy create the gravitational field; without them there is no time or space.
- Galaxies are like raisins in a loaf of bread rising in an oven: the loaf expands but the raisins stay the same size.
- Matter, energy, space and time are close companions.

## 4. Energy and matter

- Energy is the ability to do work.
- Types of energy: kinetic (a body in motion), potential (a body in a gravitational field), thermal (a heated body).
- Energy is divided into useful energy and waste energy.
- The law of conservation of energy: the total amount of energy in the universe is constant.
- Work is the ability to move something against an acting force.
- The mass of a body is a measure of the substance contained in it.
- The faster a body moves, the more its mass increases.
- The total amount of energy and mass in the universe is constant.
- On the microscopic scale, materials are held together by the electromagnetic force between atoms.
- On the cosmic scale, gravity binds matter together.
- Quarks attract each other through the exchange of gluons.
- A quark and an electron interact through the exchange of photons; there is also the exchange of bosons.
- 3/4 of the mass of the universe is hydrogen and 1/4 is helium.
- Heavy elements – everything beyond iron in the periodic table (silver, gold, lead, uranium) – can only form during the explosion of novae or supernovae.

## 5. The quantum world

- The double-slit experiment: the result is as if the particle passes through both slits simultaneously.
- All electromagnetic radiation is emitted in packets – quanta.
- Wave–particle duality: electrons or light can behave as a particle or a wave.
- Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: one can know only the speed or the position of a particle, not both simultaneously.
- The concept of entanglement: two particles become connected in a way that defies logic.

## 6. Thermodynamics and the arrow of time

- The world moves from an ordered state of low entropy to a disordered state of high entropy.
- Time in different theories:
  - Theory of relativity: connected with space.
  - Quantum mechanics: a parameter with no direction.
  - Thermodynamics: an arrow from the past to the future, towards increasing entropy.

## 7. Unification

- Newton: gravitational attraction is directly proportional to the product of the masses of two objects and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
- Maxwell: electricity and magnetism are in essence one electromagnetic force.
- There are also two further forces: the strong and weak nuclear interactions.
- String theory posits four spatial dimensions and six hidden ones.

## 8. The future of physics

- Dark matter: inferred from observations of stars; it does not feel the electromagnetic force, so a hand made of dark matter would pass through a table.
- Dark energy is a substance acting against gravity that causes the universe to expand ever faster.

## 9. The usefulness of physics

- Life has the ability to maintain itself in a state of low entropy, far from thermal equilibrium, and can store and process information.

## 10. Thinking like a physicist

- We will cease to be human if we cease to be curious.
