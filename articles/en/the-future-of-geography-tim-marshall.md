# Content

## Introduction

- The Big Bang occurred 13.7 billion years ago.
- The Sun formed 4.6 billion years ago.
- The main space powers are: the USA, Russia, and China.

## 1. Look up

- Nabta Playa in Egypt: astronomical observations made some 7,000 years ago.
- Stonehenge in England: a structure built some 5,000 years ago.
- 8th century BC: the Babylonians borrowed the signs of the zodiac from the Sumerians and divided time into 7-day weeks.
- In ancient Greece, Hipparchus invented the astrolabe, which allowed one to read position, date, time, and horoscope.
- Aristotle concluded that the Earth is round because the shadow it casts on the Moon is an arc; he estimated Earth's circumference at 72,000 km (the actual value is about 40,000 km).
- Eratosthenes of Cyrene measured Earth's circumference: knowing that the Sun shines directly overhead in Syene once a year, he measured the shadow on the same day in Alexandria and then the distance between the two cities – the result was 40,000 km.
- Around 300 BC, Aristarchus of Samos proposed a heliocentric model of the solar system.
- Around 100 AD, Claudius Ptolemy described 48 constellations (88 are recognised today).
- Around 500 AD, the Chinese scholar Zu Chongzhi developed the Great Brightness Calendar based on a 365-day year.
- Around 900 AD, the Arab astronomer Al-Battani discovered that the distance between the Sun and the Earth is variable.
- 1543: Copernicus published *On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres*, placing the Sun at the centre of the system.
- 1584: Giordano Bruno published *On the Infinity of the Universe and Worlds*, defending Copernicus's theses; he was imprisoned for 8 years and subsequently burned at the stake.
- 1610: Galileo published *Sidereus Nuncius*, criticising the geocentric model; he used a telescope and discovered gravitational acceleration by dropping objects from the Leaning Tower.
- 1623: Galileo published *Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems*, asserting that the Earth moves; he was sentenced to house arrest, where he died in 1642 (in 1992 the Vatican acknowledged its error).
- 1643: Isaac Newton was born, the year after Galileo's death.
- Newton constructed a new type of telescope.
- 1687: Newton published *Principia*, discovering that gravity acts everywhere.

## 2. The road to the heavens

- In the 9th century, Chinese monks invented gunpowder: a mixture of sulphur, saltpetre, and charcoal.
- 1903: the Russian Tsiolkovsky published a theory of spaceflight.
- The American Goddard launched a rocket powered by liquid fuel rather than gunpowder.
- The German Oberth designed V2 rockets (revenge weapons).
- 1942: the German von Braun launched the first suborbital rocket (reaching 100 km altitude).
- 1957: the Russian Korolev launched Sputnik-1.
- A few months later, the dog Laika flew into space aboard Sputnik-2.
- January 1958: the Juno 1 rocket placed the American satellite Explorer 1 into orbit.
- 1959: the Soviet rocket Luna 2 struck the Moon.
- The Soviets launched the dogs Belka and Strelka.
- 1961: the Americans launched the chimpanzee Ham.
- 1961: the Soviets launched Gagarin aboard Vostok 1; before launch he urinated on the wheel of the bus – a tradition was born; he completed one orbit of the Earth, the flight lasting 108 minutes.
- Three weeks later, Shepard became the second human in space.
- 1968: the Americans orbited the Moon.
- 21 July 1969: Apollo 11 mission – Neil Armstrong, Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin, and Michael Collins.

## 3. The era of astropolitics

- Rockets should launch near the equator, where Earth's rotational speed is greatest: USA – Florida, EU – French Guiana, Russia – Kazakhstan.
- Flight altitudes: 12 km – cruising altitude; 80 km – boundary of near-Earth space; above that – cislunar space (between Earth and the Moon); 160–2,000 km – low Earth orbit; 2,000–35,786 km – medium orbit (GPS, military AEHF); above that – high orbit.
- Lagrange points: locations where the gravitational forces of two bodies (e.g. Earth and the Sun) cancel each other out.
- Van Allen belts: regions of elevated radiation.
- Helium-3 (³He) can be used for nuclear fusion.
- A journey to Mars currently takes about 7 months.

## 4. Outlaws

- Colonies created by private investors pose a legal problem.
- Space debris is a problem.
- A solar flare could be a threat.
- An incoming satellite could be a threat.

## 5. China: the long march... into space

- Heads of national space programmes: USA – von Braun, Russia – Korolev, China – Qian Xuesen.
- 2007: China destroyed one of its own satellites with a kinetic kill vehicle (KKV).
- A Chinese lunar base is planned for completion by 2035.
- China has its own space station, Tiangong.

## 6. The United States: back to the future

- 1972: the last crewed Moon landing.
- Apollo missions were replaced by cheaper Space Shuttle missions.
- 1986: the Challenger disaster.
- 2003: the Columbia disaster.
- Gateway is a planned station orbiting the Moon.

## 7. Russia in retreat

- GLONASS is Russia's equivalent of GPS.
- 2021: Russia conducted a test of its ASAT anti-satellite weapon.
- Kalina is a laser intended to be capable of destroying satellites.
- Russia is building the Vostochny Cosmodrome.

## 8. Fellow travellers

- The ESA (European Space Agency) has 22 members.
- Europe's navigation system is called Galileo.
- The intelligence facility Pine Gap is located in Australia.

## 9. Space wars

- The most valuable element of space infrastructure is the early-warning system.

## 10. The world of tomorrow

- Moon landings will probably take place at the poles to avoid extreme temperature differences; people may live in underground caves.
- Elon Musk plans a mission to Mars.
- Jeff Bezos and his Blue Origin plan to build orbital cities around the Earth; they would need to rotate, but not too fast – a minimum circumference of at least one kilometre.
- A solar sail driven by photons can accelerate continuously, reaching up to 10% of the speed of light.
