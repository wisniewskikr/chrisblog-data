# Content

## Great minds discuss ideas, average minds discuss events, small minds discuss people; Buckle

### Introduction

- Labelling: naming the other person's emotions out loud
- Mirroring: repeating the last thing the other person said
- Active listening

## Part I. Judging a book by its cover

### Chapter 1

- Profiling does not work – in the United Kingdom it succeeded in 2.7% of cases; the FBI does not publish its data
- Barnum effect: people consider a general description accurate if they believe a special diagnostic tool was used
- Base rate: the average frequency with which a given phenomenon occurs

## Chapter 2

- Observer effect: an observer unconsciously provides answers – the phenomenon was identified through the story of Clever Hans; the solution is a double-blind trial
- Egocentric anchoring: when judging people we are correct at most 35% of the time, yet we think we are correct 90% of the time
- Lazy Brain Axiom: we read others' emotions better when we are curious about them and have motivation to do so
- To read someone's emotions accurately, observe them in different situations and with different people

## Chapter III

- HSAM is a superior autobiographical memory – the person forgets nothing that has happened to them
- First impressions are remarkably accurate: we are right 70% of the time
- Confirmation bias: we seek information that confirms our position; how to counter it:
  - Take responsibility for your own opinions
  - Distance yourself before making a decision
  - Consider an opposing opinion
- Practical tips: make a good first impression and give people a second chance

## Chapter IV

- The Friendly Journalist Method: allows lie detection – first gather background information, then show curiosity and encourage the person to talk
- Cognitive load: forcing the other person to think intensively and watching for the signs
- Lie-detection techniques:
  - Ask unexpected questions
  - Strategic use of evidence

## Chapter 5

- Summary of interpersonal abilities:
  - First impressions: 70% accuracy
  - Lie detection: 50% accuracy
  - Reading others' thoughts and feelings: at most 35% accuracy
- Better to focus not on improving your own abilities but on obtaining more signals from the other person
- Less trusting people miss many opportunities and suffer financial losses comparable to not completing a university degree; trusting people earn more
- Rather than avoiding judging a book by its cover, it is better to invest more effort in revising your judgements

## Part II. Do you find out who your true friends are in times of hardship?

### Chapter 6

- Friendship exists when costs and benefits are not the most important thing
- People with 5 or more friends are 60% more likely to consider themselves very happy
- 70% of satisfaction in marriage comes from friendship – it is 5 times more important than physical intimacy
- Having 3 friends at work is associated with 96% job satisfaction
- 58% of your happiness relates to matters of friendship
- Loneliness is equivalent to smoking 15 cigarettes a day
- Friendship is not formalised – if you do not tend to it, it withers
- In friendship, the ratio of positives to negatives is 8:1

## Chapter 7.

- Altruism is in our genes: the same brain areas are activated as for food and sex
- Aristotle: a friend is disposed towards us as we are disposed towards ourselves; a friend is a second self
- Self-expansion: the other becomes me, the friend becomes me

## Chapter 8

- Williams syndrome: people with this syndrome have extraordinary abilities in kindness, empathy, and social interaction due to overproduction of oxytocin – the bonding hormone
- The opposite of sociopaths: they have competence but lack warmth
- Signalling theory – saying you will do something versus actually doing it requires a costly signal:
  - Time: dedicate time to a friend, ideally as a ritual (e.g. gym together, a weekly call) – at least once every 2 weeks
  - Authenticity: revealing your true self signals that the person belongs to an exclusive circle; trust creates trust
- Time needed to build a friendship:
  - 60 hours: closer acquaintance
  - 100 hours: full friendship
  - 200 hours: kindred spirit
- Beautiful Mess Effect: people do not view our blunders as negatively as we ourselves do
- Terrifying Rule: if saying something terrifies you, say it

## Chapter 9

- For every 10 friends gained, we gain 1 enemy
- Frenemies are worse than enemies – we do not know what to expect from them
- Ties with Clinical Narcissists should be cut – they are unable to include others in their sense of self; it is possible to work with Subclinical Narcissists by:
  1. Emphasising similarities
  2. Emphasising your authentic feelings
  3. Emphasising a sense of community
- Narcissists do not feel empathy, but they do feel shame

## Chapter 10

- Summary:
  - A friend is a second self – which is why the dictates of Darwinism do not apply
  - In friendship, no ledger of gains and losses is kept
  - Friendship has no formal institution – it must be constantly invested in
  - The cost of friendship is time and authenticity

## III. Does love conquer all?

### Chapter 11

- Virgil: *Omnia vincit amor* (Love conquers all)
- In the USA, 40% of marriages end in divorce; 10–15% of couples are separated; 7% remain in unhappy marriages
- Divorce occurs on average around 4 years after the wedding
- Survivorship bias: studies show married people are happier than single people, but divorcees, separated people, and widowers are incorrectly counted among the "single" group
- Result: a good marriage makes you happy, a bad one makes you unhappy
- After divorce, wellbeing almost never returns to its previous level
- Loneliness within a marriage is much worse than being alone
- Historically, marriage had more to do with economics than love – it was insurance; the goal was not to find a partner but to find in-laws
- Current causes of marriage breakdown:
  - Higher expectations of marriage
  - Less time devoted to marriage
  - Marriage takes away close friends
- Today's good marriage is nonetheless better than any in history
- People who believe in a fairytale marriage are most often unhappy – fairytales are passive, while marriage requires proactive work: time and energy

## Chapter 12.

- Romantic love can be the best thing that happens to us in life
- It most often fades after 1–1.5 years
- In the past, love was treated as an illness; modern science confirms this – it resembles mania or depression, an obsessive-compulsive disorder, and an addiction
- Goals of love:
  - From an evolutionary perspective: reproduction
  - From the individual's perspective: forming bonds
- Love requires proof of madness; madness is proof of loyalty, and loyalty is the opposite of selfishness – so the irrationality of love is rational
- Features of love:
  - Madness
  - Jealousy
  - Idealisation: a predictor of happiness in marriage – you see your partner's flaws but do not dwell on them; others do not seem as attractive
- Love is blind and should be
- Post-wedding dynamics:
  - 2 years after the wedding: intensity as before the wedding
  - 4 years after the wedding: decline of 15–20%
  - Companionate love follows
- After the first year of marriage, the frequency of sex drops by half
- 15% of married couples had no sex for 6 months

## Chapter 13.

- NOS (negative observation sentiment): a dominance of negative feelings about a partner's weaknesses; the opposite of idealisation
- It starts with secrecy – something bothers you but you do not say it; you say less and less, assume more and more; communication is essential
- Arguments are fine – it is not arguments that end a marriage but the avoidance of conflict; it is better to voice concerns more often
- The goal is to regulate, not resolve conflict; observe rules akin to the Geneva Convention; people remember not what was said but how they felt
- Four mistakes of unhappy couples:
  1. Criticism: complaining is fine, criticism is not – you complain about behaviour, you criticise the person
  2. Stonewalling: silence in response to problems; adrenaline drops more slowly in men than in women – ask for a 20-minute break and return to the discussion
  3. Defensiveness: making excuses, shifting blame, saying "yes, but…"
  4. Contempt: treating the partner as inferior, mocking, belittling
- How to argue:
  - The start of an argument matters – it must not be harsh
  - Complain, do not criticise
  - Describe what worries you in a neutral way
  - Start with a positive attitude
  - Hold your partner's hand
  - Make a joke
  - Treat your partner gently
- In marriage, the ratio of positives to negatives should be 5:1

## Chapter 14.

- Types of memory:
  - Semantic: abstract things
  - Episodic: personal events
  - Procedural: how to do things
  - Emotional
- Mindsets:
  - NOS: you see only bad things
  - POS (positive observation sentiment): you see only good things
- POS is automatic at the beginning; later it must be cultivated consciously
- The 4P Method:
  - Passionate pursuits (Podsycanie): doing stimulating things together; boredom kills love; dancing, skiing, concerts; interaction, challenges, movement, play; adrenaline makes the heart more receptive; any strong emotion can generate love
  - Probing (Przywoływanie): love is three-quarters curiosity; learning as much as possible about your partner and building a love map – knowing what to avoid; e.g. sharing good things that happened at work
  - Preserving (Przywracanie): we change only when we feel we do not have to; support your partner in becoming who they want to be; treat them as if they already are that person; people see themselves as others see them
  - Plotting (Przekształcanie): we seek people who tell a similar love story – business-like, fairytale, domestic, etc.; you must know your own story – who have you been drawn to? Ask friends for feedback, as you are not objective; facts do not matter, the story does; use the word "we", avoid "you"

## Chapter 15

- Today marriage is an "all or nothing" proposition – you can win or lose a great deal
- Love is a story, not a perfect reflection of facts; we want a lie that will, over time, become better than the truth
- Shared paranoia – *folie à deux*

## IV. Is no one really an island?

### Chapter 16.

- In 1920 in the USA only 1% of the population lived alone; now it is 1/4.
- Solitude gives freedom and control.
- Loneliness is very harmful – good relationships extend life by 10 years.
- Loneliness is bad; solitude is good.
- Loneliness does not stem from solitude; it is an individual assessment of the meaningfulness or meaninglessness of encounters with others.
- Need for communal interdependence: we are free, but we still pursue goals together.

## Chapter 17

- A child's popularity influences adult happiness more than IQ, background, or psychological problems.
- Popularity and status are two different things: status gives control but is not satisfying in the long run.
- Status matters more than money – it is better to have $2 when others have $1 than to have $3 when others have $4.
- Focusing on status gives power and control but takes away likability and willingness to cooperate.
- Technology is not bad if it is used to organise in-person meetings.
- Currently we have neither community nor solitude.

## Chapter 18

- Placebo has an effect, just like care and attention.
- Effectiveness = medicine + placebo; placebo works like a service warning light – someone cares, so the light goes off.
- Depression is the ratio of problems to stabilisers (support).
- Our brains are dependent on other people.
- Homo sapiens beat Neanderthals because they were more inclined to cooperate.
- During a shared disaster people unite rather than exploit one another.
- People need to feel that they are needed.
- A friend is a second self – but this can be extended to entire communities.
- Spending time in person with people to whom you are bonded reduces the risk of death by 50%.
- The more convinced you are that happiness is a social matter, the better it is for you.

## Chapter 19

- Homo sapiens has existed for 125,000 years.
- There is a need for balance between community and solitude.
- We prioritise status, power, and fame over being likable and loved.
- Lack of community leads to a sense of threat and seeking control in life and relationships.
- Social media is not bad, but it cannot replace relationships.
- We need a sense of belonging and a sense that someone is looking after us.
- We have gone too far in individualism.
- A hero must save others and occasionally be saved themselves.

## Something like a conclusion

- The meaning of life remains unknown.
- A sense of belonging is a source of a sense of meaning in life.
- A problem arises when the story of group A does not agree with the story of group B – the solution is more stories.
