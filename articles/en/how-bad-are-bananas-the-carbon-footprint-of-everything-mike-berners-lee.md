# Content

## Introduction

- 2010: temperature rise of 0.88°C
- 2020: temperature rise of 1.1°C (a 25% increase over 10 years)

## A short lecture on carbon intensity and carbon footprints

- Greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide, methane (28× more potent), nitrous oxide (265× more potent), refrigerants (several thousand times more potent)
- Average carbon footprint in Europe: 13 tonnes of CO₂ per person per year
- Average carbon footprint worldwide: 7 tonnes of CO₂ per person per year
- Target to achieve: 5 tonnes of CO₂ per person per year

## Below 10 g

- Taxing emails is a good idea

## From 10 g to 100 g

- Root vegetables (e.g. carrots) are the most climate-friendly
- An electric bike produces 20 times less emissions than a regular car

## From 100 g to 500 g

- An aerated shower head delivers a better experience with less water use
- Solar thermal collectors for water heating are the most efficient option
- Combining photovoltaic panels with heat pumps is worthwhile
- A good eco dishwasher run at night is a beneficial choice

## From 500 g to 1 kg

- An electric car is the best choice
- A Kindle pays off after reading 36 books

## From 1 to 10 kg

- The best plant-based milks are oat or soy
- 1.5 litres of cow's milk per day amounts to about 1 tonne of CO₂ per year
- Cows, sheep and lambs are ruminants – the methane they belch raises their carbon footprint
- Asparagus is highly emissive when transported by plane
- Flowers transported by plane are very emissive and take up space meant for food
- The carbon footprint of one egg equals that of 3 bananas
- The best protein sources are nuts, peas, lentils and chickpeas
- 1 kg of cheese requires 10 litres of milk – cheese has a higher carbon footprint than meat
- Rice is very emissive because it releases methane during cultivation
- Plastic production accounts for 3% of global CO₂ emissions, and this figure continues to rise

## From 10 to 100 kg

- Crocs are the shoes with the lowest carbon footprint
- It is better to choose synthetic fibres over natural ones
- Cement accounts for 4.5% of the global carbon footprint
- Mobile phones account for 1% of global emissions

## From 100 kg to 1,000 kg

- In the carbon footprint of petrol, two-thirds come from combustion and the rest from extraction and transport
- Insulating buildings is a very good solution

## From 1 t to 10 t

- Steel production: switching from coke to hydrogen reduces CO₂ emissions by 50% with only a 10% increase in costs

## From 10 t to 1,000 t

- Electric cars can generally be considered about half as harmful as petrol cars
- Annual carbon footprint per person: 0.2 t (Malawian), 7 t (global average), 8 t (Chinese), 13 t (British/European), 20 t (Australian), 21 t (American)
- A wind turbine pays back its carbon footprint within 3 months to 1 year; the larger it is, the better
- Retrofitting homes is the most beneficial solution from a CO₂ reduction perspective

## Millions of tonnes

- Volcanoes together emit about 1% of what humans emit annually; they also eject ash and sulphur dioxide, which reflect solar radiation and cause cooling
- The carbon footprint of a single mine equals that of 650,000 people per year
- Cryptocurrencies account for 0.5% of global emissions
- The highest per-capita carbon footprints are found in the USA, Canada and Australia

## Billions of tonnes

- Positive feedback loops in the climate system: loss of white ice cover (replaced by dark surfaces) and forest fires
- The IT sector accounts for 2.5% of global emissions
- Since 1990 we have lost 10% of forests; every year we lose an area the size of Switzerland
- Causes of forest loss: 27% commercial (animal feed), 26% forestry, 24% subsistence, 23% fires
- Organisation supporting forest protection: Woodland Carbon Guarantee

## Negative emissions

- Offsetting 5 tonnes of CO₂ requires 100 m² of forest (about 4 tennis courts)
- Organisations involved in tropical forest protection: Woodland Trust, World Land Trust, WWF, Cool Earth, Puro.earth (biochar)
- Surface sequestration: spreading an 8 mm layer of dunite on the ground
- DACCS: carbon capture and storage technology

## What can each of us do?

- Two things must be done: reduce personal emissions and push for systemic change
- Typical breakdown of a carbon footprint: food 23%, vehicle fuel 11%, vehicle 3%, flights 9%, healthcare 10%
- Environmentally friendly brands: www.ethicalconsumer.org
