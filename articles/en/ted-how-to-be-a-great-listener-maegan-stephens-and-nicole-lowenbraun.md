# Content

- There is no single right way to be a great listener — great listening takes many forms.
- Responding is also part of listening; how you respond matters as much as how you hear.
- Stop listening in the way *you* prefer and start listening in the way *your conversation partner* needs — be an adaptive listener.
- There are four types of listening, forming the acronym **SAID**:
  1. **Discern** — filtering what is important from what is not. The goal is to work through all the points to reach a conclusion; it is better to spend 5 minutes thinking than 5 hours doing the wrong thing.
  2. **Immerse** — absorbing as much information as possible (e.g. on your first day at a new job). The goal is to build the big picture.
  3. **Advance** — listening in order to achieve a goal or make a decision.
  4. **Support** — listening because someone simply needs to be heard.
- Adapting your listening style to the situation builds trust and leads to better results.
