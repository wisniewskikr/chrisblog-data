# Content

- Motivation is not something you simply find — it is a skill you learn and actively shape.
- To become more motivated, you can change your situation (e.g. get a dog if you want to walk more) or change how you think about it.
- Most goals are abandoned because people are excited about the outcome, not the process of pursuing the goal.
- Motivation tends to be high at the start and near the finish, but drops in the middle — this is the „middle slump.”
- Solutions to the middle slump:
  1. Make the middle shorter by breaking big goals into smaller ones (weekly or monthly goals).
  2. Remove ego from the equation — don't dwell too much on setbacks.
  3. Combine activities, but find the right combination so neither undermines the other.
  4. Anticipate disruptions — when we predict obstacles in advance, we become less vulnerable to them.
- Social motivation is powerful: doing something with someone, or simply in someone's presence, boosts persistence.
- Friends and family can be a valuable source of ongoing motivation.
