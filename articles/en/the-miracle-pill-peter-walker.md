# Content

## Weekly goal for an adult:
- 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity
- Daily goal for children under 5: 3 hours; over 5: 1 hour

### 1. The long-term lack of a daily dose of movement
- Homo sapiens appeared approximately 312,000 years ago; agriculture began around 12,000 years ago
- Bone density is shaped by physical activity during childhood and adolescence
- The Amish take between 14,000 and 18,000 steps a day; the average Canadian takes around 5,000 steps
- Governments should focus on everyday physical activity rather than competitive sport

## 2. The miracle chart: small dose, big effect
- Physical exertion is embedded in human evolution
- Types of muscle: skeletal (consciously controlled), smooth (stomach, intestines) and cardiac
- Telomeres are shortened by poor diet, lack of sleep and physical inactivity
- Physical inactivity is responsible for approximately 10% of deaths – more than smoking or obesity

## 3. The man who rediscovered movement
- In 1948 Morris investigated why bus drivers suffered far more heart attacks than conductors; a similar pattern was observed among postal workers
- Paffenbarger conducted long-term studies at Harvard in the USA
- It was previously widely believed that physical exertion was harmful to health by overstraining the heart

## 4. The great wave. How physical inactivity is bankrupting governments
- Key health problems: comorbidities and polypharmacy
- The British NHS started with a budget of 100 million pounds; it now stands at 124 billion pounds
- The four most dangerous non-communicable diseases: cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, cancer and diabetes
- The four biggest risk factors: tobacco, alcohol, obesity and physical inactivity
- The current average length of the treatment period before death is between 16 and 20 years

## 5. Cities on a human scale
- Gehl is a Danish architect who designed Copenhagen with pedestrians and cyclists in mind

## 6. Being slim is not enough. How physical inactivity and obesity differ
- It is better to be physically active and overweight than to be slim and sedentary
- When a person becomes less active, appetite does not decrease – so one must either increase activity or endure constant hunger
- Visceral (intra-abdominal) fat poses the greatest health risk
- Instead of BMI, measuring waist circumference is recommended – it should not exceed 94 centimetres
- Maintaining weight requires between 45 and 60 minutes of moderate exercise per day
- The system (the environment we live in) works against physical activity

## 7. Your daily routine is in danger
- Sitting on a chair is a historical novelty – it was popularised by the French court and office workers; previously people squatted
- Active sitting (at work) is less harmful than passive sitting (at home)
- One should get up regularly and break prolonged periods of sitting

## 8. Youth, age and why movement matters throughout life
- The "Daily Mile" initiative encourages children to run or walk every day
- Slovenia is the only country in the world where the proportion of obese children is declining
- Good health in old age should be treated as a conscious life project
- Measuring physical fitness: the sit-to-stand (chair) test and the Araújo test

## 9. The power of social engineering
- Joensuu – a city in Finland where residents cycle all year round
- In Slovenia a national physical fitness exam for children is held every year; schools are well equipped and trips to former military bases are organised

## 10. What now? A new era of health
- Governments should stay several steps ahead of public opinion and actively guide it on matters of health
- Post-meal processing of fats by the body plays an important role
