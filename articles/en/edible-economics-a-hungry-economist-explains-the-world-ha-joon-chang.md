# Content

## Introduction

- In Korea, around 9 kg of garlic is consumed per person per year.
- Britain underwent a huge gastronomic transformation when the British discovered how poor their food was.

## Part one. On overcoming prejudice

### 1. Acorn

- Jews fled from Spain to Muslim Turkey, where they simply paid higher taxes.
- Muhammad was a merchant, which is why trade held high status among Muslims.
- Caste systems existed in India and China; in China talented peasants could become officials by passing an exam, but craftsmen and merchants could not.
- Culture itself is neither good nor bad: Islam encompasses both poor and wealthy countries (e.g. Malaysia); Confucian philosophy has been judged differently at different times, and the Japanese were once considered lazy.
- State incentives can change cultural outcomes.

## 2. Okra

- Enslaved people in the USA cultivated tobacco and cotton, which accounted for 25% to 65% of exports.
- Haiti declared independence from France in 1804 and became the first country in the world to abolish slavery.
- Napoleon sold the entire Louisiana Territory to the USA – a consequence of the Haitian uprising.
- The Oregon Territory was purchased by the USA from Britain.
- In the war with Mexico, the USA acquired half of Mexican territory for a token price.
- After the abolition of slavery, indentured labour by Indians and Chinese appeared in the USA.
- Friedman and his school defined freedom very narrowly, disregarding social and political freedom.

## 3. Coconut

- It is a myth that inhabitants of poor tropical countries work little.
- They work 60–80% longer than Europeans and 25–40% longer than Americans.
- They are equally capable – the difference lies in conditions, not people: as if a jockey switched from a donkey to a thoroughbred horse.
- Causes of lower productivity: colonially enforced specialisation in cheap goods, political problems, failures of elites, and the injustice of the international system.

## Part two. On increasing productivity

### 4. Anchovy

- Peru grew wealthy on guano – bird droppings used as fertiliser.
- The German scientist Haber isolated nitrogen from the air using high voltage and received the Nobel Prize for it; he later invented poison gas used in World War I.
- The Netherlands is the second-largest exporter of agricultural products after the USA.
- Industrialisation can overcome natural limits: synthetic dyes replaced natural ones, artificial fertilisers reduced dependence on guano, and skills – unlike resources – do not run out.

## 5. Shrimp

- In the case of new industries, the state must protect them like a child, but gradually reduce that protection.

## 6. Pasta

- Innovative products are not only the work of visionaries, but also of many other people and of the state that supports them.

## 7. Carrot

- The problem of overlapping patents – knowledge creates knowledge.
- Solutions: a patent pool, shortening the duration of patent protection, or a one-off financial prize.

## Part three. On acting well globally

### 8. Beef

- Poor countries were forced to adopt free trade.
- WTO rules favour rich countries, because they were most often the ones setting the rules.
- Five members of the UN have veto power.
- In the World Bank, voting power depends on financial contribution, so wealthier countries are more powerful.

## 9. Bananas

- Bananas can yield up to a hundredfold more than potatoes per hectare, with minimal input.
- The American United Fruit Company (UFC) and Standard Fruit Company (SFC) were called El Pulpo (the Octopus) because they controlled everything.
- American marines regularly intervened in South American countries to protect the interests of banana companies – from 1898 to 1934, the USA entered 10 countries and attacked 28 times.
- The banana massacre in Colombia – workers went on strike demanding, among other things, toilets.
- Corporations can bring profit to a country, but the state must oversee this; positive examples include Costa Rica, South Korea, and Singapore.
- A negative example is the Philippines: massive electronics exports, but no transfer of local knowledge.

## 10. Coca-Cola

- Cola was invented by Pemberton of Atlanta – from coca leaves and kola nuts; both are now synthetic. Originally the drink contained alcohol, but when Prohibition was introduced he removed the alcohol and added sugar.
- Morales in Bolivia opposed the Washington Consensus (the Treasury Department, IMF, and World Bank) and nationalised part of the economy – inequality fell and the economy accelerated.
- The Pink Tide: other Latin American countries also withdrew from neoliberalism.
- Neoliberalism preaches freedom and minimal state intervention, but excludes freedom in the areas of currencies (central banking) and ideas (intellectual property).

## Part four. On living together

### 11. Rye

- Rye originates from Turkey and is more resilient than wheat.
- Bismarck forged an alliance between rye producers and iron producers; he also invented the welfare state and social insurance.
- The welfare state is not charity but insurance; VAT burdens the poor more heavily. Collective insurance is more efficient – the USA, without universal health insurance, pays 40% more for healthcare.

## 12. Chicken

- People have equal needs only up to a certain point; beyond that, needs diverge.
- Free-market advocates argue for no restrictions, but the problem of equality of opportunity arises.

## 13. Chilli

- Chilli tricks our taste receptors – in reality nothing is being damaged.
- GDP does not count domestic work, which makes up 30–40% of all work.

## Part five. On thinking about the future

### 14. Lime

- The British Empire was governed through its navy, and the navy's greatest enemy was scurvy.
- Scurvy was prevented by grog: lemon, water, rum, and sugar.
- Agriculture accounts for 15–30% of greenhouse gas emissions.
- The state must support private initiatives in innovation.

## 15. Spices

- The search for spices led to the invention of limited liability companies: the English East India Company and the Dutch East India Company – the first corporations.
- The current problem with corporations is their short-term thinking. Proposed solutions:
  1. The longer you hold shares, the greater your voting rights.
  2. Limit the power of shareholders (who can withdraw at any time) in favour of stakeholders (employees, etc.).
  3. Restrict shareholders' options, especially speculative ones.

## 16. Strawberry

- Automation destroys some jobs but creates new ones; however, state support for retraining workers is needed.

## 17. Chocolate

- Singapore and Switzerland are among the most industrialised economies in the world.
- Manufacturing accounts for 60–70% of research and development expenditure.

## Conclusion

- How to look at economics:
  1. Appreciate diversity.
  2. Be open to new things.
  3. Trace the origin of ingredients (where ideas and resources come from).
  4. Use your imagination.
