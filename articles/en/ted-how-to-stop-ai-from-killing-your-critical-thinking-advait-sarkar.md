# Content

- Working with AI reduces several key human cognitive capacities: creativity, critical thinking, memory, and metacognition (thinking about thinking).
- Instead of doing the work for us, AI should help us *understand* the work.
- Instead of making us faster, AI should help us produce higher-quality output.
- Instead of delivering ready answers, AI should help us ask the right questions.
- Instead of automating what we already know, AI should help us explore what we don't yet know.
- Sarkar proposes a different kind of AI tool: when reading a document, rather than a chatbot that solves problems, the user gets a gentle assistant that only offers "provocations" — noticed issues and suggested angles of thought.
- These provocations are not solutions; the user decides whether to accept or ignore them and must do the intellectual work themselves.
- This approach nudges reflection and is designed to make users smarter over time, not more dependent.
