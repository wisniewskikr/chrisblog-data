# Content

## 1. The new human agenda
- For centuries humanity was plagued by three main afflictions: famine, plague, and war.
- Today more people die from overeating than from hunger.
- We are programmed for survival and reproduction, not for happiness.
- Happiness is the golden mean between excitement and calm.
- Knowledge causes change, and change makes knowledge obsolete.

### I. Homo sapiens conquers the world

## 2. The Anthropocene
- Emotions are common to all mammals.
- An algorithm is a set of steps.
- Humans are algorithms that operate through sensations, emotions, and thoughts – calculating survival and reproduction.

## 3. The human spark
- Question: why is a human life worth more than a pig's life?
- Argument 1 – the soul: if the soul is invisible, the theory of evolution cannot accept it.
- Argument 2 – the conscious mind: it consists of sensations and desires; emotions serve survival, but it is unclear what happens in the mind that does not happen in the brain; the current theory treats pain and pleasure as the brain's "by-products".
- New Zealand and Quebec have recognised animals as sentient beings.
- Argument 3 – self-awareness: the chimpanzee Santino in Sweden hid stones and threw them at people.
- Human superiority does not lie in tools or intelligence – ancient humans were more capable in those respects.
- The true advantage is the ability to cooperate with strangers through abstraction.
- Homo sapiens follows warm social logic rather than cold mathematical logic (example: the ultimatum game – rejecting an offer that is too low); we have a built-in egalitarianism essential for survival.
- With large masses, abstract stories come into play.
- Three kinds of reality: objective (always existed), subjective (felt only by a given individual), and intersubjective (invented).

### II. Homo sapiens gives meaning to the world

## 4. The storytellers
- The Sumerians invented writing and money.
- Aristides de Sousa Mendes – defying the orders of the Portuguese authorities, he issued thousands of passports to Jews fleeing France during World War II.
- Sometimes bureaucracy triumphs over reality (example: the map of Africa).
- When studying history, it is worth looking at it from the perspective of a real being and asking: is it suffering?
- Fictions are necessary, but they must not become our goals or our measure of value.

## 5. The odd couple
- Religion consists of three elements: ethical judgements (life is sacred), facts (life begins at conception), and the linking of judgements with facts (abortion is forbidden).
- Science and religion cooperate: religion seeks order, science seeks power.

## 6. The modern covenant
- Modernity: people trade meaning for power; the greatest discovery was the discovery of ignorance.
- Antiquity: the belief that all important knowledge is contained in sacred scriptures.
- The vicious circle of stagnation in antiquity: no research → no funding → no lending → no belief in progress → economy in stagnation → no research.
- Curiosity: vampire bats lend each other blood.
- Evolutionary thinking: the pie is fixed – if one person gains, another loses.
- The theory of growth: we produce and consume more; a growing population requires growth to avoid regression; without growth it is impossible to help the poor.
- Three types of resources: energy, raw materials, and knowledge – the only resource that grows; the more you use, the more you have.
- Modern people are greedy.

## 7. The humanist revolution
- Humanism: the meaning of life lies in one's own inner experiences.
- Humanism manifests in many spheres: political (the voter knows best), economic (the customer is always right), aesthetic (beauty is in the eye of the beholder), ethical (if you cause no harm, it is good), and educational (think for yourself).
- Medieval knowledge: Knowledge = Sacred Scriptures × Logic.
- Modern knowledge: Knowledge = Empirical Data × Mathematics, or Knowledge = Experience × Sensitivity.
- Experience consists of sensations, emotions, and thoughts; experience and sensitivity drive each other.
- Humanism is a gradual process of inner change – from ignorance to enlightenment through experiences.
- Humanism is the distillation of the broadest experience into wisdom.

### III. Homo sapiens loses control

## 8. The time bomb in the laboratory

## 10. The ocean of consciousness
- Two directions of technology: techno-humanism (the enhancement of humans) and the religion of data.
- With the advance of civilisation we have lost our sense of smell, mindfulness, and the ability to dream during sleep.

## 11. The religion of data
- Dataism holds that the universe consists of data flows, and the value of any phenomenon or entity is determined by its contribution to data processing.
- Four methods of expanding the system: increasing the number of processors, increasing the diversity of processors, increasing the number of connections, increasing the flow of data.
- Three main theses: organisms are algorithms and life is data processing; intelligence is decoupling from consciousness; unconscious yet intelligent algorithms will know us better than we know ourselves.
