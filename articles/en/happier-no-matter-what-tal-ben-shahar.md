# Content

## Relationships:
- Fragile relationships do not return to their previous state, resilient ones do, and antifragile ones grow stronger.
- False belief: success brings happiness. True belief: happiness brings success.
- Success or failure raises or lowers mood only momentarily.
- Happiness is a full state: the well-being of the whole person.
- The happiness paradox: the more we desire it, the more elusive it becomes.
- Resolving the paradox: take care of achieving each individual element.
- Elements of happiness (SPIRE):
  - Spiritual well-being: a sense of meaning and purpose, living here and now.
  - Physical well-being: exercise, rest, sleep, nutrition, tender touch.
  - Intellectual well-being: curiosity and openness to challenges.
  - Relational well-being: relationships with others and with oneself.
  - Emotional well-being: coping with painful emotions and cultivating pleasant ones.
- Money matters up to a point; beyond that, what counts is not the amount but how it is spent (experiences over things) and sharing it.

## 1. Spiritual well-being
- Perspective matters enormously: one can be laying bricks, building a wall, or building a cathedral.
- Spirituality encompasses two dimensions:
  - Purpose and meaning: the meaning of life.
  - The present moment: meaning in life.

### Ad 1
- Work and life can be seen as a job (sense of duty), a career (desire for advancement), or a calling (passion).
- When we have meaning and purpose, the path seems less daunting – this is often the difference between fragility and antifragility.
- It is worth spending a few minutes each day reminding oneself of one's purpose; perspective can make a big difference.

### Ad 2
- Mindfulness is awareness of the present moment, as far as possible without judgment.
- Living in the past opens us to depression; living in the future opens us to anxiety; only living in the present are we truly open.
- Research shows that meditation changes the brain: it becomes happier and the left hemisphere develops.
- Left hemisphere: greater susceptibility to positive emotions, greater resilience to negative ones, greater ability to stay calm.
- Meditation (from the Tibetan "gom") means to contemplate something.
- The hippopotamus story: it lost its eye crossing a river and found it only when it stopped thrashing and the mud settled.
- Meditation guidelines: let the mind settle on one object; return to focus when distracted; breathe slowly, gently, and deeply; accept that there is no good or bad meditation.
- The key to meditation is repetition – it is mental hygiene; even 3–5 minutes yields results, 20–30 minutes works more deeply (like a shower vs. a bath).
- Awareness can be formal (meditation) or informal (everyday activity).
- At any moment one can make a choice that moves closer to or further from one's spirit.
- Steps: disconnect from technology; savor the moment.

## 2. Physical well-being
- The problem is not stress but lack of recovery.
- Types of recovery:
  - Micro: breaks and deep breaths – at least 3 times every 2 hours.
  - Medium: individual days off, catching up on sleep.
  - Macro: vacation – like in Formula 1, one must pull into the pit stop from time to time.
- The baseline level of well-being is shaped by genes and early experiences; later in life it can be shifted up or down.
- One should not sit for more than 30 minutes without some kind of movement.
- Blue zones (high concentration of centenarians): Ikaria – Greece, Okinawa – Japan, Loma Linda – USA, Sardinia – Italy, Nicoya – Costa Rica.
- The Okinawa rule: eat until you are 80% full.
- The five-minute start strategy: when you don't feel like doing something, try doing it for just 5 minutes.
- Action precedes motivation, not the other way around.

## 3. Intellectual well-being

### I. Cultivating curiosity
- One must discover how and what to learn.
- Self-perception theory: we see ourselves the way we behave.
- By trying things, we develop an appetite for them – from cucumbers to learning.
- Questions should be positive, as this increases outcomes and the durability of change (e.g., "What is going well in my life?").

### II. Deep learning
- Quality matters more than quantity.

### III. Learn from mistakes, because you will make them
- For optimism, the word "yet" is as important as the word "yes".
- When praising a child, focus not on results but on hard work and process.
- Psychological safety: provide space for making mistakes.

## 4. Relational well-being
- Social media: 20 minutes is entertainment, 3 hours is loneliness.
- Deep relationships are possible over the internet.
- Social media has reduced empathy by 40%.
- Giving raises the level of happiness (in the short and long term) and self-confidence.
- What distinguishes depression from sadness is the absence of hope; giving allows one to feel that hope.
- "To give" in Hebrew is a palindrome (natan) – in giving, we simultaneously receive.
- Among givers, takers, and matchers, research shows that givers are both the most and the least effective; the difference is that the most effective ones also give to themselves.
- Parents should be "good enough" rather than available all the time.
- A relationship is living with a "beautiful enemy".
- Important issues in a marriage after about 3 years: children, money, sex, extended family.
- Ways to deal with conflict: find a way to say "yes"; the ratio of positive words and gestures to negative ones should be 5:1.

## 5. Emotional well-being
- Paradox: by rejecting painful emotions, we amplify them (like being told not to think of a pink elephant).
- Feeling emotions, even negative ones, means you are alive and are not a psychopath.
- When we reject negative emotions, we also block positive ones – they flow through the same channel: by blocking jealousy, we block love; by blocking anxiety, we block excitement; by blocking sadness, we block joy.
- Ways to cope with negative emotions: cry, talk about them, write about them.
- Active acceptance.

## Accept your emotions, then choose the best course of action.
- Action trumps emotions – what we do matters more than what we feel.
- You are not your emotions.

## When observing emotions, you do not say "I am the emotion" but "I feel the emotion".
- Instead of "I am" say "I feel": instead of "I am sad" say "I feel sadness".
- Emotions = thoughts + feelings.
- Gratitude.

## Mirror neurons: they spread emotions, so it is worth being grateful.
- Write a letter of thanks.

## Nurturing hope

## When we appreciate something, it increases in value.
- It is good to look forward to something – this is hope, and it therefore reduces depression.

## Summary
- The exponential nature of human relationships: one person can change the world.
