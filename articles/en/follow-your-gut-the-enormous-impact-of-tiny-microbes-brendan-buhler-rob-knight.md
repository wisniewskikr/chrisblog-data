# Content

## Introduction
- The human body contains about 10 trillion human cells and 10 times as many microorganisms.
- Differences between people: our DNA differs by only 0.01%, whereas our microorganisms differ by as much as 90%.

## 1. A body full of microorganisms
- Types of microorganisms: archaea (no nucleus, usually anaerobic), eukaryotes (fungi and yeasts), and bacteria.
- Microbes amplify body odour.

## 2. How our gut microbiota is shaped
- A baby's first microorganisms appear during childbirth.
- Further microbes are acquired through breastfeeding.
- What we eat has a major influence on the microbiome.

## 3. In sickness and in health
- Numerous studies indicate a link between obesity and bacteria – when a lean mouse was given microbes from an obese mouse, the lean mouse also became obese.
- Kwashiorkor (swollen belly associated with malnutrition) is also linked to the microbiome.
- Asthma and allergies are less common in children exposed to a variety of microbes; the theory suggests the immune system gets "bored" and seeks an alternative target.
- Recommendations for reducing a child's risk of asthma: getting a dog (ideally before the child is born), living in the countryside, avoiding antibiotics, taking probiotics, and breastfeeding.

## 4. The gut-brain axis
- The word "malaria" comes from medieval Italian: "mala aria", meaning "bad air".
- Leeuwenhoek discovered the microscope and microbes – he was a draper who examined the quality of stitching through a magnifying glass.

## 5. Hacking the microbiome
- Prebiotics supply nutrients to microbes.
- Probiotics deliver the microbes themselves.
- Clean water and vaccines are the two greatest inventions in medicine.

## 6. Antibiotics
- Antibiotics are a poison that acts on microbes more strongly than on humans.
- Microbes develop resistance to antibiotics, which is why a course of antibiotics must be completed in full.

## 7. The future
- The future lies in a better understanding of the microbiome.

- The American Gut project allows anyone to have their microorganisms analysed for $100.
