# Content

## I. What is procrastination?

- Procrastination becomes a problem when putting things off is chronic.
- Laziness occurs when a person consciously does not want to invest energy in a given activity.
- Procrastination occurs when a person wants to perform a task but at the same time feels resistance to starting it.
- Types of procrastination:
  - Avoidant: escaping unpleasant emotions.
  - Decisional: delaying the making of decisions.
  - Arousal-seeking: some people need a strong impulse to act, e.g. time pressure.
- The percentage of people who procrastinate decreases with age.
- Causes of procrastination:
  - Biological.
  - Psychological: difficulty with emotional self-regulation.
  - Environmental: faulty habits and beliefs.
- Self-regulation can proceed differently for different people: one person may deny their emotions, while another may become stuck in them too deeply.
- Zinker's cycle: sensation, awareness, energy mobilisation, action, contact, satisfaction, withdrawal.
- Procrastinators get stuck at the stage of energy mobilisation and action.
- This is the mechanism of deflection: diverting, bouncing off, veering away.

### II. Tracking down the causes

- Procrastination is a symptom, not a problem in itself.
- Psychological causes:
  - Automatic thoughts.
  - Motivation that is too low or too high.
  - Flash-in-the-pan enthusiasm.
  - Fears.
  - Poor time management.
- Types of time perspective: negative past, positive past, hedonistic present, fatalistic present, future.
- Temperament consists of: sensitivity, briskness, endurance, activity level, perseverance.
- Types of temperament: choleric, phlegmatic, sanguine, melancholic.
- We are born with our temperament, but we shape our personality over many years.
- ADHD can result in procrastination; its symptoms include hyperactivity, impulsivity, and attention difficulties.
- 9–10% of children in Polish schools have dyslexia; dyslexia can result in procrastination.
- Procrastination can also be caused by: depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, personality disorders, traumatic experiences, perfectionism, and low self-esteem.

## III. What does procrastination take from you?

- Fear, risk, and uncertainty cannot be completely eliminated, but one can try to keep them in check.

## IV. How to deal with procrastination?

- When necessary, challenge automatic thoughts by asking: is this claim a fact or an opinion? To what degree is it true? What are the arguments against it?
- Avoid the words "always" and "never" — they are extreme and lead to rigid thinking about oneself.
- The SMART method: a goal should be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
- Confronting fear helps in overcoming it.
- The Eisenhower Matrix: urgent and important, not urgent and important, urgent and unimportant, not urgent and unimportant.
- The two-minute rule: do everything that takes up to 2 minutes immediately.
- The Pomodoro method: choose a task, set a timer, work on the task, take a five-minute break, then continue or move to a new task.
- Eat the frog: start with the most difficult task.
- Create daily and weekly to-do lists and delegate tasks.
- Self-sacrifice at a low level of intensity makes sense and brings benefits.
- Principles for goals and productivity: use positive language; keep the goal concise, clear, concrete, and measurable; avoid splitting focus; concentrate; limit distractions; batch tasks; use reminders; work in solitude; surround yourself with organised people; fight routine; have someone observe you; set a time limit for completing a task.
- ADHD and dyslexia, alongside their drawbacks, also have many advantages.

## 5. Start acting

- Growth follows a sinusoidal curve.
- One must accept one's stumbles.
- Procrastination is a secondary problem.
