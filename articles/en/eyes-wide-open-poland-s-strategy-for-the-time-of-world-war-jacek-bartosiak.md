# Content

## Minerva's owl takes flight at dawn
- Strategic decisions must be made.

## I. World war

### Outbreak
- Every 100 years the great powers establish new rules.
- The Netherlands: after the Thirty Years' War.
- Great Britain: after the Seven Years' War.
- The United States: after the World Wars.

## Systemic wars
- Systemic wars change the rules on which the system operates.

## Ideology and capitalism
- Ideology followed in the wake of victory.

## Productivity and greed
- Greed has led us to the current situation.

## There will be no Casablanca
- What matters most for the balance of power after a war is who is strongest when it ends.

## Centre of gravity
- Had it not been for the Nord Stream pipeline, the war in Ukraine might never have broken out – the Russians felt they controlled Germany.

## When the war began
- The Americans struck at semiconductors.

## Trump enters the game
- The Americans began breaking free trade because it started to harm them.

## Capitalism versus capitalism
- There is a struggle between capital and politicians; in the USA, capital prevails.

## How did it go for Poland?
- Within the EU, industry was relocated to newly admitted countries.
- GDP diverts attention from the distribution of capital.

## Erosion
- The USA charged a tribute for stability.
- The USA confiscated Russia's money.
- The electronic yuan is becoming popular.

## The scale of the challenge
- Chinese industry amounts to 150% of American industry.

## Development or regression – the Polish question
- The international system is weak because there is no single arbiter or dominant force.
- Poles regard morality as their currency.

## The course of the future war
- The war will begin as a financial war.
- The Americans will not want to pay interest.

## II. Poland at war
- First principle: keeping Russia outside the European system.
- Second principle: eliminating Poland's social and economic lag behind Europe.

## Squaring the circle
- Poland faces a task that is difficult to solve.

## What do we need a state for?
- Without a state there is no security.
- The state is especially important when clear and equal rules are absent.

## Postulates and variants of Polish strategy
- Two options: optimal and sober.
- Three variants regarding the United States: 1. Status-quo variant – the Americans change nothing. 2. Offshore balancing variant – the Americans limit their activity in Europe and engage only where their interests require it. 3. Target variant – the Americans withdraw from their leadership position.

## In step
- Optimal option, status-quo variant.
- Poland should shelter behind the Americans and let them go first, so as not to be taken advantage of.

## From beyond the horizon
- The Americans may keep Europe guessing so that it does not cross over to the hostile camp.

## The risk of the great game
- Kaliningrad is Russia's weak point and Poland should exploit this.

## The Ukrainian gambit
- Reconciling the interests of a sovereign Ukraine and an imperial Russia is not possible.
- Ukraine will propose a deal to Poland.

## The European gambit
- We should pretend to side with the United States while actually siding with the European Union.

## Silos in the Świętokrzyskie Mountains
- The Americans may turn a blind eye to the question of nuclear weapons in Poland.
- It is worth having Polish mercenaries.
- A strategy regarding China must be developed.

## Brave new world?
- The new world will be much harder for Poland, and we must prepare for it.

## Being clear-headed here and now
- Do not get involved in Ukraine.
- We must keep both the European option and the American option open.
- Königsberg should become Poland's hostage.

## Growing dread
- Panic will set in when decision-makers realise they have been left alone without an ally.

## Surviving in the new Middle Ages
- Do not let yourself be drawn into war.
- Stick with Europe, because it will always endure.
- Stick with China, because China does not want war.
