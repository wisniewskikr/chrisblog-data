# Content

## Introduction
- Cotton consumes enormous amounts of water – 7,500 litres per kilogram.
- The book covers four areas: the internet and technology, food, fashion, and fuel.

## I. Technology and the internet
- Information and communications technology uses 30% of electricity – more than the aviation industry and almost as much as the shipping industry.

## 1. The physical internet
- In the United States, railway lines were laid first, then telegraph lines, and finally telephone and internet cables.
- 70% of internet traffic passes through Virginia because that is where industry specialists previously worked.

## 2. Pulling the cloud down to earth
- Many data centres are located in Iceland because cooling is unnecessary and energy is cheap.
- Rebound effect: efficiency savings are offset by increased consumption.
- Solutions: improving data centre efficiency and locating them in cold countries.
- Bitcoin relies on proof of work; Ethereum on proof of stake, which requires less energy.

## 3. Taking it offline: e-commerce
- UPS holds 57% of the market in the USA and 22% worldwide.
- Cardboard production is very energy-intensive.
- The largest industrial energy consumers in the USA are: 1. chemicals, 2. oil refining, 3. paper.

## 4. Silicon Valley: toxic waste dump?
- Silicon Valley was an important industrial centre, producing semiconductors and processors.
- Santa Clara County is one of the most polluted counties in the country.
- Factories were relocated to South Korea and other countries.

## 5. Mining for technology
- Congo is the main producer of cobalt.
- Lithium is mined in Chile, but indigenous communities receive no income from it.

## 6. Vampire energy
- The average household has 65 electrical devices.
- Three-quarters of them cannot be fully switched off.
- Standby power accounts for approximately one-quarter of a neighbourhood's energy use.
- An increasing number of devices are connected to the internet and constantly draw power.

## 7. The technology we throw away
- We generate 50 million tonnes of e-waste annually.
- Only 20% is recycled.
- Equipment is exported as donations, but it often does not work – a form of fraud.

## II. Food
- 40% of land is used for agriculture.
- 45% of harvests go to animal feed or biofuels.
- In the USA, one-third of food is wasted.

## 1. The most demanding crop
- Maize is produced in the greatest quantities and tolerates hot, dry conditions well.
- 40% of maize production goes to animal feed and 30% to ethanol.
- Monoculture is harmful: only 12 species meet 75% of our food needs.
- Monoculture is more vulnerable to disease and pests, and degrades soil quality and water quality.
- Fertilisers cause algal blooms in water, leading to fish die-offs.
- Drivers of monoculture dominance: government subsidies, biofuels, and meat production.

## 2. Waste
- In the USA, approximately 180 kg of food per person is wasted each year, representing 30–40% of total production.
- Expiry dates are not regulated by law.
- Americans throw away food based on these labels.

## 3. Organic food: how good is it?
- Organic farming uses natural fertilisers and herbicides.
- In some regions, running an organic farm is more difficult.
- Organic agriculture requires more land.

## 4. How far does our food travel?
- The USA imports 50% of its fruit and 20% of its vegetables.
- Food production emits more CO₂ than food transportation.

## 5. A sea of problems
- One-fifth of consumed animal protein comes from fish.
- 98 fish species are threatened in the USA.
- Changes in water temperature pose a serious threat to fish.
- Coral reefs feed 25% of all fish, yet they are dying.
- Ocean acidification prevents shellfish from building their shells.
- Deoxygenation of waters is another major problem.
- A proposal exists to designate 30% of the oceans as reserves.
- Companies can apply for sustainable fisheries certification.
- 15 species have gone extinct in the oceans and 500 on land.

## III. Fashion
- The fashion industry discloses no information about its environmental impact.
- Fashion is the second most polluting industry in the world and accounts for 10% of CO₂ emissions.

## 1. Thirsty for jeans
- Jeans were created by Davis as the tailor and Strauss as the fabric supplier.
- They are made from cotton, which accounts for one-third of all fibre production.
- Cotton cultivation consumes 16% of all agricultural chemicals and 70% of water used in agriculture.
- Producing 1 kg of cotton requires between 7 and 29 tonnes of water.
- The shrinking Aral Sea is a stark example of the consequences of excessive water use.

## 2. Athleisure forever!
- Polyester is made from petroleum and requires large amounts of energy to produce.
- It takes several hundred years to decompose.
- Microplastic fibres are released during washing and end up on land, in seas and in oceans.
- Wastewater treatment plants filter out one microplastic particle per 4 litres of water.

## 3. Fast fashion, but going nowhere
- Fast fashion is based on cheap imitations with no seasonal collections.
- 60% of garments are thrown away within a year of purchase.

## 4. It's not wood, it's viscose
- Viscose (artificial silk) ranks third after polyester and cotton.
- It is produced from biomass and chemicals.
- Alternatives include production from bamboo, Belgian flax, or recycled materials.

## 5. The yarn that creates a desert
- Cashmere comes from goats grazing in the Gobi Desert region between Mongolia and China.
- China and Mongolia supply 90% of the world's cashmere.
- In Mongolia, goats make up 60% of the livestock population.
- Overgrazing by goats turns land into desert.
- Companies should be required to ensure sustainable production.

## IV. Fuel
- Burning coal emits CO₂ and produces ash.
- Wood is counted as a renewable energy source.

## 1. Another problem with coal
- Coal ash is one of the largest industrial waste streams.
- It contains toxic substances: mercury, lead, arsenic, and others.
- 40% of ash is processed into concrete and plasterboard.
- Ash contaminates rivers.
- Ash ponds are located near low-income communities, limiting public awareness of the problem.
- Coal is the second largest energy source in the USA after natural gas, supplying 30% of demand.

## 2. Wood for trees
- Biomass is treated as climate-neutral.
- In Europe, half of renewable energy comes from wood.
- Only two-thirds of that wood comes from within Europe – the rest is imported.
- Producing wood pellets is energy-intensive and environmentally harmful.

## 3. Cooler inside, warmer outside
- In 2016, harmful HFC gases were phased out of air conditioning; they had previously replaced CFCs, which destroyed the ozone layer.
- More than half of the world's air conditioners are in the USA and China; they consume 2.5 times more electricity than the whole of Africa in a year.
- Air conditioning accounts for 6% of energy consumption in the USA.
- Glass-dominated construction does not support natural cooling.
- Nights are becoming warmer than days, so the body has no opportunity to cool down.
- Energy efficiency standards are the proposed solution.

## 4. The blue route of heavy cargo
- The USA removed tariffs on goods from Central American countries to draw them away from drug trade.
- Only 1% of freight is transported by air.
- Aircraft account for 2.5% of global CO₂ emissions – as much as Germany.
- By around 2050, one-third of aircraft may be unable to take off due to heat and wind conditions.
- The Paris Agreement does not cover aviation emissions.
- 25% of aviation emissions come from take-off and landing.
- Emissions can be offset through organisations such as Sustainable Travel International.

## 5. Shipping: the world in a box
- The shipping container was invented by Malcolm McLean in 1956.
- Low shipping costs enabled global production.
- The shipping industry accounts for 3% of global CO₂ emissions.
- Ships consume between 200 and 400 tonnes of fuel per day.
- Ships emit black carbon, which contributes to the melting of polar ice caps.
- Opening an Arctic shipping route will further accelerate global warming.

## 6. Cars, trucks, and justice
- In 2016, emissions from transport exceeded emissions from electricity generation.
- Transport emissions come 60% from passenger cars and 23% from trucks.
- Trucks consume approximately 3 litres of fuel per kilometre.
- Glider kits are new truck bodies fitted with old, more polluting engines.
- Many trucks travel without full loads.
- Car engines waste between 10% and 80% of fuel.

## 7. A ride for one Uber
- In 2018, Uber trips outnumbered bus trips.
- Uber increases the total kilometres driven because the driver must travel to pick up the passenger.
- Uber vehicles are empty for most of the time.
- The solution is to combine electrification, automation, and vehicle sharing.
- A ride tax directed towards public transport has been proposed.

## Conclusion
- In Maine, shrimp, cod, and herring have been severely overfished.
- Lack of transparency in environmentally impactful industries is a major problem.
- The costs of ecological disasters may prove higher than anticipated.
- Green technologies can be profitable.
- Those who cause pollution rarely bear its costs.
- Consumers should not have to investigate the environmental impact of products themselves – that information should be publicly available.
