# Content

## Introduction

- Anxiety wants to help you.

## 1. Society's perception of anxiety is wrong… and it makes you suffer

- Anxiety can rob you of the things you value.
- We used to worry about the concerns of a small group – now we worry about the concerns of the entire world.
- FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out): the fear of being left out.
- Personalised ads instil the belief that you won't be able to live without some product.
- Films: there used to be a soothing silence; now they constantly stimulate.
- Anxiety is something normal.
- Fighting anxiety makes it more intense.
- Anxiety is a flawed superhero – it tries to protect you from problems.
- Anxiety activates your superpowers: ease of focus, extra energy, greater strength, protection (fight, flight, freeze) and calming.
- We live in safe times, yet anxiety misjudges the context of situations.

## 2. Try to get to know your anxiety monster

- Unlike a gazelle, a human analyses a dangerous situation for a long time.
- Anxiety is built into our genes.
- Anxiety is also learned through our experiences.
- Anxiety thinks it is better to overreact than to regret it later.
- Sometimes anxiety is contradictory: it wants one thing and another at the same time.
- You are not to blame for your fear – you are a member of an anxious species.

## 3. Love the anxiety monster

- Compassion: sensitivity to one's own and others' suffering, and a commitment to alleviating that suffering.
- Self-compassion is a technique for communicating with anxiety.
- Anxiety gives you excitement and motivates you.

## 4. The monster's misbehaviour

- Anxiety turns into a phobia when it prevents normal functioning.
- If you react as though something is genuinely threatening you, anxiety intensifies.
- Avoiding situations means anxiety is unable to correct its strategy.

## 5. How to talk so the monster listens

- The monster in your head is a hyperactive four-year-old – you should not follow its lead in everything.
- Defusion: noticing a thought without getting absorbed in it.

## 6. What to do (and what not to do) when the monster howls

- It is important that anxiety does not cause unnecessary distress.
- Clean anxiety: anxiety that is justified; dirty anxiety: anxiety that is not.
- Reducing anxiety: sleep, exercise, diet.
- Increasing anxiety: medications, stimulants.
- Three regulation systems: threat, drive and soothing.

## 7. Training the anxiety monster

- You need to be psychologically flexible and filter anxiety through the frontal cortex.
- The monster can be taught – exposure therapy.
- The inhibitory model: strengthening the safety pathway and weakening the threat pathway; safety behaviours should be abandoned.

## 8. Travelling through life with an anxious inner companion

- Values are the direction, and goals are the stops along the way.
