# Content

## 1. Riding a beam of light

- In 1905 Einstein wrote 5 revolutionary papers.

## 2. Childhood

- He was late to learn to speak.
- He disliked authority and questioned widely held views.
- He came from assimilated Jewish traders and peddlers from Swabia.
- His father, Hermann Einstein, had a talent for mathematics, was gentle-natured, and ran a feather-bedding business.
- His mother, Pauline Koch, was a determined woman.
- Albert was born on 14 March 1879 in Ulm.
- He had a sister, Maria, known as Maja.
- The family moved to Munich, where his father worked at a gas company.
- When he was ill, he received a compass and was fascinated by how it worked.
- He learned to play the violin.
- He attended the Luitpold Gymnasium in Munich and was a good student.
- He read popular science books.
- Max Talmud, a young medical student, was a frequent guest of the Einsteins.
- His parents went bankrupt and moved to Italy.
- Albert dropped out of school and renounced his German citizenship.
- He failed to gain entry to the University of Zurich; he prepared for the next entrance exam in Aarau, where his first girlfriend was Marie Winteler.
- He eventually gained admission to the Polytechnic in Zurich.

## 3. The Polytechnic in Zurich

- Albert met Mileva Marić – the only female student in his cohort.
- In 1900 he graduated.

## 4. Lovers

- Albert gave private mathematics lessons in Zurich.
- He wrote a paper on the capillary effect: water creeps upward in a narrow tube.
- He received Swiss citizenship.
- He taught for two months at a technical school in Winterthur.
- Mileva became pregnant and failed her polytechnic exam.
- A daughter, Lieserl, was born; she lived only 2 years, and Albert apparently never saw her.
- Albert started working at the patent office in Bern.
- Together with Solovine and Habicht he founded the Olympia Academy.
- Kant's types of judgements: analytic (e.g. two plus two equals four) – cannot be revised; synthetic (e.g. all swans are white) – can be revised.
- Albert's father died.
- Albert married Mileva.
- A son, Hans Albert Einstein, was born.

## 5. The miracle year: quanta and molecules

- Four papers from 1905:
  1. On light: transmitted in quanta called photons; inspired by Planck's constant.
  2. On determining the size of molecules – on the basis of this paper he received his doctorate.
  3. On Brownian motion: movement of particles in a liquid caused by bombardment by molecules.

## 6. The special theory of relativity

- The special theory addresses a special case – when two objects move in uniform motion.
- The general theory, created 10 years later, additionally incorporates acceleration.
- Maxwell discovered that light, electricity, and magnetism are the same thing; light is the visible manifestation of electromagnetic waves.
- Albert discovered that there is no absolute time.
- Albert found that mass and energy are two different manifestations of the same thing: E = mc².

## 7. The happiest thought

- Planck noticed and endorsed Einstein's theory.
- In 1906 Albert published 6 papers, and in 1907 he published 10.
- The happiest thought: if a person falls freely, they do not feel their own weight – acceleration and gravity are the same thing.
- He wrote a habilitation thesis and became a Privatdozent.
- He received a professorship in Zurich.
- Mileva was jealous of Albert.
- At the 1909 conference in Salzburg it was established that light can be both a wave and a particle.

## 8. The wandering professor

- Mileva became pregnant again; a son, Eduard, known as Tete, was born.
- Albert lectured in an informal style.
- He obtained a position in Prague, where he met Kafka.
- Solvay, a Belgian industrialist, gathered the world's leading scientists in Brussels.
- In Berlin, Albert renewed his acquaintance with his cousin Elsa.
- He subsequently obtained positions in Zurich and then in Berlin, close to Elsa.
- He was separated from Mileva.

## 9. The general theory of relativity 1911–1915

- Einstein concluded that gravity bends light and asked astronomers to verify this.
- Euclidean geometry works well on flat surfaces; Riemannian non-Euclidean geometry applies to curved surfaces.
- The precession of Mercury's perihelion: 43 arc seconds per century.
- In 1914 Freundlich travelled to Crimea to verify Einstein's theory, but when Russia declared war on Germany he was interned.
- Haber, a chemist and friend of Einstein, and Planck supported the war; Einstein was a pacifist.
- In 1915 in Göttingen, Einstein presented his theory to Hilbert and others, then raced against Hilbert to complete the field equations – Einstein ultimately won.

## 10. Divorce

- The younger son was in a sanatorium; Mileva was ill.
- Einstein committed to giving Mileva the money from the Nobel Prize.
- Einstein married Elsa.

## 11. Einstein's universe

- In 1919 Eddington confirmed Einstein's theory – at Sobral and Príncipe Island.

## 12. Fame

- The press seized on the idea that a German's theory had been confirmed by a Briton.
- Einstein's book became a bestseller.
- Einstein was kind but kept his distance from everything and was unable to feel empathy.
- In 1922 Einstein received the Nobel Prize.

## 13. The wandering Zionist

- After World War I Einstein became passionate about the Zionist cause.
- He travelled to the USA at the invitation of Weizmann, the head of the Zionist organisation.
- The assassination of Rathenau – an assimilated Jew and Foreign Minister – altered the course of the Jewish question in Germany.
- Einstein travelled to Asia and to Palestine.

## 14. Nobel laureate

- In 1922 Einstein and Bohr received the Nobel Prize; Einstein's was awarded for 1921.
- Bohr discovered that electrons can change orbit by absorbing or emitting photons.
- Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: it is impossible to determine simultaneously the position and momentum of a particle.

## 15. Unified field theories

- Unified field theory: the unification of electricity, magnetism, gravity, and quantum mechanics.
- Participants at the Solvay Conferences in Brussels included Lorentz, Planck, Einstein, and Bohr.
- Hubble, at Mount Wilson, discovered the Andromeda Galaxy, dozens of other galaxies, and that the universe is expanding.

## 16. After fifty

- Einstein went into hiding on his 50th birthday.
- He had an affair with his secretary, Betty Neumann.
- Helen Dukas became his secretary.
- Hans Albert married Frieda Knecht; they adopted a daughter.
- Einstein spent 2 months in America working at Caltech.
- He was a committed pacifist.
- The role of the state: to protect the individual and enable their development.
- Eliminating war requires surrendering part of sovereignty to a supranational organisation.

## 17. Einstein's God

- Einstein: "I believe in Spinoza's God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of all that exists, but not in a God who concerns himself with the fate and the doings of mankind."

## 18. The refugee

- In 1931 Einstein visited the USA again as a visiting professor.
- He changed his view on pacifism: he came to support a federation with its own army.
- In 1933 he sailed to America, never to return.

## 19. America

- He worked at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.
- Ilse died.
- Hans Albert and Margot came to Princeton.
- In 1936 Elsa died.

## 20. Quantum entanglement

- When two particles are entangled, observing one tells us about the other.
- Einstein inspired Schrödinger's Cat thought experiment.

## 21. The bomb

- Szilárd, a Hungarian scientist, grasped the power of atomic fission; he knew that uranium was in the Belgian Congo and that Einstein was friends with the Belgian queen.
- Einstein and Szilárd wrote a letter to the President of the USA.
- In 1940 Einstein received US citizenship.
- The USA launched the Manhattan Project; Einstein was excluded because he was not trusted.
- Einstein was regarded as the father of the atomic bomb, though he said that had he known Germany would fail to build one, he would not have lifted a finger.

## 22. Unifier of the world

- Einstein wanted to create a supranational organisation.
- In 1950 a witch hunt began in the USA: the Soviets had the atomic bomb, Fuchs had spied at Los Alamos, and McCarthy published a list of communists in the State Department.
- Einstein became the lover of Margerita, a Russian spy.

## 23. The turning point

- German theories of the 20th century: Einstein – relativity; Heisenberg – uncertainty; Gödel – incompleteness (every useful mathematical system contains a statement that can be neither proved nor disproved).
- Mileva died in Zurich; her son Eduard was in a psychiatric hospital there.
- In 1948 the State of Israel was established; Einstein was offered the presidency.

## 24. Red scare

- Einstein protested against the death sentence for the Rosenbergs.
- He supported Frauenglass when the latter refused to provide information about his students.

## 25. The end

- Einstein turned 75.
- He created the Einstein–Russell Manifesto against nuclear armament.

## Epilogue

- Einstein was cremated and his ashes scattered in the Delaware River.
- His brain was nonetheless removed by a pathologist and transported around the USA for 40 years.
