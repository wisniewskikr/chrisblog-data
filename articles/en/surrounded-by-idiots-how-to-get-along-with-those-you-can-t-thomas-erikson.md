# Content

## 1. Whether communication is understood is decided by the recipient of the message

- It is the recipient who decides how a message is understood – this must be kept in mind.
- You need to understand the other person's communication style and adapt to it.
- Percentage breakdown of personality types: 5% single-colour, 8% two-colour, 87% three-colour.
- Most common two-colour combinations: blue/red, red/yellow, yellow/green, green/blue.

## 2. Why we became who we are

- Children learn through independent discovery and imitation.
- Values are instilled in childhood by parents and school.
- Attitude is shaped independently through experience.
- Basic behaviour is how we would act if we were alone.
- Learned behaviour is our actual behaviour, shaped by the influence of our environment.
- Behaviour = personality + influence of environment.

## 3. Introduction to the system you will be learning

- Four types of behaviour:
- Blue: an introvert focused on the task – orderly with paperwork but sees threats everywhere; asks "why?".
- Green: an introvert focused on people – not stressed, but also not very engaged; asks "how?".
- Red: an extrovert focused on the task – makes quick decisions but dresses others down; asks "what?".
- Yellow: an extrovert focused on people – easily makes connections but can be too talkative; asks "who?".

## 4. Red behaviour

- The "all or nothing" type: if they consider a task important, they will throw themselves into it; if they think it is pointless, they will give up.
- Reds enjoy competition.

## 5. Yellow behaviour

- Yellows live to enjoy life.
- Born optimists.
- They make quick decisions, but intuitively, without justification.

## 6. Green behaviour

- There are more Greens than any other type.
- They are very balanced and good listeners.
- They enjoy helping others.
- Considered the most important group.

## 7. Blue behaviour

- Organised and pessimistic.
- They check everything several times.
- They believe that silence is golden.

## 10. Body language – or why appearance matters

- Red behaviour: maintains distance, gives firm handshakes, often leans forward, looks directly in the eye, makes commanding gestures.
- Yellow behaviour: touches others, is relaxed and smiling, maintains friendly eye contact, gestures expressively, enjoys closeness.
- Green behaviour: relaxed and not afraid of closeness, works methodically, tends to lean back, maintains friendly eye contact, excels at small gestures.
- Blue behaviour: prefers to keep others at a distance, has limited body language, looks directly in the eye, does not gesticulate.

## 11. A real-life example

- The effect of alcohol on each type:
- Yellows become sad.
- Blues become cheerful.
- Reds insist they are not so bad.
- Greens gain courage.

## 12. Adaptation

- Adapting to Reds: be quick, eliminate small talk, stay on topic, present a proposal and ask if they're on board, don't hesitate to say what you think, show how hard you work, focus on details (Reds cannot), show when acting too quickly has caused losses, they focus on the present, they must understand that cooperation requires adapting to others, address inappropriate behaviour immediately.
- Adapting to Yellows: Yellows perform best when in a good mood, skip the details, Yellows follow their gut feelings, they like novelties, be approachable, have a framework and ask Yellow to confirm, establish details precisely, give them a rough action plan, they must understand that other people exist too, gently press them to start taking action, be persistent and tenacious when conveying information.
- Adapting to Greens: they are mainly driven by fear, taking action stresses them, support Green in planning, give feedback in private, be patient when discussing changes, take charge.
- Adapting to Blues: show that you are well prepared, stick to business matters, put aside vision and stick to facts, you cannot be sloppy, explain that others have feelings, communicate expectations for the future clearly, say that when data is lacking one should act as best as possible, pay attention to the end of stages – it is hard for them to make a decision.

## 13. How to deliver bad news, or constructive is still criticism

- Giving feedback to a Red: don't beat around the bush, be specific, there will always be a fight – they are always right, go point by point, ask them to repeat what was said.
- Giving feedback to a Yellow: Yellows are the hardest to change, they like change but only their own, you must accommodate their ego, let them think they came up with the changes themselves, they quickly forget unpleasant situations so you need to remind them.
- Giving feedback to a Green: use concrete examples and gentle words, show that you are criticising the behaviour, not the person, ask them to repeat what was agreed.
- Giving feedback to a Blue: present specific, detailed arguments – preferably in writing, do not reduce the distance, be prepared for questions, ask them to repeat what you said.

## 14. Which colours are compatible and why

- Natural combinations: blue and green; red and yellow.
- Complementary combinations: blue and red (both task-oriented); green and yellow.
- Risky combinations: blue and yellow; green and red.

## 16. What annoys us so damn much

- Metaphors for temperaments:
- Red: a shot glass of vodka.
- Yellow: a glass of milk – you can watch the anger slowly rise.
- Green: a barrel of beer – it takes a long time to fill, but when it explodes, it does so properly.
- Blue: a barrel of beer with a tap that leaks.

## 17. Stress triggers and energy thieves. What stress really is

- Stress occurs when the environment sets the bar too high: too many tasks, pressure, demands, excessive expectations.
- Everyone reacts to stress differently – it depends on character and mood.
- Stress amplifies characteristic traits:
- Red: becomes even sharper and more aggressive.
- Yellow: sulks more and becomes more disorganised.
- Green: withdraws and lapses into passivity.
- Blue: shuts down completely and splits hairs.

## 18. A brief historical overview

- Hippocrates identified four types: choleric (chole – bile) – violent, governed by the liver; sanguine (sanguis – blood) – optimistic, governed by the heart; phlegmatic (phlegma – phlegm) – rigid and slow; melancholic (melaina chole – black bile) – sad and gloomy.
- The Aztecs used elements: fire – violent and explosive; air – open like the wind; earth – stability and security; water – calm and reliable.
- Marston distinguished: dominant personality – activity in an unfavourable environment; inspiring personality – activity in a favourable environment; stable personality – passivity in a stable environment; conscientious personality – passivity in an unfavourable environment.
