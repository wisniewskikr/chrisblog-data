# Content

## Introduction

- Daily food production amounts to approximately 3,000 kcal per person.
- Around 1,000 kcal per person are wasted every day.

## 1. What do we owe to agriculture?

- Around 6 million years ago we separated from other primates.
- Around 2 million years ago the first Homo appeared.
- Around 300,000 years ago Homo sapiens emerged.
- Agriculture developed around 7,000 years ago.
- Gathering and hunting worked well for small populations; groups of gatherers numbered between 25 and 500 people.
- At one point the number of Homo sapiens dropped to as few as 20,000 individuals, with a minimum population of 100,000.
- The domestication of plants and animals followed gradually.
- In 1810 the world population was 1 billion; by 2020 it reached 8 billion, meaning 1 hectare must feed 5 people.
- Recommended macronutrient intake: carbohydrates (45–65%), fats (20–35%), proteins (10–35%).
- Cereals and legumes have become the dominant source of energy.
- There is no realistic alternative to the current model of food supply.

## 2. Why do we eat the plants we eat and not others?

- Just 20 plant species account for 75% of global harvests.
- Such a small number reflects the need for plants with high energy density that also contain all three macronutrients.
- Domesticated plants are those adapted to human needs – they are sweeter, larger, and so on.
- Sugarcane and ethanol are used to produce ethanol as automotive fuel.
- Wheat has 7 times the energy density of fruit and 18 times that of vegetables.
- Wheat does not, however, provide sufficient fat and protein – this role is filled by legumes.
- Storage losses for wheat are around 1%, while for rice in China they reach around 15%.
- After accounting for losses during harvesting, storage, and sowing, roughly 50% of the crop remains available for consumption.
- Cassava can be harvested year-round, whereas potatoes can only be harvested during a specific period.
