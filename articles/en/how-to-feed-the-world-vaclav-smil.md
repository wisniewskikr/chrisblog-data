# Content
## Introduction
- We currently supply an average of 3,000 kcal per person per day.
- At the same time, roughly 1,000 kcal per person per day is wasted.

## 1. What do we owe to agriculture?
- About 6 million years ago we diverged from other primates.
- About 2 million years ago the first members of the genus Homo appeared.
- Homo sapiens emerged around 300,000 years ago.
- Agriculture arose approximately 7,000 years ago.
- Hunter-gatherer lifestyles worked well only for small populations — groups ranged from 25 to 500 people.
- At one point the number of Homo sapiens dropped to as few as 20,000 individuals; 100,000 is considered the minimum viable population.
- The domestication of plants and animals followed gradually.
- In 1810 the human population reached 1 billion; by 2020 it had grown to 8 billion, meaning 1 hectare of land must feed an average of 5 people.
- Recommended macronutrient ratios: carbohydrates (45–65%), fats (20–35%), proteins (10–35%).
- Grains and legumes have become the dominant source of energy in the human diet.
- There is no realistic alternative to the current food system.

## 2. Why do we eat the plants we eat?
- Just 20 plant species account for 75% of global harvests.
- So few species are used because crops must have high energy density and provide all three macronutrients.
- Domesticated plants are those adapted to human needs — sweeter, larger, and easier to cultivate.
- Sugarcane and maize are used to produce ethanol for use as vehicle fuel.
- Wheat has 7 times the energy density of fruit and 18 times that of vegetables.
- Wheat does not provide enough fat or protein, however — this is where legumes play their role.
- Storage losses for wheat are around 1%, while losses for rice in China can reach 15%.
- After accounting for harvest losses, storage, and seed retention, only about 50% of a crop reaches consumers.
- Cassava can be harvested year-round, whereas potatoes can only be harvested in a specific season.
