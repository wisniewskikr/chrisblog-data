# Content

## I. Hard to believe

- Sector shares in pollution emissions: electricity 25%, agriculture (animals) 24%, industry 24%, transport 14%, buildings 6%, other 7%.

## II. How to prevent the greatest extinction

- Main greenhouse gases: CO2, methane and nitrous oxide.
- 37% of methane comes from livestock farming; methane has a greenhouse effect 34 times greater than CO2.
- 65% of nitrous oxide comes from livestock farming; nitrous oxide has a greenhouse effect 309 times greater than CO2.
- Since the Industrial Revolution, CO2 concentration has risen by 40%.
- Albedo effect: fewer white ice sheets and more dark oceans.
- 59% of agricultural land is used for animal feed.
- 1/3 of the world's water resources go to livestock; only 1/30 goes to direct human use.
- There are 30 livestock animals for every one person.
- 80% of deforestation is caused by the creation of pastures.
- CO2 emissions per kilogram of product: beef 2.99%, cheese 1.11%, pork 0.78%, poultry 0.57%, eggs 0.4%, milk 0.32%, rice 0.07%, legumes 0.04%, carrots 0.03%, potatoes 0.01%.
- Giving up meat reduces CO2 emissions by 1.3 tonnes per year.

## III. The only home

- Key strategies for mitigating climate change: reducing food waste, educating girls, reproductive health care and family planning, plant-based diet.

## IV. Argument with the soul

- The wealthiest 10% of the world is responsible for 50% of CO2 emissions; the poorest 10% is also responsible for 50% of emissions yet suffers the most from climate change – as if you smoked the cigarettes but someone else got the cancer.
- Bangladesh: possible flooding of 1/3 of the country and displacement of 30 million people. The average Bangladeshi emits 0.29 tonnes of CO2 per year and eats 4 kg of meat per year.
- The average Finn emits 11.15 tonnes of CO2 per year and eats 4 kg of meat in 18 days.
- 1 calorie of meat requires up to 26 calories of feed.
