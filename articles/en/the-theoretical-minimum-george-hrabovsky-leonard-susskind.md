# Content

## Lecture 1. The nature of classical physics

### What is classical physics?

- Classical physics is physics before quantum physics.
- It encompasses: particle motion (Newton), electromagnetic fields (Maxwell-Faraday), and general relativity (Einstein).
- Deterministic laws: knowing the state of a system at a given moment and the laws governing it, one can predict its future.

### Simple dynamical systems and state space

- A system is a collection of objects; it can be closed (isolated) or open.
- State space: the set of all states a system can occupy (e.g. for a coin: heads or tails).
- A dynamical system changes over time; it consists of a state space and a law of motion.
- A degree of freedom (sigma) takes the value 1 or -1.
- A cycle is a repeating pattern of states.

### Forbidden laws: the minus-first law

- Laws governing motion are deterministic (they determine the future) and reversible (they determine the past).
- The law of conservation of information: every state has exactly one incoming arrow and one outgoing arrow.

### Dynamical systems with an infinite number of states

- When the number of states is finite, the system forms a cycle and eventually returns to its starting point.
- The number of states can also be infinite.
- A state space may contain one or more cycles.

### Cycles and conservation laws

- Conservation law: if a system starts in a given cycle, it must remain in it — it cannot change cycles. It is denoted by the letter Q.

### Limits of precision

- The resolution of an experiment is the ability to distinguish neighbouring states in a system.
- Chaos: small differences in the initial state lead to large differences in outcomes.
- Most real systems do not exhibit perfect predictability due to limited resolution.

## Interlude 1. Spaces, trigonometry and vectors

### Coordinates

- The origin of a coordinate system can be chosen freely, but once chosen it must be kept fixed.
- Similarly, the zero point of time requires establishing a direction and a unit of time.
- The three axes of a coordinate system are mutually perpendicular.

### Trigonometry

- Trigonometry deals with the properties of right triangles.
- In physics, angles are expressed in radians; one radian equals approximately 57 degrees.

### Vectors

- A vector has magnitude and direction.

### Mathematical interlude: calculus

- Mathematical analysis deals with limits (e.g. the sequence 0.9, 0.99, 0.999 approaches 1).
- Differential calculus studies how the value of a function changes over time.
