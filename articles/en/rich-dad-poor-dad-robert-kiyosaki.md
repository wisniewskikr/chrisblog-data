# Content

## Introduction. Rich dad, poor dad
- School focuses on academic and professional problems, but not financial ones.
- You must ask yourself: how can I afford this?
- The rich don't work for money – money works for them.
- Money gives power, but financial education gives even greater power.

## 1. Lesson 1. The rich don't work for money
- Triangle of learning effectiveness (from most to least effective): 1. Performing a real action; 2. Giving a lecture; 3. Observing a real action; 4. Watching images; 5. Listening to speech; 6. Reading.
- Life pushes us here and there: some people allow it passively, some lash out at their boss or loved ones without realising life is pushing them, and some take that push as a lesson and adapt.
- The poor and the middle class work for money, while the money of the rich works for them.
- Most people's lives are driven by two emotions: fear of poverty and greed when they receive money.
- A person's life is a tug-of-war between ignorance and enlightenment.
- When great civilisations collapse, the gap between the rich and the poor has grown too large.

## 2. Lesson 2. Why financial literacy must be taught
- Life is not about how much money you earn, but how much you keep.
- To be rich, you must acquire financial skills.
- You must know the difference between assets and liabilities and buy assets.
- The rich buy assets; the poor buy liabilities thinking they are assets.
- Assets put money in your pocket; liabilities take it out.
- Accounting is not about numbers but about what they say – just as a text is not about words but about what they mean.
- If you want to be rich, you must be able to read and understand numbers.
- A financial statement covers income and expenses.
- A balance sheet covers assets and liabilities.
- An entrepreneurial team is more important than money.
- Wealth is a measure of how long you can survive if you stop working.

## 3. Lesson 3: Mind your own business
- The main reason for financial caution (risk avoidance) is a lack of financial knowledge.
- Mind your own business: don't quit your job, but start buying assets instead of liabilities.
- What to buy: businesses that don't require your presence, stocks, bonds, real estate, promissory notes, royalties.
- The rich buy luxury goods last; the poor buy them first.

## 4. Lesson 4. The history of taxes and the power of corporations
- Administration: the more people employed and the more money spent, the better.
- Business: the fewer people employed and the less money spent, the better.
- The rich are taxed less because the government gives them incentives to invest and hire more.
- The rich avoid taxes through corporations.
- A corporation first spends everything it can and only then pays taxes on what remains.
- A corporation protects against lawsuits.
- You need knowledge in: 1. Accounting; 2. Investing; 3. Understanding markets; 4. Law.
- The rich: earn → spend → pay taxes.
- The poor: earn → pay taxes → spend.

## 5. Lesson 5. The rich invent money
- In the real world, it is most often the unwise but bold who move forward.
- Wealth used to be land, then factories, and now information – and information knows no borders, unlike land and factories.
- The Cashflow game teaches financial thinking.
- What matters most is how many financial solutions you can devise – how creative you are.
- Key skills: 1. Seeing opportunities with your mind, not your eyes; 2. Raising financing without a bank; 3. Hiring a good team.

## 6. Lesson 6. Work to learn – don't work for money
- It is better to have a little knowledge of many fields than to be a narrow specialist.
- Young people should look for work that teaches rather than merely pays.
- Core skills: 1. Managing money; 2. Managing systems; 3. Managing people.
- Sales = revenue.
- The better you are at communicating, negotiating, and handling rejection, the better you do in life.
- A rich person must be both a good teacher and a good student.

## 7. Overcoming obstacles
- Main obstacles: fear, cynicism, laziness, bad habits, arrogance.
- People are so afraid of loss that they end up losing.
- Winning means not being afraid of losing.
- Failures inspire winners and crush losers.
- If you start early, you can play it safe; if you start late, you must take risks.
- You need courage, patience, and the right attitude toward failure.
- Cynicism is noise – internal or external – that causes you to miss opportunities.
- The best money is made when conditions seem hopeless.
- Cynics criticise; winners analyse.
- The cure for laziness is a little greed.
- Instead of saying "we can't afford it", say "how can I achieve this?".

## 8. Getting started
- 1. Find your motivation: define what you want and what you don't want.
- 2. Make choices: choose education.
- 3. Relationships: spend time with wealthy people and learn from them.
- 4. Learn quickly – you become what you learn.
- 5. Be disciplined.
- 6. Have good advisers and don't cut corners on them.
- 7. Invest: an experienced investor always thinks about how quickly they can get their money back.
- 8. Focus: when you want something, don't borrow – try to earn it.
- 9. Role models: find role models and emulate them.
- 10. Teach others: when you want something, give first – it usually comes back multiplied.

## 9. Want to know more? Here is what you can do
- Finding deals is like going on dates – you have to try many times.
- A crash or correction is like a sale at a supermarket.
- Action is better than inaction.

## Final thought
- There are the games Cashflow 101 and the more advanced Cashflow 202.
- We have two great gifts: time and mind.

## What is your life's goal?
- Growth: physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual.

## Which quadrant are you in? Is it the right one for you?
- Types of workers: E – employee; S – self-employed; B – business owner; I – investor.
- You must work smart, not hard.
