# Content

## I. The problem

### 1. The curse of being the default parent

- Invisible work is work that neither partners nor often the person doing it can see.
- Research shows that men frequently engage in household duties before a child is born, but reduce that involvement after the birth.
- It is worth making invisible work visible – by writing down a list of everything one does.
- What can be measured can be managed.
- The Fair Play game helps solve these problems.

## 2. The hidden costs of doing everything

- Costs to marriage: overburdened women feel greater aversion to their marriage.
- Costs to identity.
- Costs to career.
- Costs to well-being.
- Costs to society.
- Women perform three times more unpaid family-related work than men.
- It is worth writing duties on cards and asking both partners to take the ones they actually perform.

## II. The solution

### 3. Rule number 1: Everyone's time has the same value

- Women's time is treated like sand, men's time like diamonds.
- After a child is born, the time men spend on duties increases by 40 minutes, while for women it increases by 2.5 hours.
- We count time in minutes, not in currency.
