# Content

## 1. The daredevils

- Musk's grandfather, Joshua Haldeman, was an adventurer – he moved the family from Canada to South Africa, flew a plane, and died in a plane crash.
- He had a twin daughter, Maye, Musk's mother, who worked as a model and dietitian.
- Errol Musk was an engineer who built hotels and apartment buildings, flew a plane, and smuggled emeralds.
- Errol had known Maye since childhood; they married, and Maye became pregnant during their honeymoon trip to Europe.

## 2. A lonely mind

- Musk's siblings are Kimbal and Tosca.
- Musk was unable to form friendships.
- Musk could completely tune out and hear nothing.
- Musk has Asperger's and lacks empathy.
- Musk's parents divorced; the children went to live with their mother.

## 3. Life with father

- At age 10, Musk chose to live with his father in Pretoria.
- The Musk brothers stuck together with three cousins.

## 4. The seeker

- Musk loved reading, mostly science fiction.
- Musk bought a Commodore computer and taught himself BASIC.
- He wrote computer games.

## 5. Escape velocity

- Musk applied for a Canadian passport through his mother.
- Before his 18th birthday he flew to the USA.

## 6. Canada

- Musk travelled across Canada visiting family; in Vancouver he cleaned boilers at a lumber mill.
- Maye flew to Canada with Kimbal and Tosca and they all moved in with Musk in Toronto.

## 7. Queens University

- Musk began his studies at Queen's University in Kingston and met Navid Farooq there.
- He played strategy games.
- Musk did an internship at Scotiabank.

## 8. Penn

- In his third year Musk transferred to the University of Pennsylvania (Penn), where he studied physics and management.
- He met Robin Ren there.
- He rented a house and threw parties.

## 9. Going west

- In 1994, between his third and fourth year, Musk completed two internships in Silicon Valley: at Pinnacle Research Institute (supercapacitors) and at Rocket Science (computer games).
- After graduating he enrolled in a doctoral programme at Stanford but immediately put it on hold.
- He began designing Virtual City Navigator – displaying businesses on a map.

## 10. Zip2

- Elon and Kimbal moved to Palo Alto, California, and named their programme Zip2 – displaying businesses on a map.
- They established contact with Navteq, which held digital map databases.
- Mohr Davidow invested 3 million dollars and brought in its own CEO, Sorokin; they made the service available to newspapers.
- In 1999, four years after founding, they sold the company for 307 million dollars; the Musk brothers held 12% (split 60/40): Elon earned 22 million and Kimbal 15 million.

## 11. Justine

- Musk met Justine Wilson at Queen's University; she spent a year in Japan teaching English, and they became a couple after she returned.
- They argued a lot but got married.

## 12. X.com

- Musk invested 12 million dollars in X.com, an internet bank; his partner was a banking acquaintance, Harris Fricker, with whom he eventually parted ways.
- Moritz invested money.
- The service enabled payments by email, which were used on eBay.
- X.com competed with Levchin and Thiel's PayPal, and the two later merged.

## 13. The coup

- The board staged a coup to focus on PayPal rather than the internet bank.
- Musk is filled with self-confidence that leads him to always bet everything.
- PayPal was sold to eBay for 1.5 billion dollars; Musk received 250 million.
- He went on holiday to South Africa, and after returning nearly died from malignant malaria – he was hours from death.

## 14. Mars

- Musk obtained a pilot's licence.
- Musk received US citizenship.
- Musk moved to Los Angeles.
- Musk met Cantrell, a rocket engineer.

## 15. Rocket Man

- Musk flew to Moscow to buy rockets, but the Russians mocked him.
- In 2002 Space Exploration Technologies – SpaceX – was founded.

## 16. Fathers and sons

- His son Nevada was born but died of sudden infant death syndrome.
- Musk's father visited with his new family; Musk bought them a house, after which his father returned to South Africa.

## 17. Putting the pedal to the metal

- Musk recruited Tom Mueller to SpaceX; Tom worked at TRW and built amateur rockets on weekends.
- Their headquarters were at Los Angeles airport.
- They began building the Falcon 1 rocket.

## 18. Musk's rocket-building rules

- One must present an ambitious plan that looks achievable.
- An iterative approach was used.
- They bought a test site in McGregor, Texas.

## 19. Mr. Musk goes to Washington

- Musk hired Gwynne Shotwell as director of business development.
- As a publicity stunt, Musk brought the Falcon 1 rocket to Washington.
- Musk won a lawsuit against NASA after a competitor won a contract without a tender because it was on the verge of bankruptcy.
- As a result of the court victory, NASA moved away from cost-plus contracts – which covered costs plus a profit margin.

## 20. The founders

- Gage of AC Propulsion built an electric car called the tZero.
- Eberhard bought the licence and registered the Tesla Motors brand.

## 21. Roadster

- Tesla's most important decision was to manufacture its own parts.
- The Roadster – Tesla's first car – was nonetheless built from off-the-shelf components.
- VantagePoint invested 40 million dollars in Tesla.
- Musk's plan was to start with luxury electric vehicles and move downmarket over time.

## 22. Kwaj

- SpaceX relocated to Kwaj island; the engineers moved there with the company.

## 23. Two failures

- On 24 March 2006, Falcon 1 launched from Kwaj but crashed shortly after liftoff; corrosion caused by the island's salt air was to blame, and the decision to use aluminium nuts had been made by Musk.
- The 2007 launch also ended in disaster – anti-slosh baffles had not been installed.

## 24. The special task force

- Gracias and Watkins assessed Tesla's condition; the company had very long supply chains – 9 months.
- Production cost was 140,000 dollars while the cars sold for 100,000 dollars.

## 25. Taking the helm

- Eberhard left Tesla.
- Michael Marks was CEO briefly but was too soft.
- Eventually Musk took the helm himself.

## 26. Divorce

- Through IVF, Justine first gave birth to twins and later to triplets.
- Musk has a strong will and emotional distance, which serves him well in business but not in marriage.
- Musk and Justine divorced.

## 27. Talulah

- In London, at a club, Musk met actress Talulah Riley.
- They got engaged after a few weeks.

## 28. The third failure

- The third launch of Falcon 1 – this time carrying several satellites – failed; after stage separation, residual fuel in the second stage caused it to strike the first.
- The fix was simply to extend the separation delay.

## 29. On the edge

- On 1 February 2008 the first Roadster rolled off the line, still assembled by hand.
- The 2008 financial crash hit; Kimbal lent his brother money, and then the bank collapsed.
- Everyone advised Musk to choose between Tesla and SpaceX, but he chose both.

## 30. The fourth launch

- Former PayPal colleagues invested in Musk.
- A fourth launch attempt was made; the rocket was flown by plane but pressure damaged it – they repaired it on Kwaj in five days.
- On 28 September 2008 Falcon 1 launched successfully.
- Shotwell was to share responsibilities at SpaceX with Musk.
- In December SpaceX won a contract for 12 cargo deliveries to the International Space Station for 1.6 billion dollars.

## 31. Saving Tesla

- Salzman refused to provide funding for Tesla.
- Tesla received a loan from the Department of Energy.
- Tesla showed a modified Smart car to German firm Daimler; a contract for battery supply was signed and Daimler also invested in Tesla.

## 32. Model S

- Von Holzhausen took charge of the car's design.
- The batteries were placed in the floor.
- The door handle became a Tesla signature feature.
- A touchscreen panel was installed, enabling over-the-air software updates.

## 33. The private space industry

- To meet NASA requirements, a rocket with 5 engines instead of 1 was developed – the Falcon 9.
- The Dragon capsule was created to dock with the station and then return to Earth.
- They leased launch pad 40 at Cape Canaveral.
- Obama supported Musk's programme.

## 34. The Falcon 9 launch

- Falcon 9 was launched; the first attempt was successful.

## 35. The wedding with Talulah

- In 2010 Musk and Talulah got married.

## 36. Production

- Tesla bought a factory in the USA from Toyota.
- Tesla went public and shares jumped 40%.
- In June 2012 the Model S rolled off the line.
- Tesla and Panasonic built a battery factory in Nevada; Panasonic covered 40% of the costs.

## 37. Musk and Bezos

- Bezos founded Blue Origin, a rocket-building company.
- Musk secured launch pad 39A and Bezos filed a lawsuit.

## 38. The falcon hears the falconer

- The first attempt to land the Grasshopper – a rocket designed to land vertically – failed.
- The landing of the first stage of the rocket was later successful.

## 39. Talulah and the emotional rollercoaster

- Musk divorced Talulah but they reconciled.
- They remarried.
- They divorced again.

## 40. Artificial intelligence

- Google acquired DeepMind and Musk grew concerned about artificial intelligence.
- He co-founded OpenAI – an artificial intelligence project based on open-source principles.

## 41. Autopilot

- Autopilot means the car is driven by a computer; initially Tesla and Google were to develop this together, but Musk fell out with Page over AI.
- Google chose radar; Tesla chose cameras, though Musk agreed to include sensors as well.

## 42. Solar

- Musk's cousins, the Rives, founded SolarCity, which installed solar panels.
- Musk was already producing the Powerwall – a refrigerator-shaped energy storage unit; he then acquired SolarCity and they began producing solar roof tiles.

## 43. The Boring Company

- Musk founded a tunnel-drilling company and invested 100 million dollars of his own money in it.

## 44. Difficult relationships

- When Trump withdrew the USA from the Paris Agreement, Musk stopped serving on his advisory councils.
- Musk entered a relationship with actress Amber Heard.
- Musk's father fathered a child with his stepdaughter; from that point on Musk cut off all contact with him.

## 45. The descent into darkness

- Musk began producing the Model 3 and calculated he needed to produce 5,000 cars a week.
- He went to the battery factory in Nevada and began streamlining the production process.
- Musk learned that one must first perfect a process and only then automate it.

## 46. Hell in Fremont

- Musk moved to the car factory in Fremont.
- He tied his compensation to the factory's success.
- Red lights indicated problems – that is where Musk headed.
- He set up a tent in which he launched the third production line.
- Musk's algorithm:
  1. Question all requirements – if one exists, its author must be identified.
  2. Eliminate everything you can.
  3. Simplify everything you can.
  4. Accelerate the cycle.
  5. Automate.
- Additional principles:
  - Technical department managers must have hands-on experience.
  - Fraternizing is dangerous.
  - There is nothing wrong with making a mistake.
  - Never ask subordinates to do what you would not do yourself.
  - Discuss problems not only with managers but also with workers.
  - Attitude matters – skills can be learned, but changing attitude requires a brain transplant.
  - Mandates are merely the laws of physics – everything else is a recommendation.

## 47. Open loop alarm

- Musk built a mini submarine to help the boys trapped in a cave in Thailand.
- The submarine was not used, and one of the cave divers mocked the idea – Musk called him a pedophile.
- Musk first announced his intention to take Tesla private, then changed his mind.
- Musk lost the case against the Securities and Exchange Commission.

## 48. Fallout

- Musk smoked a joint during a live interview.
- A Flamethrower was manufactured and sold.
- Kimbal briefly cut ties with Musk when Musk refused to support his restaurant.

## 49. Grimes

- Musk entered into a relationship with singer Grimes – they met on Twitter.
- Rapper Banks, who had worked with Grimes, publicly described Musk on Twitter.
- Musk switches between different personalities.

## 50. Shanghai

- Musk wanted to manufacture cars in China; the Chinese government agreed to allow the venture without a joint-venture requirement.
- The Chinese Tesla operation was run by Tom Zhu, a friend of Musk's from university.

## 51. Cybertruck

- Tesla designed the futuristic Cybertruck.

## 52. Starlink

- Musk wanted to cover the sky with his satellites – up to 40,000 of them.
- The construction of the satellites was significantly simplified.

## 53. Starship

- The new rocket Starship was designed to carry 100 tonnes of payload.
- Stainless steel was chosen as the construction material.
- The rocket was built in Boca Chica.

## 54. Autonomy Day

- Musk wanted to secure funding for Tesla and therefore decided to present an autonomous car to investors.

## 55. Giga Texas

- Austin joined Fremont, Berlin, and Shanghai as another Tesla factory location.
- The Austin factory used the Gigapress – a chassis was produced in 80 seconds.

## 56. Family life

- Grimes gave birth to a son, X.
- Musk's son Xavier became Jenna.
- Musk sold his houses – his main base became Boca Chica.

## 57. Full flow

- In 2020, SpaceX sent astronauts into space.

## 58. Bezos vs. Musk, round two

- Billionaires Bezos and Branson flew to space.

## 59. Surge: Starship

- The rocket was to be caught by a tower, eliminating the need for landing legs.
- Idiot index = cost of material / total cost.

## 60. Surge: solar roofs

- Tesla Energy installs approximately 30 solar roofs per week.

## 61. Nights on the town

- Musk celebrated his 50th birthday.
- He and Grimes repeatedly got together and broke up.

## 62. Inspiration4

- Inspiration4 was the first fully civilian spaceflight.

## 63. Raptor shake-up

- Work began on slimming down the Raptor engine.
- Work began on the engine designated 1337.

## 64. The birth of Optimus

- Optimus is a humanoid robot being developed by Musk's team.

## 65. Neuralink

- Neuralink is a chip designed to enable communication between humans and computers.

## 66. Vision only

- Musk relied exclusively on a vision-based system, though he also explored the capabilities of modern radars.

## 67. Money

- In 2021, Musk's net worth reached $190 billion – he was the richest person in the world.
- He paid $11 billion in taxes.

## 68. Father of the year

- Musk fathered twins with Shivon Zilis, an employee working on Neuralink – the children were conceived via IVF.
- He did not tell Grimes.
- At the same time, Grimes gave birth to a daughter, Y.

## 69. Politics

- Tesla factories downplayed the threat of Covid-19.
- Musk shifted his political views – from Democrat to Republican.
- Musk loves playing the strategy game Polytopia.

## 70. Ukraine

- Musk sent thousands of Starlink kits to Ukraine.
- He turned off Starlink coverage around Crimea, stating that the system was intended for defensive use only.

## 71. Bill Gates

- Musk had a falling-out with Gates, who was betting against Tesla stock.

## 72. Active investor

- Musk joined Twitter's board and acquired a 14.9% stake in the company.

## 73. I made an offer

- Musk made a bid to acquire Twitter; users would be required to verify their identity.

## 74. Hot and cold

- Musk alternately wanted and did not want to acquire Twitter.

## 75. Father's Day

- A complicated Father's Day – Grimes found out about the twins with Shivon Zilis.

## 76. Starbase shake-up

- Musk ordered another surge – instead of 10 days, he set a 1-day deadline to complete the task.

## 77. Optimus Prime

- The Optimus robot began walking and carrying packages.

## 78. Uncertainty

- Musk went to court against Twitter's board.

## 79. Optimus presentation

- Optimus was unveiled during AI Day 2.

## 80. Robotaxi

- Tesla began designing a fully autonomous taxi priced at $20,000.
- Until its launch, vehicles were to be equipped with a steering wheel and pedals.

## 81. Let that sink in

- Musk visited Twitter's headquarters.

## 82. Takeover

- Musk executed a hostile takeover to save $200 million.
- He did not pay severance packages to the executives.

## 83. The three musketeers

- Musk brought in his cousins – engineers from Tesla and SpaceX – to identify employees to be let go.
- He fired 90% of the engineers.

## 84. Content moderation

- Musk initially wanted to establish a content moderation council but later abandoned the idea.

## 85. Halloween

- Musk amplified a tweet suggesting that a prominent congresswoman's husband had been attacked by a male prostitute – advertising revenue fell by 80%.

## 86. Blue checkmarks

- Musk wanted to introduce Twitter Blue – account verification – but the rollout went badly.
- Another problem was Apple's policy of not sharing user data.

## 87. All in

- Musk sent employees a survey asking those who wanted to stay to click "yes."

## 88. Hardcore

- The new Twitter policy: freedom of speech but not freedom of reach – hateful content must be actively searched for.
- Musk reinstated many suspended accounts, including Trump's.
- Twitter's workforce shrank from 8,000 to 2,000 employees.

## 89. Miracles

- Neuralink was also intended to help paralyzed patients and to restore sight and hearing.

## 90. The Twitter files

- Musk invited independent journalists to examine how censorship had previously operated on the platform.

## 91. Rabbit holes

- Musk began personally suspending journalists' accounts.
- Musk shared statements by Roth, forcing Roth to move out of his home.

## 92. Holiday antics

- Musk and his three musketeers personally flew to Sacramento to move servers.

## 93. AI for cars

- Musk decided that artificial intelligence would develop the autonomous driving software for Tesla.

## 95. Starship launch

- Starship launched but exploded in the atmosphere.
