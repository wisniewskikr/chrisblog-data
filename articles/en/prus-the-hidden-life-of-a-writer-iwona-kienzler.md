# Content

## 1. An orphan's fate – the sad childhood of Bolesław Prus

- Aleksander Głowacki was born in Hrubieszów in 1847; the pen name "Prus" comes from his father's family coat of arms.
- His father was a steward on the estate of Jan Wydżga; his older brother was Leon.
- His parents separated; his mother died in 1850.
- He was raised by his grandmother in Puławy; at the age of 7 he moved to Lublin, to his mother's sister.
- His father died when he was 8–9 years old.
- His brother Leon became a teacher in Siedlce and Aleksander moved in with him; they then both moved to Kielce.

## 2. The insurgent Aleksander Głowacki

- When the January Uprising broke out, 16-year-old Aleksander joined the fighting.
- There was no regular army in the uprising.
- He had doubts and experienced his first nervous breakdown.
- He was wounded at Białka; his eyesight weakened and he began wearing tinted glasses.
- His brother Leon fell mentally ill.
- Prus developed a fear of open spaces (agoraphobia).

## 3. In search of a path in life

- After the uprising he returned to school, but was arrested for four months for his participation in the fighting.
- After his release he returned to school in Lublin.
- He enrolled in mathematics at the Main School of Warsaw, but failed his exams in his second year.
- He worked as a private tutor on an estate, then enrolled in the Forestry Department of the Institute in Puławy, from which he was expelled.
- He began writing for the weeklies *Opiekun Domowy* and *Niwa*; he also worked in a factory.
- He joined the editorial staff of *Kurier Warszawski*.

## 4. Beloved Taciunia – Oktawia Trembińska

- Oktawia Trembińska was a distant cousin of Prus; the wedding took place in Lublin.
- For twelve years the couple cared for his mother-in-law.
- They took on the upbringing of Emil, the child of Oktawia's deceased brother.
- Emil committed suicide as a young boy.

## 5. Journalist, chronicler, writer

- In his columns he engaged with social issues, writing about education, hygiene, and physical fitness.
- His first more serious novel was *Anielka*.
- He wrote *The Doll* in instalments, inspired by Sienkiewicz, though the latter was far more disciplined than he was.
- He wrote the novels *The Emancipated Women* and *Pharaoh*; the action of *Pharaoh* is set in Egypt, even though he never went there – he prepared thoroughly but could not avoid mistakes.
- Prus was the first writer in Polish lands to use a typewriter.

## 6. Romantic Nałęczów

- Prus disparaged students in the press; in response, students attacked him.
- His illness worsened; he went to a sanatorium in Nałęczów, where he wrote and rode a bicycle.
- He fell in love with Oktawia Rodkiewiczowa, who subsequently married Stefan Żeromski.
- He had a relationship with Alina Sacewiczowa, a widow of a doctor from Nałęczów; they had a son, Jan Bogusz.
- Jan Bogusz became an engineer, took part in the Warsaw Uprising, and died in 1945 in an officers' prisoner-of-war camp.
- Prus died in 1912.

## 7. Illnesses and other troubles – the writer at the end of his life

- Prus visited Kraków, Lviv, the Tatra Mountains, and Nałęczów.
- He went abroad once in his life, visiting Germany, Switzerland, and France.
- He bought a typewriter and a camera.
- When Tsar Nicholas II ascended to the Russian throne, Prus took part in a committee raising money for the emperor's visit.
- He opposed the construction of a Mickiewicz monument, instead wanting to build a dormitory for poor students named after Mickiewicz.
- He died in 1912.
