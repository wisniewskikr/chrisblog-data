# Content

## I. A „peaceful, angelic” childhood

- His father, Mikołaj Mickiewicz, was a lawyer and a court bailiff.
- His mother was Barbara, née Majewska.
- He was born on 24 December 1798 in Nowogródek or Zaosie.
- His siblings were: Franciszek (older), Aleksander, Kazimierz, Barbara, and Michał.
- His father was injured in a fire and died.

## 2. Learning, secret societies, and carefree fun – Adam Mickiewicz's youth

- He studied at Vilnius University, supported by a scholarship obtained through a relative.
- He joined the Society of Philomaths.
- He made his debut in 1818 in the "Vilnius Weekly" with the poem "Zima miejska" ("City Winter").
- After his studies he taught in Kaunas, working off his scholarship.
- He wrote, among other works, "Ballads and Romances", "Dziady", "Grażyna", "Konrad Wallenrod", and "Ode to Youth".
- He was arrested by Novosiltsev and exiled to Odessa.

## 5. European peregrinations and love affairs

- He travelled through Berlin and Dresden.
- Antoni Odyniec became de facto his secretary during the journey.
- They travelled around Italy; Mickiewicz spent a year in Rome.
- He fell in love with Henriette.

## 6. The insurgent legend of Adam Mickiewicz

- He was in no hurry to join the uprising, probably not believing in its success; he presumably acknowledged the tsar's authority.
- He arrived in Greater Poland, where he celebrated.
- He began a romance with Konstancja Łubieńska, a married woman.
- He wrote Part III of "Dziady" very quickly.
- He went to Dresden.

## 7. On the Paris streets

- In Paris he was a rival of Słowacki.
- Two main societies were active in the émigré community: Czartoryski's and Lelewel's.
- He helped publish the works of Garczyński.
- He wrote "Pan Tadeusz", which was not popular at first.
- He married Celina Szymanowska.

## 8. Husband and father

- Daughter Maria, son Władysław, and daughter Helena were born.
- Celina began to suffer from mental illness.
- Mickiewicz took up a professorship in Lausanne, Switzerland.
- The family returned to Paris.

## 9. In Towiański's circle

- He was appointed to a professorial chair in Paris.
- Celina's mental illness returned.
- Andrzej Towiański appeared and was said to have healed Celina.
- The governess Ksawera Deybel became Mickiewicz's mistress; they had at least one child together.
- With Celina he had further children: Aleksander, Jan, and Józef.
- He was given the post of librarian at the Arsenal Library.
- Celina died probably of cancer.

## 10. The end of the road

- In Italy he formed the Polish Legion, which supported the Italians in their fight against the Austrians.
- He went to Constantinople to unite the quarrelling factions of the Polish émigré community.
- He died of cholera.
- He was buried in France, and four years later his remains were laid to rest at Wawel Cathedral.
