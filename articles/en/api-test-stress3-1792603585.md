# Content
- Stress is commonly viewed as harmful, but research suggests it is only bad for you if you *believe* it is bad for you.
- A study of 30,000 adults found that high stress increased the risk of dying — but only among people who believed stress was harmful. Those who did not view stress as harmful had no elevated risk.
- When you change your mindset about stress, your body's physical response can change too — for example, seeing a racing heart as your body energizing you for a challenge.
- The stress hormone oxytocin encourages you to seek social support; it is also cardioprotective, helping your heart recover from stress.
- Reaching out to others is a powerful and underappreciated way to reduce stress and build resilience.
- Making stress your friend — rather than an enemy — may be one of the most important changes you can make for your health.
