# Content

- Stress itself — a racing heart, faster breathing, sweating — is not necessarily harmful to health.
- Research suggests that stress is only bad for you if you *believe* it is bad for you; your mindset matters.
- Viewing your body's stress response as helpful (preparing you for action) can make you more resilient and even courageous.
- People who reframe stress as a positive tend to have better health outcomes than those who see it as a threat.
- Stress also triggers the release of oxytocin, a hormone that encourages you to seek social connection and support.
- Reaching out to others — caring and connecting — is a powerful, often overlooked way to reduce stress and protect your health.
- The goal is not to eliminate stress, but to change how you think about it.
