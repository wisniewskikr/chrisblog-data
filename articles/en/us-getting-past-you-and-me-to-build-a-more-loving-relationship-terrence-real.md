# Content

## 1. Which version of you shows up in a relationship?
- Relational Life Therapy
- During arguments, the primitive part of the brain takes over and does not care about maintaining intimacy
- Couples have two elements: stressors and dynamics
- Trauma is not remembered – it is relived
- Emotional coldness has no positive characteristics
- There is an Adaptive Child and a Wise Adult
- The culture of individualism reinforces the Adaptive Child, which takes a toll on personal life
- The solution is mindfulness

## 2. The myth of individualism
- Neuroplasticity: you can change yourself
- Conditions for change: what is hidden must be revealed; a sense of contradiction must arise and previous behaviour must be rejected
- The mind exists in a social context – partners grow to resemble each other
- We are individualists whose existence depends on belonging
- The prefrontal cortex consumes the most energy – it therefore delegates tasks to the primitive brain and to others
- We can only control ourselves, and only under favourable circumstances
- Men transmit information, while women communicate
- Example of a repair response: "I'm sorry you felt bad. Is there anything I can say or do to help you feel better?"

## 3. How "we" gets lost and "you and I" takes over
- The Adaptive Child should not be destroyed – it should be mothered
- Attacking from the position of a victim
- Children are like gas – they will find every crack, so boundaries must be set
- Small trauma: something repeated 100,000 times
- Two axes (health in the middle): abandonment–intrusion and false empowerment–low self-worth
- The Adaptive Child acts in opposition to what it experienced, or models – repeating what it experienced

## 4. The individualist at home
- John Locke's individualism – of reason
- Goethe's individualism – of emotion
- Franklin: "We must all hang together, or we shall all hang separately"
- When one partner wins and the other loses, both lose

## 5. Start thinking like a team
- We should not be passive passengers in our own relationship
- You cannot change your partner, but you can change your interactions with them by changing your own behaviour
- Do not defend yourself – yield to criticism
- Do not try to fix your problems from the level of the Adaptive Child – wait for the Wise Adult

## 6. You cannot love from a position of inferiority or superiority
- Individualism celebrates separateness
- Patriarchy celebrates dominance
- Grandiosity and shame are the same feeling – contempt
- Every person has inherent worth: no one is better, no one is worse
- A boy asks: "What do you have for me?"; a man asks: "What can I offer?"

## 7. Your fantasies have collapsed – you can start building a real relationship
- Betrayal ends the marriage as it was – it can end the relationship or become the start of a new one
- 2/3 of marriages survive infidelity
- Your partner will not heal you, but they will keep pressing on the most tender spots
- Reason for fidelity: I prefer to live in integrity – I do what I say and think
- Reasons for infidelity: ego outweighs relational commitment ("I deserve it") or an unsatisfying relationship
- Three phases of a relationship: harmonious (love without knowledge), disharmonious (knowledge without love), knowing love
- 7 mismatches to 3 matches can be sustained if both partners are willing to improve
- Partners must regulate distance: not too close, not too far

## 8. Hot intimacy, soft power
- Couples must have a repair mechanism
- Causes of breakdown: couples fight (ignore complaints and remind each other why they are together) or couples ignore each other (bring all contentious issues to the surface)
- Key phrases: "I feel that…" and "I imagine that you feel…"
- The Feedback Wheel: (1) I remember this happening, (2) this is what I imagined, (3) this is what I felt, (4) this would help me feel better
- How to communicate effectively: start by praising your partner and the relationship, state your intention, then use the Feedback Wheel
- What to avoid: escalating from an event ("you were late") to a tendency ("you are always late") to a character judgment ("you are selfish")
- There are 7 core feelings: joy, love, pain, anger, fear, shame, guilt
- Receive a message directly and clearly

## 9. A better future for children
- Family pathology is passed from generation to generation until someone stops it
- Children respond simultaneously through reaction (doing the exact opposite) and modelling (doing the same thing)
- Research shows that anger and indignation only produce anger or withdrawal in a partner

## 10. Becoming whole
- Racism is based on the illusion of individualism and is a narcissistic disorder
- Types of children in a dysfunctional family: the hero child, the scapegoat child, the lost child
- Intimacy is vulnerability
- Wealth has a negative effect on empathy

## Epilogue. The refraction of light
- One should be neither too open nor too closed
