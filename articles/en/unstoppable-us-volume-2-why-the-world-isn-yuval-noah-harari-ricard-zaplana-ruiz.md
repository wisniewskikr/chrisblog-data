# Content

## 1. Everything under control

- About 10,000 years ago a great change took place.
- In the Middle East, grains grew wild – there was no need to travel in search of food.
- The advantage of grains: they can be stored for many months.
- Permanent villages emerged and people began cultivating wheat.
- In bad years, crop diseases, locust invasions, or droughts occurred.
- Temples and granaries were built to store grain reserves for hard times.
- Walls were erected to protect the grain from being stolen by other tribes.
- People began to raise animals.
- Dogs are unrivalled at sensing human emotions.
- Farmers grew in number and gradually displaced hunter-gatherers.

## 2. Oops, we didn't see that coming

- The skeletons of hunter-gatherers are taller, have more teeth, and show fewer signs of hunger and disease than the skeletons of farmers.
- Farmers' skeletons are shorter, with missing teeth and many signs of famine and illness.
- Relying on only a few food sources turned out to be very dangerous.
- Farmers had far more children than nomads: nomads had 3–4 children because the little ones had to walk long distances on their own and were breastfed until their third or fourth year; farmers stayed in one place and could wean children sooner, replacing breast milk with cow's or goat's milk.
- Farmers were plagued by diseases – not moving around meant poorer hygiene, and close contact with animals facilitated the spread of illness.
- The more possessions, the more reasons for conflict.
- Farmers began to practise delayed gratification.

## 3. Things adults are afraid of

- Agriculture made life more complicated – mainly because of the need to live in large groups.
- Adults are afraid of taxes.
- Writing was invented to store information.
- Bureaucracy was invented to retrieve and organise information.

## 4. Pipe dreams

- People follow rules when they genuinely believe in them.
- Rules are based on stories.
- Stories were told by priests.
- If you hear a story many times, you begin to believe it is true.
- Adults are most afraid of the unknown.
- Rituals were meant to confirm the truth of the stories.
- Three kinds of things: (1) things you can see, (2) things you can feel, (3) shared dreams.
- The most important question: is anyone suffering because of this story?
