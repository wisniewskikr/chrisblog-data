# Content

## Fall in love with yourself

- Changing your attitude toward yourself is important – otherwise a self-fulfilling prophecy takes hold.
- The pillars of self-esteem: beliefs about yourself (what you think of yourself), self-image (how you evaluate your appearance), self-reward, and sense of self-efficacy.
- I deserve everything that will allow me to grow and make me happy.

## Towards positive beliefs about yourself

- Our culture glorifies self-sabotage: we attribute mistakes to ourselves and success to luck.
- Happiness is not a station to arrive at, but a way of travelling.
- Guidelines for positive beliefs about yourself:
  - Be more flexible in judging yourself and others.
  - Revise your goals and assess the realistic chances of achieving them.
  - Don't focus only on what is bad about yourself.
  - Don't think badly of yourself.
  - Love yourself whenever possible.
  - Try to bring your ideal self closer to your real self.
  - Learn how to lose.
- Important factors: moderate self-criticism, objective self-observation, constructive assessment of yourself and your capabilities, setting rational and reasonable goals.

## Towards a positive self-image

- The art of life is not about having good cards, but about playing well with the ones you've been dealt.
- Beauty is a relative concept, dependent on era and region (e.g. in Guatemala a large woman is considered ideal because she can carry a lot of firewood).
- Sometimes we are enchanted by a person who does not match our aesthetic criteria.
- How to improve your self-image:
  - Define your own criteria for what is beautiful or aesthetic.
  - Forget about physical perfection and imposed aesthetic standards.
  - Discover and accept what you like about yourself.
  - Remember that the way you see yourself is transmitted to others.
  - Remember that appearance is only one component of your image.
  - Don't exaggerate what you dislike about yourself.
  - Believe that someone will want to love you.
  - Avoid unfair comparisons.

## Towards better self-reward

- You should appreciate, encourage, reward, and show affection to yourself.
- Deferring pleasure is a positive trait, but it should not be taken too far.
- Hedonism: if something causes no harm to you or anyone else, it is permitted.
- Happiness does not knock on our door by itself – you have to seek it out and fight for it.
- We have drifted away from exploratory behaviours (curiosity about life, the pleasure of discovery and surprise) and from the capacity to feel (feelings are automatic, require less effort, and are difficult to stop or put into words).
- Feelings and logic are intertwined – logic can strengthen or weaken feelings.
- You may feel anything, as long as you do not violate the rights of others, do not harm yourself, and it makes you happy.
- Our mind is an untamed chatterbox – what matters is the tone of your inner conversation with yourself.
- Obstacles to praising yourself:
  - "I don't deserve it / It was nothing great" – humility is good, but not to the point of hypocrisy.
  - "It was my duty / I had to do it" – your primary duty is to take care of yourself.
  - "Praising yourself is in bad taste" – not if you do it in private.
- Types of compliments: impersonal (e.g. you have a nice home), personal (e.g. you've decorated your home very tastefully), focused on specific traits (e.g. you are very intelligent), involving the engagement of the person giving the compliment (e.g. I like your figure). Each type can be applied to oneself.
- A miser lives in poverty but has a lavish funeral.
- Harmful ideas: the cult of habit, the cult of rationalisation, the cult of self-control, the cult of modesty.
- Self-control is good for resisting destructive temptations or gaining a greater benefit in the future.
- How to reward yourself effectively: reserve time for pleasures, live hedonistically, rationalise positive emotions less often, praise yourself, be modest – but not excessively so, enjoy yourself, free yourself from psychological and emotional limitations.

## Strengthening the sense of self-efficacy

- One should neither be overly ambitious (negative impact on self-esteem) nor lack ambition entirely (prevents psychological development).
- Sense of self-efficacy: belief in oneself and the conviction that one is capable of achieving the expected results.
- Reasons for low self-esteem:
  - The feeling that nothing can be done – learned helplessness; one should never stop fighting.
  - Locus of control: internal (I steer my own behaviour) versus external (someone or something steers my behaviour).
- Ghosts are scarier from a distance than up close.
- Avoidance is good when we face a real, actual danger.
- Strategies: never say "I can't", don't be a pessimist or a fatalist, try to be a realist, don't remember only the bad things, revise your goals, take on challenges and expose yourself to risk.
- We learn best not from successes, but from mistakes.
