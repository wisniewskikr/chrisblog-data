# Content

## 1. Why can't I sleep?

- Serotonin: the calm hormone, produced from foods such as bananas and cherries.
- Melatonin: the sleep hormone; newborns do not start producing it until around 3 months of age.
- Adrenaline: the fight-or-flight hormone, produced instantaneously.
- Cortisol: the stress hormone, released slowly; it provides motivation and supports the fight against viruses.
- Fermented foods: sauerkraut and pickles, sourdough bread, kombucha.
- Tryptophan: white rice, nuts, lentils, sesame and sunflower seeds, cherries, avocado, bananas.
- The parasympathetic nervous system is responsible for calming; it is stimulated by the vagus nerve, which loves cold water (not every day).
- The sympathetic nervous system is responsible for arousal.
- Deep breathing is helpful.

## 2. Why can't I stop eating?

- The belief that "everything in moderation" is fine is nonsense — highly processed food is addictive.
- Good fat enables the body to burn fat.
- Ghrelin: the hunger hormone.
- Leptin: the satiety hormone.
- Insulin: regulates blood sugar levels.
- Dopamine: the desire hormone.
- Type 1 diabetes: a congenital condition — the body does not produce insulin and cannot take up sugar from the blood.
- Type 2 diabetes: acquired — the insulin response does not work properly.
- Bad products: vegetable oils; emulsifiers (soy lecithin, carrageenan, mono- and diglycerides); sugar under various names (fructose, glucose, sucrose, dates, honey).
- Good products: MCT oil, olive oil, flaxseed oil, avocado oil.
- Cholesterol: HDL (good) and LDL (bad).
- MCT oils suppress appetite.
- Apple cider vinegar before a meal.
- Berberine: a natural appetite suppressant.
- Healthy snacks: olives, avocado, almonds, boiled potatoes.

## 3. Why do I feel like I'm losing my mind?

- Neurotransmitters: brain signals that influence thoughts, feelings, and reflex responses (movement, heartbeat); produced in the brain and acting over a short distance — across a synapse.
- Hormones: chemical signals influencing mood, growth, development, and reproduction; produced by glands and organs throughout the body, acting over a long distance — through the bloodstream.
- Hormones do not operate in isolation but interact with each other.
- Maldon salt contains many minerals.
- Listening to white noise is helpful.

## 4. Why do I feel so depressed?

- Red light therapy.
- EPA: a supplement.
- Bath: first hot, then cold.

## 5. Where does all this anger come from?

- Most stress and anger is actually fear.
- It is worth washing fruit and vegetables with apple cider vinegar and baking soda.
- Use turmeric with pepper and salt.

## 6. What is happening to my hormonal cycle?

- The DUTCH test is used to measure hormone levels.
