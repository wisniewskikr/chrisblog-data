# Content

## Foreword
- Happiness often requires deferring gratification and avoiding short-term solutions.
- Motivation is what connects what we say with what we do.

## 1. The power of motivation
- Optimism is the most important predictor of success and happiness.
- By improving yourself, you improve society.
- You can only control your own emotions and thoughts.
- When you act, you focus on one thing, which helps you get rid of negative emotions.
- The best remedy for worry is consistent action and pursuit of a goal.
- Roosevelt: Do what you can, with what you have, where you are.
- What distinguishes realized ideas from unrealized ones is the motivation of their creators.
- We fail to achieve goals for the following reasons: 1. comfort zone, 2. fear of failure, 3. not knowing how to do it.
- Many problems arise because people express views and opinions without thinking.
- Decisions made too quickly are bad decisions.
- Always start with the question "why".
- Time management: 1. plan ahead, 2. choose the most important tasks and see them through to the end, 3. achieve success step by step.

## 2. Motivational myths
- Specific goals increase the level of motivation.
- Identity consists of three elements: 1. the image of the ideal person you would like to be, 2. your self-image, 3. self-esteem.
- Pillars of mental state: 1. I am responsible, 2. I like myself.
- Statistically, successful people have far more failures than the average person.
- An important question: what do I expect from life?
- Motivation is not permanent – it is like a muscle and can get tired.
- Choose a goal that matters to you and put effort into achieving it, no matter how long it takes.
- 80% of a goal will be achieved in 20% of the time.

## 3. Unlocking the drive for success
- People are a bit like horses with blinders – we see nothing beyond what our beliefs allow us to see.
- We all have some false beliefs that hold us back.
- If you want to be confident, act as if you are confident – action will trigger the emotion.
- The principle of causality: if you want to achieve something, see what others who have achieved it do and do the same.
- The principle of beliefs: what you deeply believe becomes reality.
- The principle of expectations: what you confidently expect becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy. Types of expectations: parents' expectations, a superior's expectations, other people's expectations, and your own expectations of yourself.
- You become whoever you most often imagine yourself to be in your thoughts.
- The only way to become a good public speaker is practice – nothing else can replace it.
- Everyone has negative experiences that have influenced their beliefs. Most people can identify them on their own, without the help of a psychologist.

## 4. From setting a goal to achieving a goal
- Learned helplessness: because people have often failed in the past, they also expect failure in the future.
- Do not confuse a goal with a wish – a wish is a goal without an action plan.
- Goals are like a road you travel; they provide a sense of direction and clarity.
- Our aim should not be to look for what is distant and unclear, but to do what is within our reach.
- Only action is action – not talking, not wishing, not hoping, not intending.
- Seven steps of goal-setting: 1. decide what you want – specifically, 2. write down your goal, 3. set a deadline, 4. make a list, 5. develop a plan, 6. take the first step, 7. pursue your goal.
- Write down 10 goals you would like to achieve within 12 months.
- Goals should be personal, positive, and expressed in the present tense (e.g., "I achieve", "I lead").

## 5. Proper action and flexibility
- There is nothing more important than taking action that leads to achieving your goals. This results in: 1. immediate feedback, 2. new ideas emerging, 3. increased self-confidence.
- The cycle of action: you start with an idea, act, fail, draw conclusions, and try again.
- The foundation of action is an unshakeable belief that success will be achieved sooner or later, and that failure is temporary.
- Reflecting on your value system is truly important – it is the essence of who you are.
- Your freedom of choice is defined by well-thought-out and prepared options.
- The goal should be to reach the top 10% of specialists in your field.
- Reaching the top 10% takes 5 to 7 years – the same amount of time it takes to transform a company into the best.
- Usefulness lies in simplicity. Sometimes the best thing you can do is to stop doing something.
- Re-engineering: reducing the number of steps by 30% – you can eliminate steps, merge 2 or 3 into 1, remove a process or part of it, or outsource a task.
- Incorrect assumptions lie at the root of all failures.

## 6. The importance of daily self-talk
- 95% of your emotions are determined by how you talk to yourself.
- The natural state of the human mind is negative self-perception and thinking about the worst.
- Stages of self-conditioning: 1. idealization – imagining what the ideal should look like; 2. visualization – imagining and creating a mental image of success; 3. verbalization – powerful words activate your mind; 4. realization – taking actions aligned with achieving the goal; 5. execution – at the right moment and in the right way, the goal will be achieved.

## 7. Long-term thinking
- The urgency factor: people have an innate tendency to achieve what they desire as quickly and easily as possible, without regard for long-term consequences.
- Research shows that if you want to succeed, think and plan your goals with reference to the future.
- Deal with the present while thinking about long-term goals.
- The marshmallow test: children raised in a safe environment were more likely to wait for the second marshmallow. When we feel safe, we can defer gratification; when we feel unsafe, we want it immediately.
- It is worth creating a reward system for taking positive action – give yourself a reward after completing each step.
- Two factors primarily disrupt proper brain function today: inattention (distraction by electronic devices) and multitasking (performing several tasks at the same time).
- With children, it is good to praise them for completing any task, even a very small one.
- Time management using a four-quadrant matrix: 1. immediate action quadrant (important and urgent), 2. effectiveness quadrant (important, not urgent), 3. illusion quadrant (urgent, not important), 4. wasted time quadrant (not important and not urgent).

## 8. Key factors of resilience
- You need to prepare your subconscious for countless mistakes, failures, and challenges.
- As a result, when you encounter an obstacle, the shock and disappointment will last a relatively short time and you will get back on your feet quickly.
- The times-two and times-three rule: if you are planning something, it will cost twice as much money and take three times as long (or vice versa).
- Proof of concept: showing the first version of a product or service to a potential customer and making many changes to ensure the customer would buy it.
- Sales is a game of failure – you get far more failures than successes.
- When a crisis occurs: stay calm, gather the facts, and ask questions.
- Staying calm allows the cerebral cortex to function; when you lose your composure, the primitive part of the brain responsible for fight or flight takes over.
- In personal life, laughter is the most important thing; conversations also matter.
- Most wealthy people have gone through bankruptcy or came close to it.
- The three-percent rule: if the probability of an event is 3%, you should prepare for it.

## 9. Motivating others
- In companies that have achieved success, employees at every level of the organization know clearly defined goals and intentions.
- The second characteristic was clearly defined indicators, standards, and criteria.
- The third characteristic was schedules and deadlines for individual tasks.
- The fourth factor was high rewards for exceptional achievements.
- At General Electric there were four performance levels: average, excellent (manager received 10–20% extra pay), "wow" (double salary), and "double wow" (triple salary).
- Principles of a good company: 1. shared values (everyone should know 1–3 company values), 2. shared goals and intentions, 3. shared action plans, 4. continuous review of results.
- Managers should lead their teams by example.
- The law of three factors: if you could do only three activities throughout your working day, what would they be?
- Three words define the need of a satisfied and motivated employee: clarity, clarity, clarity.
- The best management style is benevolent dictatorship – the manager sets goals and tasks, but "please" and "thank you" are not foreign to them.
- Two traits of the best superiors: clarity and understanding.
- Very few people are born leaders – most learn to become them.

## 10. The power of rituals

- The more routine you have in life, the more time you have to think and focus on what is important and interesting.
- Franklin: early to bed and early to rise makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise.
- Eat your last meal three hours before going to sleep.
- It is a good idea to write a to-do list for the next day before going to bed.
- It is beneficial to exercise in the morning after waking up; the recommended amount is 200–300 minutes per week.
- Regular exercise creates a dependence on endorphins — a good kind of dependence.
- The first hour of the day sets the course for the entire day; it is best to start with positive input rather than the news.
- Breakfast is the most important meal of the day.
- Dedicate two hours a day to learning new skills.
- Neither possessions nor accolades bring happiness.
- 80% of happiness comes from relationships with other people.
