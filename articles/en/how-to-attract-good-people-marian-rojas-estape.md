# Content

## 1. The cuddle hormone

- Oxytocin: a hormone responsible for childbirth and lactation; it makes us more generous and helpful.
- Cortisol: the stress hormone, released primarily in moments of tension and threat; excess cortisol is harmful.
- When oxytocin levels rise, cortisol levels fall.
- Two powerful tools for keeping impulses in check: willpower and intelligence.
- Freedom is the jewel in the crown of human behaviour; a free person will go further than a merely intelligent one.
- Foundations: (1) the human being is a social creature, (2) society requires solidarity.

## 2. Emotional bonds

- The autonomic nervous system governs the functioning of the heart, lungs, etc.
- Sympathetic nervous system: activated during threat, associated with cortisol.
- Parasympathetic nervous system: activated during relaxation; governed by the vagus nerve.
- Proper nutrition, breathing, and heart-rate control allow us to manage emotions, thoughts, and intuitions.

## 3. Empathy works like a vitamin

- Empathy is the ability to put oneself in another person's situation.
- Empathy releases oxytocin and opens us up to others.
- Other hormones: cortisol (stress), dopamine (pleasure and reward), serotonin (sense of happiness, well-being, and libido).
- The digital world keeps us in a state of constant stimulation; the mind cannot distinguish real threats from abstract ones.
- An excess of stimuli requires ever stronger stimuli to prompt action, which reduces our capacity for empathy.

## 4. Men: high testosterone levels, low oxytocin levels

- Oxytocin affects sexual pleasure: it rises fivefold in men and even more in women.
- Testosterone and oxytocin work in opposition.
- In mature men testosterone declines and they begin to devote more attention to their children.

## 5. And what happens with women?

- In women, peak testosterone levels occur during ovulation.
- Between 10 and 20% of women develop postnatal depression.
- The first 1,000 minutes of a child's life are crucial; the child should be with its mother.
- Infants recognise their mother's scent and voice.

## 6. Touch is essential for survival

- Touch is perceived by Vater-Pacini corpuscles.
- Holt's book discouraged picking babies up, which had a very negative effect on child mortality, especially in orphanages.

## 7. Oxytocin never stops surprising us

- Neoteny: the process of retaining childlike features into adulthood (e.g. Mickey Mouse).
- The sight of childlike features triggers feelings of calm and tenderness, induced by oxytocin.
- One should act with passion and hope.
- Being around animals increases oxytocin levels.
- The ability to express feelings through the body and hugs helps improve communication.
- Oxytocin is raised by: listening to music, massage, contact with animals, play, meditation, prayer, and mindfulness.

## 8. A person should not be alone

- Loneliness is an imbalance between what we have and what we would like to have.
- A likeable person is one who makes themselves easy to like.
- Helping others is a remedy for loneliness.

## 9. What is attachment?

- Attachment is an emotional bond built between two people, whose primary purpose is to seek and maintain closeness.
- A child's contact with caregivers shapes its later attachment style; friendships play an important role afterwards.
- Attachment can be changed later in life.
- Trauma: when it appears, the hippocampus and amygdala fill with cortisol and the person remembers nothing.

## 10. Do we love the way we were loved?

- Emotional foundations: what we take away from our home.
- Three possible paths after experiencing hurt: (1) suffer, neither heal nor overcome it; (2) suffer, overcome it, and work through it maturely; (3) suffer, remain unaware of the wound, and let it drive you.

## 11. The confusion of upbringing

- Parents should earn their child's respect in order to become its "safe harbour", and then be able to listen to it; attention and patience are essential.
- To achieve autonomy, a child must first be dependent on its parents — that is the pattern.
- Everything that happens in the first two years of life is stored in emotional memory, in the right hemisphere of the brain.
- One should attend to a child when it cries, not ignore it.
- Excessive care can undermine a child's sense of security — it will not learn to manage fear.
- Needs concern what is essential for survival and the maintenance of physical and mental health.
- A wish is something additional that brings pleasure and gratification but is not essential for survival.

## 12. Very harmful treatment

- Three attachment styles: secure (emotions are present), avoidant (absence of emotions), ambivalent (overly intense emotions).
- Resilience: the ability to get back up and start again after encountering difficulty.
- Two pillars of attachment: independence and bond — they should remain in balance.
- In the anxious-ambivalent style the bond predominates; in the avoidant style independence predominates.
- Disorganised attachment style: alternately avoidant and ambivalent.

## 13. How to heal scarred wounds

- Managing emotions is one of the keys to joy in life: keeping control in moments of stress, anger, sadness, or frustration.
- Happiness: healthy anchoring in the present, rising above wounds from the past, looking towards the future with hope.
- The EMDR technique allows one to gain mastery over the past.
- Happiness is not what happens to us but how we interpret it; interpretation depends on: our belief system, our mental state, and our ability to filter information.
- The sequence: perception → feeling → thinking → action.
- The "recorder" from childhood becomes the inner voice in adulthood: 50% is shaped by age 6, 25% between ages 6 and 12, 25% later.
- If you feed a child large doses of love, its fears will die of hunger.

## 14. Pleasure

- To live well with someone else, you must first live well with yourself.
- Three types of sexual relationships: sex out of love (the first months of falling in love), sex without love, sex with love and commitment (a long-term relationship).

## 15. Let's talk about love

- Love at first sight happens most often to attractive people and more often to men than to women.
- The dazzle, attraction, and falling-in-love phase lasts on average 17 months; it is worth waiting at least 1.5 years before deciding to marry.
- Three types of love: sexual desire, romantic love (focus on one object), attachment (passion fades, warmth and care grow).

## 16. A good choice is success

- Four stages of choosing a partner: sparks, the head, love is born, the will to last.

## 17. The pyramid theory

- Partner-selection criteria: I – basic (a threshold below which we do not go), II – additional.
- The ARAS system: when we strongly desire something, the brain filters information and seeks it out.
- Passion is putting your heart into what you do.
- A sense of humour: an attitude to life, the ability to find something amusing everywhere.

## 18. How to increase the chances of a good relationship

- Important issues to discuss: children, fidelity, money, in-laws and relatives.
- Features of a good relationship: physical attraction, psychological attraction, appreciation.
- Three types of relationships: those that work on their own, those that do not work, and those that require work and effort.
- Taking care of a relationship: develop communication, examine your sensitivity, be careful with imagination, pay attention to small things.

## 19. Highly sensitive personality

- A highly sensitive person (HSP) perceives stimuli far more intensely; this is an advantage, but one must learn to manage it.

## 20. What does it mean to be a toxic person?

- A toxic person is one who causes cortisol to be released in us.
- The coping scheme: know yourself → understand yourself → accept/understand the problem → forgive.
- Two types of toxic people: universal and personal.

## 21. Classification of difficult people

- Universal toxic types include the egoist and the pessimist, among others.
- The higher you are on the emotional vector, the less toxic people affect you.
- A crisis is an opportunity for growth and emerging stronger.

## Vitamin people

- People who love deeply and are deeply loved sometimes recover in a remarkable way.
- Characteristics of a vitamin person: raises your self-esteem, supports and motivates you, finds the best in you, tries to ease your suffering, tries to understand rather than judge, gives joy and a will to live, is capable of gratitude and shows it, disperses negative emotions and strengthens positive ones, has a sense of humour.
