# Content

## Prolog
- ALDI (Albrecht Discount) was founded by the Albrecht brothers.
- In 2023 the last nuclear power plants were shut down.
- Germany was dependent on Russia for gas and on China for exports.
- In a modern economy services matter more than manufacturing.
- Germany supports established companies but not startups.
- Large companies cope with bureaucracy; small ones do not.

## 1. The canary
- Neo-mercantilism: an economy oriented toward surpluses in physical goods.
- State-owned banks were fully protected from bankruptcy and remained under political control.
- Collusion between politicians, bankers and industrialists.
- WestLB invested in risky assets in the USA and collapsed in 2008.
- After state guarantees were withdrawn, many banks failed due to a lack of competence.
- Many banks operated cum-ex schemes: by trading large share packages on dividend payment days they claimed tax refunds, effectively defrauding the state.
- The number of state banks fell from 11 to 5; savings banks and cooperative banks took over.
- Germany's population accounts for 24% of the EU's population.
- Germany lacks a venture-capital sector and investment in research.
- The banking sector's difficulties preceded those of industry by a decade.

## 2. Terra incognita
- Germans invented the printing press and the automobile, yet Germany is disconnected from everything digital.
- Schools lag behind in digitalisation.
- In 1982 Kohl, a proponent of analogue technology, rejected the digital path.
- Only 10% of internet connections in Germany use fibre optic cables.
- The lack of digital infrastructure contributes to, among other things, the slow uptake of electric vehicles.
- There is a lack of coordination between the federal government and the 16 federal states; the division of responsibilities is unclear.
- Cars account for 20% of German exports; the automotive industry has a decisive influence on politics.
- Trade union representatives hold 50% of seats on supervisory boards.
- Green energy could offer a way forward but faces bureaucratic obstacles.
- In the USA the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) strengthens the competitiveness of American companies.

## 3. Energy shortfall
- Schröder won an election by saving a steelworks and surrounded himself with a circle of friends.
- Schröder was a populist; he disliked Berlusconi, another populist.
- After the Fukushima disaster Germany abandoned nuclear energy.
- Dependence on Russian gas rose from 30% to 50%.
- After the annexation of Crimea there was a shift in policy, yet 70% of companies continued doing business with Russia.

## 4. The China syndrome
- The just-in-time method was invented but revealed its weaknesses during the pandemic.
- China chose Duisburg as the European city of the Belt and Road Initiative – freight trains from China arrived there.
- After the pandemic priorities shifted from supply-chain optimisation to security.

## 5. Releasing the brake
- Germany does not even debate abandoning its identity as an industrial nation.
- Germany avoided taking on debt, which resulted in a lack of investment.

## 6. Us and the rest of the world
- Germany struggles to attract highly skilled workers.
- Soon the number of people leaving the labour market will exceed the number of new entrants by 400,000.
- Extensive bureaucratic procedures are a major problem.

## Epilogue
- When there are no credible opposition parties, angry voters turn to extremists; far-right and far-left parties together obtained 25%.
- The heating law, which required the installation of expensive heat pumps, proved to be a government failure.
- A court ruled that funds from the Covid relief fund could not be redirected to climate goals, which led to the collapse of the coalition.
- The right wanted profits from industry, the left wanted jobs from industry, and the Greens wanted green industry.
