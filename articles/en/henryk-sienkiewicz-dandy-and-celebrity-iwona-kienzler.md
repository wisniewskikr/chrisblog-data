# Content

## I. In the family home
- His mother, Stefania Cieciszowska, came from wealthy nobility; his father, Józef Sienkiewicz, was handsome, and the family traced its roots to Lithuanian Tatars.
- His parents' marriage was a misalliance.
- Henryk was short and was born in Wola Okrzejska; the Sienkiewicz family lived in Wężyczyn.
- His mother published the novel "Jedynaczka" in the magazine "Bazar".
- He had a brother, Kazimierz, and four sisters.
- At the age of 12 he began his studies in Warsaw; the family moved to Warsaw and bought a tenement house.
- Henryk was not accepted into the uprising; his brother Kazimierz was accepted but was never heard from again.
- He worked as a private tutor for the Wyyher family.
- He passed his school-leaving exams with good grades in Polish, history and geography.
- He enrolled successively in law, medicine and finally history; he failed Greek.
- He published his first review in "Przegląd Tygodniowy" and his first novel "Na marne".

## 2. A journalist in love
- He began working as a theatre critic at "Niwa".
- He travelled to Vienna, from where he sent columns.
- His mother died.
- He fell in love with Maria Keller, but her parents forced her to break off the engagement — they disapproved of his columns and his financial situation.

## 3. Mr Henryk in America
- He fell under the spell of the actress Modrzejewska.
- He travelled with Modrzejewska and a group of about 20 people to write columns.
- Sienkiewicz chose the location for the commune.
- His "Letters from America" saved the newspaper.

## 4. Travels and romances
- He returned to Europe, but spent almost a year in Paris to avoid military conscription.
- He returned to Poland.
- In Szczawnica he noticed Maria Szetkiewiczówna with her sister and father.
- He followed her to Venice.
- He wrote reportages and short stories.

## 5. A short chronicle of marital happiness
- Henryk became editor of "Słowo".
- His son Henryk Józef Sienkiewicz was born.
- He began writing the Trilogy and publishing it in "Słowo".
- His daughter Jadwiga was born.
- They went to San Remo because his wife Maria was ill, and later to the spa town of Reichenhall.
- After four years of marriage, Maria died.

## 6. The possessive sister-in-law and the unwanted fiancée
- He travelled around Europe constantly.
- Intensive work on "The Deluge" protected him from depression.
- He made a journey to Turkey via Greece and Rome.
- His works were being translated, but the writer received nothing from it.
- He proposed to his cousin Marynia Babska.
- He broke off the engagement under the influence of his sister-in-law.

## 7. In Africa
- Henryk spent 40 days in Spain.
- He then set off for Africa, visiting Zanzibar among other places.
- After 2–3 weeks he fell ill with fever.

## 8. Marynuszka – a marital catastrophe in lilac
- Maria Wołodkowicz was the adopted daughter of wealthy parents and was selfish.
- He proposed to her in Odessa; he was 27 years her senior, and her mother pressed for the marriage.
- The wedding took place in Kraków.
- They divorced because Sienkiewicz had not published the banns.

## 9. The last great love – Maria Radziejewska
- He wrote "The Połaniecki Family" and "Quo Vadis".
- He travelled constantly; he went to Nice, among other places, where his son was receiving treatment.
- He began writing "The Teutonic Knights".
- "Quo Vadis" was adapted into a film.
- He fell in love with Maria Radziejewska.
- He was given the estate of Oblęgorek.

## 10. Maria Babska – a companion in the autumn of life
- He married Maria Babska.

## 11. The Nobel laureate
- Young writers campaigned against Sienkiewicz.
- In 1905 he received the Nobel Prize.

## 12. A turbulent decade
- He wrote "Whirlpools", "In Desert and Wilderness" and "Legions".
- World War I broke out; he went to Switzerland.
- He died in Switzerland, and his body was brought to Warsaw.
