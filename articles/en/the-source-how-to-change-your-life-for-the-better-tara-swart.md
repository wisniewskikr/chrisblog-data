# Content

## I. Science and spirituality

### 1. The law of attraction

- Space of intention: intuition, emotions and rational thoughts align.
- Principles of the law of attraction:
  1. **Abundance**: there are many opportunities – one must be unafraid and reach for them; this is an optimistic attitude.
  2. **Manifestation**: imagining the outcome, but not waiting passively; it works through "selective attention" (knowing what interests us and consciously focusing on it) and "assigning meaning" to important things.
  3. **Magnetic desire**: a mental attitude that we can make something good happen.
  4. **Patience**.
  5. **Harmony**: body, mind and emotions must work together; two ways are listening to the body's signals (interoception) and paying attention to what the gut tells us (intuition).
  6. **We are all connected**: Gandhi – "Be the change you wish to see in the world"; we are a composite of the 5 people we spend the most time with.

## 2. Visualisation

- Imagining something can bring great results – the brain treats the situation not as new but as already familiar, making it easier to handle.
- Visualisation should engage all senses: sight, hearing, smell, touch, feeling.
- Research on finger curling: physical exercise gave a 53% increase, mental visualisation 35%, no exercise 0%.
- Two brain networks:
  - **Default**: abstract thinking, without details; active when we are relaxed.
  - **Control**: task focus and analytical thinking.

## II. The plastic brain

### 3. The amazing brain: the genesis of the Source

- Phineas Gage, after damage to his frontal lobe, was unable to plan or suppress impulses.
- The brain consists of the cerebral cortex, the brain stem and the cerebellum.
- Lobe division: frontal (reasoning, planning, short-term memory), occipital (vision), temporal (hearing, smell, taste), parietal (hearing, smell, touch).
- Brain development: in the first weeks it grows approx. 1% per day; within the first 3 months it enlarges by approx. 64% – from 1/3 to more than 1/2 of the size of an adult brain.
- An optimal brain: different viewpoints, new solutions and new responses.

## 4. The plastic mind: how to change neural pathways

- The brain seeks minimum effort, but we can obstruct this when needed.
- The brain can compensate for the lack of one element by developing another.
- Mechanisms of neuroplasticity: learning (new synapses), refinement (speeding up neuronal activity) and re-education.

## III. The agile brain

### 5. Brain agility: how to switch nimbly between different types of thinking

- Six modes of thinking:
  - Emotional intelligence: managing emotions.
  - Physicality and interoception: knowing oneself.
  - Gut feeling and intuition.
  - Motivation.
  - Logic.
  - Creativity.

## 6. Emotions: master your feelings

- Types of emotions: fear, anger, disgust, jealousy, sadness, surprise, joy/excitement, love/trust.
- Sensitivity is the influence of one's own feelings on oneself; emotional intelligence is the ability to influence the feelings of others.
- Meditation helps recognise one's own emotions – we have influence over them.

## 7. Physicality: know yourself

- Interoception is reading signals from the body, e.g. hunger, thirst, dehydration.

## 8. Intuition: trust your gut feelings

## 9. Motivation: maintain resilience to achieve your goals

- Primary motivation: sleep/wakefulness, hunger/thirst, reproduction.
- Positive motivation: helping others, intellectual challenges, financial success, introducing innovation.
- Negative motivation: fear, revenge, anger, addictions.
- Motivation – not giving up; resilience – the ability to get back up.
- Steve Jobs: if you follow your passion, you will succeed.
- It is worth asking: what do I truly want from life and why?
- When facing problems, helpful questions: "How important will this be in 5 years?" or "What advice would you give to a sibling?"

## 10. Logic: make good decisions

- One should limit the number of unnecessary decisions made each day – each consumes energy; routine is therefore helpful.

## 11. Creativity: design the ideal future

- Creativity is the ability to imagine the future.
- One should frequently think about one's desires, dreams and hopes, and give the brain time and space to process them.

## IV. Ignite the Source

- Four stages:
  1. Heightened awareness.
  2. Action map.
  3. Focused attention.

## 12. Heightened awareness – switch off the autopilot

- For the brain, the difference between what actually happened and what we merely imagined is very small.
- In childhood, "models" of what family, love and the self mean to us are formed; the brain then looks for them in the real world – it is worth checking whether they have become distorted.
- The only mistakes are those we do not learn from.
