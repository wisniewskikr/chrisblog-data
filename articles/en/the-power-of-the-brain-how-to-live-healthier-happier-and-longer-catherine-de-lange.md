# Content

## I. Diet

- The brain consumes 20% of the body's energy.

### 1. What to eat to improve your mood

- The gut–brain axis operates through the vagus nerve, hormones, and other channels.
- The vagus nerve is sometimes called the sixth sense – it detects organ activity and relays that information to the brain.
- Both good and bad bacteria live in the gut.
- To take care of the gut flora, one should eat a varied, plant-based diet; currently 75% of food comes from only 12 plants.

## 2. Hunger helps extend the brain's youth

- An appropriate diet can contribute to a longer life.
- Ketosis is a state in which the body draws energy from fat instead of sugar.
- Types of intermittent fasting: 5:2 (eat for five days, fast for two), OMAD (one meal a day), 8:16 (eat for 8 hours, fast for 16).

## 3. Promising foods

- Diet can influence depression.
- The recommended ratio of omega-3 to omega-6 fatty acids is 1:2.
- Iodised salt should be chosen.
- Red wine and dark chocolate may also be beneficial.

## 4. Alzheimer's disease may be a diabetes of the brain

- In type II diabetes the pancreas fails to process the insulin signal effectively – after eating something sweet, the body cannot properly convert sugar into energy.

## II. Sleep

### 5. Sleep on a problem and you will find a solution more easily

- Sleep phases: REM (Rapid Eye Movement) and NREM; each lasts approximately 90 minutes.
- Memories are transferred from the hippocampus to the neocortex – much like files moved from a USB drive to a hard drive.

## 6. The whole truth about sleep and Alzheimer's disease

- Sleep disturbances lead to the accumulation of amyloid, which in turn disrupts sleep and causes forgetfulness.
- During sleep, glial cells cleanse the brain of toxins.

## 7. Fatigue impairs cognitive abilities

- Caffeine boosts energy, but its effect is an illusion.
- Fatigue can be measured by testing the pupil response.
- Sleep deprivation impoverishes us morally.

## 8. Sleep is overnight therapy

- Lack of sleep amplifies negative emotions.

## 9. How much sleep do we need?

- Between 7 and 9 hours of sleep is recommended.
- A nap should last 20 minutes and take place at least 6 hours before bedtime.

## 10. Fatigue can be a state of mind

- People have difficulty accurately assessing the quality of their own sleep.
- Sleep inertia persists for approximately 30 minutes after waking.

## III. Physical activity

- One quarter of adults and 80% of teenagers are physically inactive.

### 11. Movement as one of the keys to preventing dementia

- Exercise is a small effort now but a huge dividend in the future.
- It is estimated that 40% of dementia cases could be prevented through diet and exercise.

## 12. Exercise gets the brain moving

- The effectiveness of exercise depends on its intensity, duration, and frequency.

## 13. Physical exercise improves wellbeing

- Exercise has a positive effect on mood.
- The optimal workout length is approximately 45 minutes.
- Exercising about 5 times a week is recommended.
- Team sports produce the best results.

## 14. Yoga can change the brain and calm the mind

- Yoga reduces stress.

## 15. Be careful with mindfulness

- Mindfulness involves receiving stimuli without interpreting or judging them.
- The Headspace app is an example of a mindfulness tool.
- In approximately 40% of people, mindfulness can actually worsen mood.

## IV. Mental exercises

### 16. You can increase the number of cells in your brain

- Changing one's environment stimulates brain development.
- Over a lifetime we lose between 2 and 4% of our brain mass.

## 17. Learning protects the brain

- Learning acts like a savings account – it protects the brain in later life.
- Well-educated people have a 47% lower risk of dementia.
- We inherit only half of our IQ; the other half we develop ourselves.

## 18. Bilingualism develops the brain

- Bilingualism develops the brain by forcing it to exercise constant language control.

## 19. Music should be part of life

- Learning music has a positive effect on the brain.

## V. Contacts with people

### 20. Marriage protects against dementia

- Marriage is good for the brain.
- Having one or two children is beneficial for the brain.

## 21. How loneliness changes the brain

- Being alone and feeling lonely are two different states.
- Loneliness is associated with inflammation and a sense of threat.

## 22. The healing power of animals

- Owning pets is good for the brain.

## 23. The beneficial effects of light and darkness on mood

- Blue light is beneficial in the morning; red light is beneficial in the evening.

## 24. Contact with nature has a positive effect on the psyche

- Nature allows involuntary attention to rest.
- The optimal time spent in nature is between 2 and 5 hours.
- Contact with water is more beneficial than time spent in a forest.

## VI. A healthy mind in a healthy body

### 25. Inflammation muddles the mind

- Inflammation responds to every event occurring in the body.
- Stress and obesity contribute to its intensification.
- Inflammation is an indicator of biological age.

## 26. Take care of your teeth to avoid Alzheimer's disease

- There is a hypothesis that Alzheimer's disease may result from a gum infection.

## 27. Hearing loss is linked to dementia

- Mild hearing loss doubles the risk of dementia.
- A hearing aid should be worn when needed.

## VII. Your own influence

### 28. Character affects mental health

- Genetics definitely influences personality.
- Five personality traits are distinguished: openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion (drawing energy from being with others), agreeableness, and neuroticism (higher levels of anger and fear).
- Perfectionism also comes into play alongside these traits.

## 29. Stress doesn't have to be bad

- Amusement eliminates stress.
- Stress is meant to save lives, not shorten them.
- One's attitude towards stress is crucial.
- Practical steps: first – name the stress (e.g. "I am stressed because I won't finish the book on time"); second – consciously change one's attitude.

## 30. How to adopt healthy habits?

- The striatum converts actions into habits.
- It is worth using defensive pessimism as motivation – imagine what will happen if you give up a given behaviour.
- Writing down a bad habit at the moment it occurs hands control back to the thinking part of the brain.
