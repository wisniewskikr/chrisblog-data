# Content

## 1. Decision theory

- People make decisions guided not by logic, but by psycho-logic.
- There are two decision-making systems.

### System 1: Intuitive

- Processes 11 million bits of information.
- Cocktail party effect: when someone at a party says our name, we immediately pick it up.
- Association: neurons that fire together, wire together.
- After 10,000 such connections we act intuitively (e.g. reading an ECG like an expert).
- Framing: the background gives meaning to a decision or perception; brands are frames.

### System 2: Explicit

- Processes only 40–50 bits (7 ± 2 items of information).

## 2. The moment of truth

- A purchase activates the brain's reward area.
- Price activates the same brain area as pain — price hurts.
- Net value = reward − pain.
- There are two net values: explicit and implicit.
- The perceived reward is influenced by: price (in luxury goods), packaging, and language.
- The felt pain of price is influenced by: the anchoring mechanism (which price you start from) and the order of presentation (better to start with more expensive options).
- Cost is not only money, but also time.
- We choose things based on relative value (comparing with similar things), not absolute value.
- Value and cost can also change depending on context.

## 3. Decoding the interface

- Humans see only a small part of their visual field sharply; the rest is blurred.
- We recognise things through diagnostic values — the most important, characteristic features.
