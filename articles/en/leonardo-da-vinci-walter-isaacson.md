# Content

## Introduction

- Leonardo was a left-handed vegetarian, a homosexual, and an illegitimate child.
- He was extraordinarily curious about the world.
- He painted two masterpieces: *Mona Lisa* and *The Last Supper*.

## Chapter 1. Childhood

- Leonardo's father's family had been notaries for generations; Leonardo himself would have become one had he not been born out of wedlock.
- His father, Piero da Vinci, was a notary in Florence; during a visit to his hometown of Vinci he had an affair with an unmarried peasant girl.
- His mother, Caterina Lippi, was a 16-year-old orphan.
- Leonardo was born in 1452.
- Eight months after Leonardo's birth, Piero married Albiera, the 16-year-old daughter of a wealthy shoemaker from Florence; they had no children. His third and fourth wives bore him 11 children.
- His mother married a farmer, Antonio di Piero del Vaccha; they had four daughters and a son.
- From the age of 5, Leonardo was raised mainly by his grandfather Antonio; also living there was his father's younger brother, Francesco, only 15 years older than Leonardo.
- As an illegitimate son, Leonardo was suspended between two worlds, which forced him to be more creative.
- He had a taste of arithmetic at an abacus school; the rest he taught himself. The invention of the printing press made books accessible to him, and the era was favorable — 40 years of peace in Italy.

## Chapter 2. Apprentice

- When Leonardo was 12, his grandfather and stepmother died; his father brought him to Florence, a city combining art and technology — the textile, silk, and banking industries.
- Leonardo was left-handed; to avoid smearing ink, he wrote in mirror script — from right to left, with reversed letters.
- He apprenticed with Verrocchio, a client of his father. The workshop produced, among other things, artistic spectacles for the Medici — the informal rulers of Florence.
- One of Leonardo's earliest works is a painting on a shield.
- At the age of 20 he finished his apprenticeship and joined the painters' guild. Together with his master he created *Tobias and the Angel* and *The Baptism of Christ*.
- Works from this period created on his own: *The Annunciation*, *Madonna with a Carnation*, *Benois Madonna*, and the *Portrait of Ginevra* — the first three-quarter portrait in Italy that shows the subject's eyes.

## Chapter 3. Independence

- At the age of 24, Leonardo was accused of using the services of a male prostitute.
- Working independently, he created, among others, *The Adoration of the Magi* — considered the most significant of all unfinished works in history — and *Saint Jerome in the Wilderness*.

## Chapter 4. Milan

- At the age of 30, Leonardo left Florence and moved to Milan, where he spent the next 17 years; the city was ruled by the Sforzas.
- Leonardo invented the wheel lock mechanism for firearms.

## Chapter 5. Leonardo's notebooks

- Leonardo kept notebooks throughout his entire life.

## Chapter 6. Court artist

- At the Sforza court, Leonardo was mainly occupied with creating stage sets; he loved it because it combined his passions as an artist and engineer, and the work had to be delivered on time.

## Chapter 7. Personal life

- Giacomo Caprotti, known as Salaì (Little Devil), came to live with Leonardo — a 10-year-old son of a poor peasant from near Milan.
- He was a thief, a liar, stubborn, and gluttonous, yet very beautiful; over time he became Leonardo's secretary and later his lover.

### Chapter 8. Vitruvian Man

- Vitruvius was a soldier of Caesar; after military service he became an architect and wrote a treatise on architecture in which he described the ideal proportions of a building and of a human being.
- Leonardo illustrated the man described by Vitruvius: the navel is the center of the circle, the penis the center of the square.

## Chapter 9. The equestrian statue

- Leonardo received a commission for an equestrian statue of Sforza's father, becoming the official artist of the Sforza court with a salary, quarters, and a vineyard.
- The bronze intended for the statue was used for military purposes instead.

## Chapter 10. The scholar

- Quote on the superiority of experiment over theory alone: "Whoever can go directly to the source will not drink from the jug."
- Knowledge is the result of a dialogue between empirical observation and theory.
- Deductive reasoning: you have a theory and use it to predict an outcome.
- Inductive reasoning: you have an experimental result and use it to build a theory.

## Chapter 11. Birds and flight

- Leonardo was fascinated by birds and flight throughout his entire life.

## Chapter 12. The art of mechanics

- Leonardo was the first to recognize that creating a perpetual motion machine is impossible — due to friction on land, water resistance, and air resistance.
- He also discovered bearings.

## Chapter 13. Mathematics

- Leonardo loved geometry but not arithmetic.

## Chapter 14. The nature of man

- Leonardo performed autopsies — initially to paint better, later purely out of curiosity.

## Chapter 15. Virgin of the Rocks

- Leonardo painted *Virgin of the Rocks*, depicting the meeting of the infant Jesus with the infant John the Baptist.
- Two versions exist: the original and a copy he helped produce.

## Chapter 16. Milanese portraits

- *Portrait of a Musician* — the only male portrait by Leonardo.
- *Lady with an Ermine* — Cecilia Gallerani, Sforza's mistress, whom he later married off to a count.
- *La Belle Ferronière* — Lucrezia Crivelli, Sforza's mistress and a lady-in-waiting to his wife.
- *La Bella Principessa* — Bianca Sforza, the duke's illegitimate daughter; the painting was probably part of a book currently held in Warsaw.

## Chapter 17. The science of art

- Leonardo frequently took part in debates known as Paragone, covering various disciplines.
- He combined observational talent with imagination.
- He valued shadows, sfumato (blurred outlines), and perspective (with distance, objects lose size, color, and sharpness of outline).

## Chapter 18. The Last Supper

- *The Last Supper* was painted in a monastery in Milan.

## Chapter 19. Personal troubles

- Leonardo's mother came to Milan and died there.

## Chapter 20. Back in Florence

- Sforza was forced to flee Milan before the French; Leonardo first stayed, then moved to Florence.

## Chapter 21. The Virgin and Child with Saint Anne

- The painting was commissioned by the monks with whom Leonardo stayed after returning to Florence.
- It depicts Saint Anne, the Virgin Mary, and Jesus playing with a lamb.

## Chapter 22. Lost and found paintings

- *Leda and the Swan* — a painting on a mythological theme: Zeus, in the form of a swan, seduced the princess Leda.
- *Salvator Mundi* — a painting of Jesus; bought for $100, later sold for $80 million, then for $120 million.

## Chapter 23. Cesare Borgia

- Cesare's father was pope; Cesare became a bishop at 15 and a cardinal at 18, then renounced the title to become commander of the papal armies.
- Machiavelli, the modest son of a Florentine lawyer, met with Borgia to discuss surrender terms; Florence sent Leonardo to serve Borgia.

## Chapter 24. Hydraulic engineer

- Pisa rebelled against Florence; Leonardo was sent to divert the course of the Arno River, but an engineer ruined his plans.
- Leonardo also proposed a network of canals to give Florence access to the sea; the project was abandoned for lack of funds.
- He also planned to drain the marshes at Piombino.

## Chapter 25. Michelangelo and lost battles

- Leonardo received a commission for a fresco, *The Battle of Anghiari*, in the council hall of Florence.
- Michelangelo was painting a different battle scene in the same hall.
- Leonardo abandoned his work, partly due to technical problems with the ground layer.

## Chapter 26. Return to Milan

- Leonardo's father died, leaving him nothing in his will.
- King Louis XII summoned Leonardo to Milan.
- Leonardo adopted Francesco Melzi.
- Leonardo's uncle died and bequeathed him an estate near Vinci; his half-siblings contested this and brought a lawsuit against him.

## Chapter 27. Anatomy, the second approach

- Leonardo dissected the body of a 100-year-old man.
- He performed autopsies together with Marcantonio, a young professor from Pavia; had Marcantonio not died in a plague, they would likely have published an outstanding anatomy textbook together.

## Chapter 28. The world and its waters

- Leonardo discovered that springs arise through the circulation of water on Earth — water evaporates and then falls as rain.
- The sky is blue because of moisture in the air through which light passes; the higher one goes, the darker the sky becomes, until it turns black.

## Chapter 29. Rome

- In Rome, the new pope was Leo X — formerly Giovanni de' Medici; his brother Giuliano was also there. Both sons of Lorenzo the Magnificent became Leonardo's patrons.
- Leonardo spent 3 years in Rome, painted nothing, and was mainly occupied with mirrors.

## Chapter 30. Pointing the way

- Leonardo painted *Saint John the Baptist*.
- He also drew *A Pointing Lady*.

## Chapter 31. Mona Lisa

- Mona Lisa was the young wife of a silk merchant, Francesco del Giocondo.
- Leonardo began the painting in Florence and was still working on it on his deathbed — a total of about 16 years.
- The painting's alternative name, *La Gioconda*, means "joyful."

## Chapter 32. France

- King Francis I of France invited Leonardo to his court; Leonardo settled in France.
- He had partial paralysis following a stroke and a new companion, Battista de Vilanis.
- Leonardo died on 2 May 1519 at the age of 67; his presumed remains rest in Amboise, France.

## Conclusion

- Principles to draw from Leonardo's life: curiosity, acquiring knowledge for its own sake, childlike wonder, careful observation, taking small steps, imagination, fearlessness in the face of failure, allowing distraction, respect for facts, giving oneself time, and collaboration.

## Epilogue: describe the tongue of a woodpecker

- A woodpecker's tongue is three times longer than its beak.
- When retracted, it wraps around the skull, protecting the brain during pecking.
