## 1. Tatar (and other) roots

- The Sienkiewicz family traced its origins to the Lithuanian Tatars.
- Father – Józef: a landowner who eventually sold his village estate and moved to Warsaw.
- Mother – Stefania (née Cieciszowska): wrote poetry for her desk drawer.
- Siblings: Kazimierz (participant in the January Uprising, killed near Orléans during the Franco-Prussian War), Henryk (born 5 May 1846 in Wola Okrzejska), Aniela, Helena (a nun who translated and adapted her brother's works), Zofia (wrote popular works for the common people), Maria (died in childhood).

## 2. Sienkiewicz, a failing grade. The school and university education of the creator of the Trilogy

- Initially educated at home in Wężyczyn.
- At the age of 12 he was sent to a gymnasium in Warsaw; later the family moved there and they lived together.
- He received a failing grade in botany but excelled in history.
- Forced by financial circumstances, he spent a year working as a tutor in Poświętne.
- He sat the entrance exam for the Main School in Warsaw and was admitted despite failing grades in Greek and natural history.
- He successively studied law, medicine, and finally philology.
- He wrote articles for newspapers.
- He nearly completed his studies but failed the Greek examination.

## 3. The beginnings of the Polish Nobel laureate's literary career

- The novel "Ofiara" (The Sacrifice): written while still in Poświętne, later destroyed by the author.
- The poem "Sielanka młodości" (Idyll of Youth) submitted to "Tygodnik Ilustrowany" – rejected.
- A review of the play "Nasi najserdeczniejsi" (Our Dearest Ones).
- 1872: the novel "Na marne" (In Vain) and articles for "Wieniec" and "Niwa".
- Three short stories: "Stary sługa" (The Old Servant), "Hania", "Szkice węglem" (Charcoal Sketches) (Swoim mirza).

## 4. Henryk the traveller. Sienkiewicz's trips abroad

- 1874: together with friends he acquired "Niwa" and took on its editorship; he proposed to Maria Kellerówna (her father broke off the engagement); he also visited Paris.
- 1876–1878: stay in the USA as a journalist for "Gazeta Polska", travelling via London.
- After returning he spent a year in Paris.
- A stay in Italy, where he met his future wife Maria Szetkiewiczówna; they married in 1881.
- Maria died of tuberculosis in 1885.
- 1886: a trip to Turkey; they also visited Bulgaria, Romania, Greece, and Italy.
- 1888: 40 days in Spain.
- 1890: Africa – Egypt, Zanzibar.

## 7. Quo Vadis: if I publish it, it will be a good thing

- 1893: marriage to Maria Romanowska and a honeymoon trip to Italy; his wife left Henryk during the trip, yet it was precisely then that he conceived the idea for "Quo Vadis".

## 8. Works the Nobel laureate never finished

- "Szkice węglem II" (Charcoal Sketches II).
- "Kochankowie leśni" (Forest Lovers) – the story of 10 years of hiding in a forest after the January Uprising.
- "Bajka o królu Władysławowie" (A Tale of King Władysław).
- A book about George Washington.
- "Julian Apostata" (Julian the Apostate).
- A new trilogy – only "Na polu chwały" (On the Field of Glory) was written.
- "Legiony" (The Legions).

## 9. How are you, my dear little cat?

- He had two children with his first wife.
- Łaciasty – Henryk Józef Sienkiewicz (born 1882): studied architecture in France, settled in Oblęgorek, and married Zuzanna Cielecka.
- Dzinka – Jadwiga (born 1883): suffered from tuberculosis, studied painting in France, translated novels; she married Tadeusz Korniłowicz.

## 12. Oblęgorek: the ancestral seat

### The writer's day

- He rose early.
- He ate a light breakfast.
- He wrote from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.
- He rested – hunting, playing cards, walking, receiving guests, and reading.

### Oblęgorek

- Presented to Sienkiewicz on the 25th anniversary of his literary work.
- He stayed there only in summer.
- After his death the estate was looked after by his wife – Maria née Babska.
- Subsequently his children took care of it.
- For a time it housed the State Manufacturing and Wholesale Plant of Herbal Preparations.
- It is now a museum; the first curator was Zuzanna Sienkiewicz, the writer's daughter-in-law.

## 13. Whole generations draw and will continue to draw from the spirit of Sienkiewicz

- He died in Vevey, Switzerland, on 15 November 1916.
- On 21 October 1924 his coffin was brought to Poland in a chapel wagon.
- A solemn funeral took place on 26 and 27 October.
