# Content

## Part I. User manual for a man

### 1. Women are from Venus, men are from Mars

- A woman – thanks to the female hormones oestrogens – is destined to experience far more feelings and emotions than a man.

## 2. Woman – a perfect being

- A hormonal exchange is taking place: men have increasingly more oestrogen, women increasingly more testosterone.
- Female hormones in women: oestrone, oestradiol, oestriol, progesterone.
- Male hormone in men: testosterone.

## 3. Male and female hormones

- Testosterone is the hormone of sex – in both men and women.
- Women produce more of it, but ultimately have ten times less, because they use most of it to produce female hormones.
- Up to week 8 of pregnancy, all of us are female.

## 4. The making of a man

- After the age of 40, a man begins to lose testosterone at approximately 2% per year.

## 5. Testosterone versus oestrogens

- Testosterone: plays on instincts, acts before the act.
- Oestrogens: play on emotions, act after the act.
- The sun influences hormones and thereby sexual attributes; sizes (from largest): Africa, Europe, Asia.

## 6. The sun – a friend of hormones

- Melatonin is the head of all hormones; it controls sleep (the longer it is dark, the more of it there is) and the hypothalamus, where other hormones are produced.
- Testosterone testing includes: total testosterone, free testosterone, and SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin) – the more SHBG, the less free testosterone; often elevated in vegetarians.

## 7. Male menopause

- Testosterone → adrenaline → erythrocytes (red blood cells) → haemoglobin.
- Haemoglobin may also fail to be produced due to iron deficiency; sources of iron: red meat, egg yolks, green vegetables.

## 8. The history of testosterone and how it works

- Testosterone was discovered by Butenandt – first in Göttingen, later in Gdańsk.
- In 1939 he received the Nobel Prize, but as a member of the NSDAP he did not collect it; he collected it after the war, but without the monetary award.
- Testosterone affects: the psyche, body composition, and sexual behaviour.

## 9. Testosterone – a miraculous panacea?

- Recommended vegetables: cauliflower, broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, radish.
- Carbohydrates to limit: flour, sugar, fruit, alcohol.

## II. Health

### 10. Diseases of the 21st century

- In medicine, the best doctor is the last one, because all the previous ones did the dirty work for him.
- Psychosomatic diseases originate in the psyche – there is nothing physically wrong with the body.
- Pillars of health: nutrition, physical activity, peace of mind, sexual activity, sleep.

## 11. Typically male problems

- Importance of penis characteristics for women (from most to least important): overall appearance, hair, skin smoothness, girth, shape of the glans, length.
- For women, the most important thing in a partner's sexuality is physical and psychological engagement.
- It is the mother's role to ensure the testicles descend from the abdomen into the scrotum, should that situation arise.
- The testicles are external because temperature is dangerous for them – even 37°C is harmful.

## 12. The prostate

- The prostate is like a hot dog: the urethra (the extension of the penis) is the sausage, and the prostate is the bun.
- With age, the prostate enlarges and presses on the urethra.
- Recommended checks: ultrasound examination and PSA (prostate-specific antigen) – total and free – once a year.
- The PCA-3 gene: high sensitivity and specificity for prostate cancer.
- Regular emptying of the prostate of semen prevents its enlargement.
- Pumpkin seed oil or pumpkin seeds (containing sitosterol) may slow prostate enlargement.

## III. Male problems, sex and fertility

### 13. We cannot conceive

- In the 21st century, every 7th couple had difficulty conceiving; currently every 5th.
- Research findings: 30% of cases the man is at fault, 30% the woman, 40% joint dysfunction.
- Problems with sperm: insufficient motility, abnormal morphology, too few (formerly the norm was 150 million, now 60 million).
- A spermiogram measures sperm parameters.
- The male reproductive hormonal axis: the hypothalamus releases gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) in pulses every 90 minutes → GnRH stimulates the pituitary gland to secrete LH and FSH → LH stimulates Leydig cells in the testes to produce testosterone → FSH stimulates Sertoli cells to produce sperm → sperm are stimulated by both FSH and testosterone.
- Factors that limit testosterone: age, stress, oestrogens in water and food, plastic packaging, tight trousers.

## 14. Trouble in bed

- Male sex comprises: desire (libido), intercourse (erection + ejaculation), satisfaction from intercourse.
- Disorders of desire result from testosterone deficiency, which occurs when cholesterol is "taken" by stress.

## 15. Medications and sex

- Almost every medication has an effect on sex.

## 16. When it ends before it begins

- Types of premature ejaculation: before penetration, during the first 1–2 minutes of penetration, when the man has no control over ejaculation.
- It is considered a problem when this occurs in the majority of sexual encounters over 6 months; affects approximately 30% of men.

## 17. Homosexuality

- It is not yet fully understood what influences homosexuality.

## IV. Male love

### 18. The hormonal rush of love

- Falling in love is the moment when hormones flood us: phenylethylamine (rises at the start of a relationship), oxytocin (empathy increases), testosterone (desire, protectiveness).
- Effects: obsessive thoughts, illusions, a distorted view of the world.
- The 4 S's: sex, sleep, sport, style of eating.

## 19. The colours of love

- Testosterone should be tested between 7 and 9 in the morning to obtain an undistorted result.

## 20. The palette of colours of love

- There are different kinds of love.

## V. A man's lifestyle and diet

### 21. What about sport?

- Sport is good, but amateur sport.
- Sport produces hormones: dopamine (the happiness hormone) and serotonin (regulates temperature, sleep and appetite).
- Recommended cardio training: walking, nordic walking, swimming, cycling, rollerblading.

## 22. What should a healthy man eat?

- Digestion issues: lactose (from milk), fructose (from fruit), gluten (wheat and rye).

## 23. The whole truth about lactose

- Lactose is milk sugar.
- The enzymes that break down lactose begin to die off between the ages of 2 and 5, when we stop drinking mother's milk.
- Lactose tolerance depends on the country: where it is warm, calcium can be obtained from sunlight, so tolerance fades; in the north, we have adapted to obtaining calcium from animals.
- Other sources of calcium: green vegetables, kale, broccoli, lettuce, desiccated coconut, coconut milk, sesame, nuts, almonds.

## 24. Gluten

- Known since time immemorial: oats, barley.
- Known only since Mesopotamia: wheat, rye.
- Carbohydrates from flour cause atherosclerotic plaques (noodles, dumplings, pasta, bread).
- Adding fibre-containing grains to flour slows carbohydrate absorption and protects against diabetes.

## 25. Fructose intolerance

- Eat vegetables 4 times a day and fruit once – in small amounts (a handful).
- Best fruits: wild strawberries, raspberries, blackberries, cranberries, gooseberries, bilberries, blueberries.
- Recommended vegetable juices: cucumber, mint, celery, carrot, beetroot, parsley root.

## 26. Meat

- The paleo diet: lean meat, fish, berries, leafy vegetables, root vegetables, brassica vegetables.

## VI. What will become of men?

### 27. Metrosexuality and testosterone

- A metrosexual man is more refined: he uses sunbeds, gets manicures, and knows about fashion.

## 28. The most important hormone

- A man without testosterone would be sad and lazy.
- Testosterone takes care of: sex, physique, well-being.

## 29. Has testosterone died?

- There is less and less testosterone.
- Women are coping better.
