# Content

## Part I. The beginning of a relationship

### 2. Are you the person I'm looking for?
- Love at first sight: 28% of women need to get to know a partner's character first; 48% of men fall in love at first sight.
- People who fall in love at first sight are more often satisfied with their sex life.
- After 6–9 years, 68% of respondents consider their partner extremely attractive.
- Similarities and differences: the truth lies in the middle – similarities allow you to do things together, while differences inspire each other.
- Men miss most: communication, affection, and sex.
- Women miss most: communication, affection, and financial stability.
- Couples evolve: at first they are very passionate, and later become best friends. Traits of a best friend: communication (sharing what has happened to you), trust, quality time spent together.

## 3. Romance in small... or large doses
- Romance is about surprise – a partner wants to delight and please us. Its features: uniqueness, privacy, sex.
- Types of romance: romantic gifts, evening dates (ideally once every two weeks), romantic getaways, back massages, passionate kisses.

## 4. Tender gestures
- Tenderness is one of the fundamental sources of strength in a relationship.
- Types of tenderness: holding hands (in public and in private), passionate kisses, saying "I love you", compliments, hugging, affectionate nicknames.

## 5. Physical love – it can be perverse
- A relationship should maintain a balance between sex, intimacy, and love.
- It is important that the difference in sexual interest between partners is small.
- It is perfectly normal for couples to enrich their erotic life with masturbation.

## II. Living together

### 6. Wonderful understanding
- Communication is the most important thing – listening to and understanding your partner is key. It should feel safe, fun, and interesting.
- Interrupting can serve communication.
- Laughter is important.
- Arguments are good because they lead to a resolution; disputes are not.
- Criticism destabilises a relationship.
- Exercise: write down the 5 most important things you need to be happy (unrelated to your partner or children) and the 5 most important changes you expect from your partner.
- Sharing your thoughts deepens the intimacy of a relationship.

## 7. What love looks like
- Appearance affects relationships.
- After 6–9 years, almost half of respondents feel their partner has let themselves go.
- Weight gain threatens relationships.
- Exercising together can strengthen a relationship.
- If you want your partner to change, don't criticise – lead by example.
- Exercise: in a shop, show your partner the clothes you like most, then ask them to choose something for you.

## 8. Full-time work, and then some
- Sharing household duties equally has a positive effect on a relationship.
- The division of household chores gives more room for negotiation than professional matters.

## 9. Money and sense
- Couples should talk openly about money.
- A prenuptial agreement is no longer unusual.
- More than half of couples have not drawn up a will.

## 10. Friends and family
- It is good to set boundaries in advance regarding friendships, meetings with former partners, etc.

## III. Maintaining a relationship

### 11. Keeping the flame of passion alive
- The happiest couples make love 3–4 times a week.
- Men want from their partners: sexual variety, less passivity, more feedback.
- Women want from their partners: foreplay, romance, less predictability.

## 12. Secrets and lies

## 13. Infidelity
- Fantasies about sex with others may be a natural way of preparing for a partner's potential death or divorce.
- Infidelity is determined primarily by an unsatisfying sex life, not by the length of the relationship.
- Relationships are most fragile between years 6 and 9.
- Between years 2 and 5, couples are susceptible to the influence of former partners.
- Causes of infidelity: anger at a partner, boredom.
- Solutions: avoid temptations; tell your partner honestly about the temptation – it will lose much of its power.

## 16. Striving for happiness in a relationship
- Statistics: 14% of couples are extremely happy, 25% very happy, 35% relatively happy.
- You must not think only of yourself, but you also must not completely subordinate yourself to your partner.
- Key principles: compromise, balance in giving and taking, sometimes yielding and sometimes standing your ground.
