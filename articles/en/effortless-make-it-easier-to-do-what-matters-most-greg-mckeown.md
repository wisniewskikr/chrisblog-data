# Content

## Introduction
- The book *Essentialism* is about choosing what matters; this book is about how to do those important things well.
- We all want roughly the same things: good health, savings, professional fulfillment, and close relationships with others.
- Achieving goals effortlessly is not a sign of laziness but of cleverness.
- Linear result: effort invested once produces a single outcome.
- Residual result: effort invested once produces outcomes for an indefinitely long time.

## I. Effortless state

### 1. Invert
- Ask yourself: is there an easier way?
- It is not a law of nature that important things are hard and trivial things are easy.
- The principle of least effort: the brain operates by it.
- Very often we fail when we try too hard.
- When we eliminate complexity, even the smallest effort can move things forward.

## 2. Enjoy
- Instead of enduring difficult tasks, enjoy them.
- It is not a law of nature that important things and enjoyable things are separate — combining them means effortless action.
- Important things often reward us with a delay; it is worth trying to give yourself something immediately.
- LEGO comes from the Danish words "leg godt," meaning "play well."
- Habits tell you what you do; rituals tell you how you do it.

## 3. Give up
- Revisit your goals regularly so you are not carrying unnecessary baggage.
- When problems arise, the easiest response is complaining — but is it the best?
- The more you complain, the more reasons to complain you see.
- The more grateful you are, the more reasons for gratitude you see.
- Be grateful for your resources, strengths, and skills.
- When you focus on what you have, you get what you lack.

## 4. Rest
- The optimal work pattern is 3 sessions of 90 minutes each.
- Take a 10–15 minute break between sessions.
- Taking a hot shower 90 minutes before going to bed is beneficial.
- A nap helps reduce sleep debt.
- Salvador Dalí napped holding a key — the key would wake him as he drifted off.

## 5. See
- You must be aware, alert, and present even when other information distracts you.
- Three types of responses to a partner's bid for connection: 1. turning toward the partner; 2. turning against the partner; 3. turning away (indifferent response).
- When someone asks for advice, instead of sharing your opinion you can ask clarifying questions or summarize what you have heard so far — the person asking must make their own decision.

## II. Effortless action
- The law of diminishing marginal returns: beyond a certain point, greater effort does not produce better results but actually reduces performance.

## 6. Define
- For a goal to be achieved, the desired outcome must be clearly defined.
- Create a list of things that can realistically be done in a single day.

## 7. Start
- Once you have a strategy, it is enough to focus on the first step.
- In Silicon Valley, development begins with a minimum viable product, designed to test customer expectations.

## 8. Simplify
- The key question: what is the minimum number of steps required to complete the task?
- Do not simplify a complicated process — start from scratch and add only the minimum number of steps needed to succeed.

## 9. Make progress
- If you want to achieve something, you must have the courage to build something imperfect in the first phase.
- It is best to make mistakes of a didactic size so that you do not later make mistakes of a practical size.

## 10. Maintain pace
- The Norwegians reached the South Pole only because they set a daily pace and stuck to it regardless of good or bad weather.
- The costs of inconsistency: on days when you give everything, you feel exhausted; on other days you feel discouraged and dissatisfied.
- Principle: slow means smooth, smooth means fast.
- A good approach is to set a minimum and maximum limit for yourself and not exceed either.

## III. Effortless results
- Linear result: you invest effort once and receive a reward once.
- Residual result: you invest effort once but receive rewards over a long time, even while you sleep.
- Benjamin Franklin: money makes money, and the money that money makes makes more money.
- To achieve effortless results you must use leverage — good leverage brings great gain, bad leverage brings great harm.

## 11. Learn
- Instructions can be used once; principles can be used repeatedly.
- There may be a million methods but few principles; a person who understands a principle can create their own methods, while a person who uses a method without understanding the underlying principles is asking for trouble.
- Knowledge can be divided into three types: 1. inorganic (e.g. geology); 2. organic (e.g. the animal world); 3. human (e.g. principles of coexistence).
- The principle of reciprocity: you get what you give.
- Knowledge is like a tree — you must first understand the trunk and branches before moving to the leaves; otherwise the leaves will have nothing to hold on to.
- Life wisdom is interconnected knowledge from different areas of life.
- Reading books is one of the greatest forms of leverage in the world — a small investment of time and money yields knowledge for a lifetime.
- In 1968, Dick Fosbury invented a new technique for the high jump.

## 12. Elevate
- There is no better way to teach than through stories.
- Identify and then simplify the most important messages.

## 13. Automate
- There is ever more knowledge — a strategy for using it is needed.
- One such strategy is the checklist.
- Another strategy is automation.

## 14. Trust
- Trust is like lubricant for an engine.
- Seek people who are honest, intelligent, and show initiative; without the first quality, the other two are dangerous.

## 15. Prevent
- Solve problems once and quickly.
- Pull up roots instead of cutting off branches.
