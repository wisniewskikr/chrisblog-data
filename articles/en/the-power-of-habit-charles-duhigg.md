# Content

## Introduction

- Changing a key habit influences other habits and one's entire life.
- Life as a whole is usually a collection of habits.

## I. The habits of individuals

### 1. The habit loop: how habits work

- One can fail to remember new things and yet form new habits – this is handled by a different, older part of the brain.
- The habit loop: cue → routine → reward.
- Habits never completely disappear. This can be beneficial (after a holiday you don't have to relearn how to drive) or harmful (bad habits don't disappear either).
- Even a small change can override a habit.

## 2. The craving brain: how to create new habits

- Craving is the driving force behind a habit.
- The rules of Hopkins, an advertising expert: find a simple and obvious cue, clearly define the reward (e.g. dental plaque → beautiful teeth).
- For a habit to persist, there must be an anticipation of the reward.

## 3. The golden rule of habit change: why transformation occurs

- To change a habit, keep the old cue and the old reward but change the routine in the middle of the loop.
- The golden rule: you cannot eradicate an old habit, you can only replace it with a new one.
- Wilson founded Alcoholics Anonymous; 2.1 million people seek help annually, and 10 million people have overcome their addiction.
- Belief turns a redesigned habit loop into lasting behaviour.

## Part II. The habits of successful organisations

### 4. Keystone habits, or the ballad of Paul O'Neill: which habits matter most

- In an organisation it is worthwhile to change habits related to one issue and then move to the level of the entire organisation.
- Keystone habits can trigger the change of other habits.
- Example: a focus on workplace safety translates into quality.
- Regular exercise in individuals works similarly, as does eating dinner together or making the bed.
- Small Wins play an important role, leading to big victories.
- Keeping a food diary can help with losing weight.

## 5. Starbucks and the habit of success: when willpower becomes automatic

- Self-discipline predicts academic achievement more accurately than IQ.
- The best way to strengthen self-discipline is to turn it into a habit.
- Starbucks taught its employees to deliver not only coffee but also joy to customers – this required controlling one's own emotions and practising self-discipline.
- About 30% of children were able to wait in order to receive two sweets later instead of one now.
- Willpower is a skill that can be learned.
- Willpower works like a muscle – it gets tired; when overworked, less of it remains for other tasks.
- Strengthening willpower through a physical exercise programme or financial management carried over to other areas of life, such as diet and work.
- When willpower grows stronger, it benefits every area of life.
- That is why enrolling a child in piano lessons or sports activities is so important – by learning to push through practice outside school hours, a child builds the capacity for self-regulation.
- Writing down clear plans helps maintain self-discipline.
- Starbucks developed brochures with instructions on how to handle difficult situations.
