# Content

## Introduction

### I. Telling the truth about sex

### 1. What do people say about their expectations of sex? What do they really want?

- Sexual intelligence = information + emotional skills + body awareness
- Not worrying about what your partner is thinking – like trying to enjoy dinner while wearing a white suit
- Being genuine and relaxed
- Not focusing on "performing"
- Thinking about feeling, not functionality – being uninhibited and passionate
- Sex is a means to an end, not an end in itself

## 2. Am I normal?

- Seek sex full of intimacy rather than "normal" sex
- Two dimensions of sexual normality:
  - Practical: the functioning of the genitals and mouth
  - Moral: control and depravity

## 3. What is sexual intelligence?

- Three components of sexual intelligence:
  - Information and knowledge
  - Emotional skills
  - Body awareness and comfort
- A relationship is strengthened in order to enhance sex – not the other way around
- It is important to identify what is unimportant and to have the discipline to ignore it

### II. Components of sexual intelligence

## 4. Your brain

- In most women, orgasm comes from clitoral stimulation, not vaginal
- Sexual response cycle: excitement → plateau → orgasm → resolution
- Orgasm is dessert, not the main course
- A woman is fertile from approximately age 13 to 50; ovulation occurs roughly in the middle of the menstrual cycle
- Sperm can survive up to 5 days, an egg up to 2 days
- For safety, the fertility window is approximately 13 days
- The rhythm method: 25% of couples become pregnant within a year
- The morning-after pill is not an abortion pill
- Syphilis: a bacterial infection that can be cured
- Herpes: a viral infection that cannot be cured but can be managed through medication and lifestyle
- Humanity is sexually diverse – there is no single normality

## 5. Your heart

- Emotional skills are needed before sex
- Key emotional areas:
  - Self-acceptance
  - Trust: overcoming reluctance to trust another person
  - Communication
  - Maturity: the ability to laugh at the quirks of sex
  - Maintaining self-worth in the face of disappointment: the outcome of a sexual encounter should not determine anything
  - Coming to terms with partial incompatibility

## 6. Your body

- If there is a lot of pain in bed, it may be a neuro-sexual problem
- Sex is a domain to which the normal rules of aesthetics do not apply
- Sex requires crossing boundaries
- In Western countries, approximately 90% of men and two-thirds of women masturbate at some point in their lives
- Be mindful during sex

### III. Suggestions and methods

## 7. Letting go

- Do not try at all costs to perform
- Erection and lubrication: brain → spinal cord → pelvic nerves
- Focus on why you can have sex, not on why you cannot

## 8. New focus, new approach

- Three types of conditions conducive to sex:
  - Relating to ourselves: absence of obligations, etc.
  - Relating to the environment: privacy, etc.
  - Relating to the partner: mutual fascination, etc.
- Be mindful during sex
- We decide for ourselves what is sexy
- Communicate during sex

## 9. Accepting the inevitable

- Alcohol arouses desire but takes away the ability to act

## 10. Creating sex that will not be a failure (or a success)

- Sex is not inherently intimate – you have to make it so
- Only two important questions: how do I like it? and is my partner enjoying this with me?
- Physical functions – erection, lubrication, orgasm – are means to the end of pleasure
- Establish how to initiate sex and how to decline gently
- Set aside time for sex; if there is none, postpone it
- Do not divert attention away from something unpleasant – instead, focus on something pleasurable

- People have their own narratives (storylines) about sex
