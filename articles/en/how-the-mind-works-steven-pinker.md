## Preface

## 1. Standard equipment

- Facts + Rules = Conclusions
- Psychology is reverse engineering: regular engineering builds something, reverse engineering reconstructs it – what the mind is for.
- Information is independent of medium: cable (telephone), air (speech), neurons (brain).
- Beliefs and desires are information (computation).
- The mind works only thanks to assumptions that have been confirmed millions of times.
- Knowledge comes from culture and from learning in new situations.
- Genes and environment answer the question: what makes X work better than Y.
- Morning sickness in pregnant women helps expel toxins; research shows that the more severe the nausea, the lower the risk of miscarriage.
- It is easier to change the mind than the body, because it is easier to change software than hardware.
- People do not selfishly spread their genes – it is genes that selfishly spread themselves and modify our behaviour.
- Emotional life is a currency between desire and conscience.

## 2. Thinking machines

- Intelligence is the ability to achieve goals despite obstacles through decisions based on rational rules: a goal, the current gap from the goal, and operations that close that gap.
- Intelligence comes from information; information is a cause that produces effects.
- A symbol both means something and causes something.
- Forms of representation: visual imagery, phonological representation, grammatical representation, mentalese.

### Defending the championship title

### Replaced by a machine

- Neural network modelling: AND, OR, and NOT operations; continuous values with probabilities (not 0/1); learning by adjusting weights based on input and output data.

### Connectoplasm

- Two laws govern thought (1748): contiguity – ideas that often appear together become associated in the mind; similarity – whatever was associated with the first idea becomes associated with the second.
- Problem with the individual: neural networks easily distinguish classes and subclasses (animal/horse) but struggle with the individual (horse/Ed).
- Problem with compositionality: the order of elements in thought matters (the dog bit the man vs. the man bit the dog); solution: actor, action, and object of action all linked to the sentence.

### Aladdin's lamp

- Consciousness encompasses: self-knowledge, access to information, and conscious experience.
- Features of consciousness: we have information about the world; we can place it in short-term memory and use it there; every piece of information comes with an emotional flavour; we have a "self" that makes choices.
- We remember frequent and recent events.

## 3. Revenge of the nerds

- Evolution is about goals, not means – the goal is to pass on genes; intelligence is a means, not an end.
- Intelligence is a gadget (like a trunk): we have it because in prehistory its benefits outweighed the costs.
- Correct theory: natural selection.
- Flawed theories: Lamarckism (inheritance of acquired characteristics), macromutation (a new, fully functional mechanism arising immediately from copying errors), random drift.

### The blind programmer

- Natural selection does not deliver information but builds brains.
- Evolution and learning are two different processes but can occur simultaneously.
- Learning changes the problem of finding a needle in a haystack into the problem of finding a needle when you are already close.

### Instinct vs. intelligence

- The human mind is built from ready-made components – instincts.
- Species develop at the expense of others; humans occupied the cognitive niche.
- Humans have an advantage because they attack animals that can defend themselves only evolutionarily, in subsequent generations.

### Why us

- Possible reasons for intelligence: we are visual creatures (we see "what" and "where"); group living requires being smarter than others; we have hands; hunting provided better nutrients for the brain.

### The modern Stone Age family

- At some point in our history we were probably close to extinction – there were about 10,000 of us; hence our biological similarity.
- Culture spreads like infectious diseases; evolution created the psyche.

## 4. The eye of the mind

- Vision is a process that creates from images of the external world a description that is useful to the viewer, uncluttered by irrelevant information.

### Depth of the eye

- The eyes cannot determine the distance of objects; the brain calculates it using the difference between the images of the two eyes and by observing objects during movement.
- The genome builds the best mechanism it can; when it cannot predict something, it provides a constructor that operates during a critical period (e.g., setting depth perception, language).

### Illuminating, shadowing, shaping

- Vision consists of several specialised processes and always selects the cheapest, most probable option.

## 6. The nutcases

- Disgust prevents eating something harmful; children probably narrow down what they find disgusting in the first two years of life.
- Ethnic food taboos most often prohibit foods eaten by enemies, to avoid fraternising with them.
- Wealth is like health: its absence makes one unhappy, but having it does not guarantee happiness.
- There are twice as many negative emotions as positive ones; losses are felt far more acutely than gains.
- Directly pursuing happiness is a recipe for an unhappy life (Campbell).
- Listening to emotions instead of reason means sacrificing a long-term goal for short-term gain.
- Forms of altruism: kin altruism and reciprocal altruism.
- Emotions associated with altruism: liking (someone who will reciprocate), anger (someone who cheated), gratitude (someone who reciprocated), sympathy (a desire to help), guilt (someone might catch me cheating).
- Love makes partners reluctant to trade each other in for someone better.
- Perhaps the worst emotion is self-deception: a sense of being right when one is not; the strength to fight when one should give up.

## 7. Family values

- Homicide rates (per million): 0.5 – Iceland; 10 – Europe; 25 – Canada; 50 – USA and Brazil.
- Homicides in the USA: 1/4 family (of which 2–6% are biological relatives), 1/4 complete strangers, 0.5 acquaintances.
- No society can be simultaneously just, free, and equal.
- Five personality dimensions: extraversion–introversion; neuroticism–stability; conscientiousness–carelessness; agreeableness–antagonism; openness–lack of openness.
- Research shows that children are raised by their peer group, not by their parents.
- Sexual attraction within a family depends on whether its members were raised together; people assume that those who live with them share the same genes.
- Sex is a defence against parasites and pathogens.
- We reproduce sexually because of mitochondria: if mitochondria came from both parents, they would compete; the male contributes only DNA, without mitochondria.
- In the world there is: male competition and female choice.
- Women are more selective than men, but once they consent there is no reason to assume they respond less strongly to sexual stimuli.
- Affairs: a woman is attracted to a lover who surpasses or complements her husband; a man is attracted by the fact that the woman is not his wife.
- Infidelity: men are more hurt by physical infidelity, women by emotional infidelity.
- The purpose of friendship is to rescue you in need, when it would not be worth anyone else's effort.
- War is waged by: humans, chimpanzees, dolphins, and possibly bonobos.

## 8. The meaning of life

- The psychology of art is the psychology of status.
- Art, like religion, has no adaptive function.
- We laugh 30 times more often in the company of other people.
- Laughter was perhaps a signal of mock aggression – indicating play, not a real fight.
- Three components of humour: incongruity, substitution, and degradation.
- Humour probably serves to maintain friendship: it allows people to reject dominance while preserving relationships in which both dominance–submission and friendship are at play.
- Religion is a technique for success.
