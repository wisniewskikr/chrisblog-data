# Content

## I. Why can't we get along with each other?

### 1. What the experts say

- Deficit theory: bad relationships are caused by a lack of skills.
- Related theory: men and women cannot get along with each other.
- It is not events that irritate us, but what we think about them.
- Other causes of bad relationships: low self-esteem, burnout in the relationship, lack of trust, and fear of vulnerability.
- Motivational theory: bad relationships stem from a lack of motivation to change.
- Marriage therapies do not work.

## 2. The dark side of human nature

- People may have a dark side: hitting and humiliating others gives them more satisfaction than love.

## 3. Why we secretly love to hate

- Motives that compete with love: power and control, revenge, justice and fairness, narcissism, pride and shame, scapegoating, truth, guilt, self-pity, anger and bitterness, rivalry, hidden motives.

## 4. Three things that can change your life

- Good communication means: expressing feelings openly and directly, listening to your partner without being defensive, treating your partner with respect.
- Principles of Interpersonal Cognitive Therapy:
  1. We all provoke and maintain the very same relationship problems we complain about.
  2. We deny our role in the conflict and are secretly rewarded by the problem.
  3. We can make changes very quickly.

## Relationship diagnosis

### 5. How good is your relationship? The relationship satisfaction test

- In troubled relationships, test scores are often at opposite extremes.
- In successful relationships, scores are often similar.

## 6. What do you really want?

- Three options when facing a troubled relationship: maintain the status quo, end the relationship, work on improving it.
- Within the third option, the possibilities are: wait for the other person to change; change the other person; change yourself.

## 7. The price of intimacy

- You must take responsibility for solving problems in the relationship.

## 8. The relationship journal

- You write down what one person said and then what you replied.

## 9. Good communication vs. bad communication

- Good communication is EAR: empathy (you try to see the world through the other person's eyes), assertiveness (you express feelings openly and directly, often using the word "I feel"), respect (you treat the other person with kindness, care, and respect).
- Common communication mistakes: truth always on your side, blame on the other side, defensiveness, martyrdom, belittling, labeling (e.g. jerk, loser), sarcasm, counterattack, scapegoating.

## 10. How we control other people

- The way we address people influences the way they address us.

## 11. Three troubled relationships

- The chapter presents examples of troubled relationships.

## III. How to build a loving relationship with the people you care about

### 12. The five secrets of effective communication

- The five secrets: the disarming technique (you find truth in the other person's words), empathy, follow-up questioning, feeling statements, stroking (acknowledging the positive aspects of the other person).

## 13. The disarming technique

- The law of opposites: when you agree with criticism, you render it invalid.
- Shame and fear are among the greatest barriers to intimacy.
- You do not have to agree on the details, only in general.
- It is good to acknowledge the other person's point and ask for details.

## 14. Thought and feeling empathy

- Empathy means assessing what other people feel and think — we are poor at this.
- Thought empathy: you repeat what your conversation partner said.
- Feeling empathy: "I imagine you feel… Am I right?"
- Empathy means listening and setting yourself aside.

## 15. Follow-up questioning to check understanding

- Empathy should be supplemented with follow-up questions to make sure you have understood everything correctly.

## 16. Feeling statements

- You should use sentences containing the word "feel."

## 17. Stroking: I-it vs. I-thou relationships

- Stroking means expressing a positive opinion about the other person: kindness, care, respect.

## 18. Putting all the techniques together: solutions to common relationship problems

- Complaining: when someone complains, you need to listen; it is worth using the disarming technique.
- Big ego: use stroking primarily.
- Lazy and stubborn: share your feelings.
- Bossy: use mainly the disarming technique and stroking.
- Jealous person: apply the appropriate techniques from the set.

## IV. What to do to make the five secrets work for you

### 19. Polishing the five secrets

- You should practice communication consistently.

## 20. Applying the five secrets in real time: the intimacy exercise

- Practice the techniques with your partner.

## 21. Intimacy training for couples: the one-minute exercise

- The one-minute exercise is designed to improve communication between partners.

## V. Common pitfalls and how to avoid them

### 22. Help. The five secrets aren't working

- There are common mistakes when applying this technique.

## 23. Helping and problem-solving

- Communication obstacles: addiction to helping, addiction to problem-solving.

## 24. Burying your head in the sand: fear of conflict and fear of anger

- It is very hard for people to notice anger in themselves.
- We are taught that if you love someone, you never fight with that person.
- Ways of expressing anger: active aggression, passive aggression, sharing anger.
- Power and aggression are exciting and are in our genes.

## 25. Apologizing: can't I just say "I'm sorry"?

- Apologizing can serve to silence the other person or to avoid intimacy.

## 26. Submissiveness: "I have to please you"

- Self-destructive beliefs act like a self-fulfilling prophecy.
- The opposite of submissiveness is entitlement.

## 27. Back to resistance: "why should I do all the work?"

- You can only change yourself, but your change is a catalyst for change in the other person.

## VI. Advanced techniques

### 28. Changing the focus: have we glossed over something?

- Changing the focus is useful when tension is hanging in the air — like an invisible elephant in the room. Instead of pretending there is no conflict, you gently point it out.

## 29. Positive reframing: opening the door to intimacy and success

- Positive reframing means seeing the other person's motives and behavior in a more positive light.
- It also involves helping the other person realize that conflict is nothing to be afraid of.

## 30. Multiple-choice empathy: how to talk to someone who doesn't want to talk to you

- Multiple-choice empathy involves presenting a list of possible feelings your conversation partner might have and asking whether any of them ring true.
