# Content

## Phase I. Launch

### 1. Flight in the face of uncertainty

- We cannot live with uncertainty, so we make up stories and live as if they were true.
- Herschel discovered Uranus beyond Saturn – the last planet of the solar system, which thereby became twice as large.
- Le Verrier determined that there must be another planet between Saturn and Uranus – one that causes Uranus to alternately slow down and speed up. He discovered this using only a pen.
- Mercury presented a similar puzzle, failing to conform to Newton's laws.
- Since 2006, Pluto (and Eris) are no longer classified as planets.
- Redundant elements are useful, but only up to a point – beyond that point they increase the risk of failure.
- It is worth planning with a safety margin.

## 2. First principles

- Musk was born in South Africa; at the age of 12 he wrote and sold his first video game, then moved to Canada and later to the USA, where he studied physics and economics at the University of Pennsylvania.
- He dropped out of a doctoral programme at Stanford.
- Together with his brother Kimbal he founded Zip2 (online city guides), which he sold to Compaq.
- He launched the online bank X.com, later renamed PayPal, which eBay acquired for 165 million dollars.
- Knowledge is valuable but can lead to conventional thinking.
- People default to the status quo and are wired to follow the herd.
- Procedures should serve people, not people serve procedures.
- First principles: return to the source and discard everything superfluous.
- Occam's Razor: the simplest solution is the best.

## 3. Mental diversions

- Thought experiments are fundamental – they are rooted in curiosity.
- Land invented the Polaroid in 1943 after his little daughter asked why she couldn't see the photo right away.
- Boredom is very important for creativity, yet we no longer allow ourselves to be bored.
- Rowling came up with the story of Harry Potter when her train from Manchester to London was 4 hours late.
- It is easier to think outside the box when you have many boxes to begin with.
- Bowerman came up with the Nike shoe design when he looked at the pattern on a waffle iron.
- The best cycles: focus – boredom, solitude – group work.

## 4. Moonshot thinking – reach for the stars

- Google X devised Project Loon: internet delivered via balloons.
- Divergent thinking means generating ideas; convergent thinking means focusing on a goal.
- The two should be combined: first generate ideas, then choose the best one.
- The sunk-cost effect: it is hard to abandon something we have already invested effort in.
- Focus on the hard things first, then tackle the easy ones.

## Phase II: Acceleration

### 5. What if you sent two rovers instead of one?

- Premature babies die because they cannot regulate their body temperature – they lack fat; incubators address this.
- In 1968 Fosbury introduced the technique of jumping over the high-jump bar backwards rather than forwards, and won the Olympics.
- A breakthrough begins with a good question.
- Do not focus immediately on the answer, but on the question – on strategy, not tactics.

## 6. The power of the pivot

- Confirmation bias: we undervalue facts we disagree with and overvalue facts we agree with.
- Mars Climate Orbiter crashed because one company calculated in metres and the other in inches – a loss of 193 million dollars. The tests revealed this error, but navigators believed it would self-correct.
- An opinion expressed aloud is very hard to change – over time it becomes part of one's identity, which is even harder to change.
- It is better to form hypotheses than opinions: opinions are defended, hypotheses are tested; having several hypotheses is preferable.
- When observing something, it is worth asking what cannot be seen.
- The 2, 4, 6 rule puzzle: the answer is simply numbers in ascending order – only 1 in 5 people get it right.
- Theories: Aristotle → Galileo → Newton → Einstein → quantum mechanics.
- Pseudoscience relies on non-falsifiable arguments – claims whose falsity can never be proven.
- The goal is not to be right, but to find out where the truth lies.

## 7. Test as you fly, fly as you test

- If something contradicts the result of an experiment, it is wrong.
- A second problem is the gap between real-world conditions and test conditions.
- Hitler came to power through two constitutional paragraphs: the president could dissolve parliament, and could declare a state of emergency (which parliament could overturn by a simple majority).
- The first American in space was the chimpanzee Ham.
- The horse Hans could seemingly count – in reality he was reading the reactions of the person asking.
- A double-blind trial eliminates the observer effect: scientists do not know who received the medicine and who received the placebo.

## Phase III. The finish line

### 8. Nothing works as well as failure

- Avoiding failure is in our genes, but it means we can miss many opportunities.
- Banning failure means banning progress.
- One should celebrate the lesson, not the failure.
- Musk's SpaceX suffered three failures – only the fourth attempt succeeded, and between attempts 3 and 4 only a single number was changed.
- Most things look positive at first glance and negative at second glance (e.g. spending money instead of saving for retirement) – the solution is delayed gratification.
- Focus not on the outcome but on the input: a bad decision can produce a good result, and a good decision can produce a bad result due to a random event.
- Every time a mistake is made, it helps to say: "That's interesting."
- Psychological safety: employees are not afraid to report failures, ask questions, or raise doubts.

## 9. Nothing fails like success

- Challenger disaster (28 January 1986): engineer Roger Boisjoly warned about problems with the O-ring seal; the first teacher to fly was Christa McAuliffe; Richard Feynman conducted a demonstration experiment in front of viewers.
- Columbia disaster (1 February 2003): the shuttle had been in space for 16 days; during launch a piece of insulating foam broke off and struck the wing, puncturing the thermal shield; engineer Rocha raised the alarm because cameras had recorded the impact, but management dismissed the concern.
- Success is a wolf in sheep's clothing – when we achieve it, we assume everything is fine.
- After completing a project, one should analyse what role luck played in the success.
- Near-miss situations should be subjected to analysis.
- Premortem: assume failure and search for what could have gone wrong.
- Risk homeostasis: reducing risk in one place increases it in another.

## Epilogue

- Only 66 years passed between the Wright brothers' first flight (1903) and the Moon landing.
