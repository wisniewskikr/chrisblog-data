# Content

## Introduction

- When jumping into water from a great height, a person dies because the molecules do not have time to move apart.
- Liquid is a state of matter between gas and solid.

## Explosive

- Kerosene was discovered by the Persian scholar Rhazes.
- Kerosene produces no smoke, which was revolutionary.
- The invention did not catch on in Persia, where olive oil was the dominant fuel.
- An oil lamp works through capillary action (surface tension) – the oil burns, not the wick.
- Surface tension: molecules at the surface of a liquid act under different conditions than those deeper inside.
- Plants use surface tension to absorb water; microfibre materials exploit it as well.
- Kerosene lamps were invented, supplying more oxygen; before that, whale oil was used.
- Sobrero invented nitroglycerin, and his student Nobel turned it into dynamite.

## Poisonous

- Yeast consumes sugar and excretes alcohol.
- Alcohol is toxic – it slows down the nervous system.
- Ethanol is less toxic; methanol is more toxic.
- Smell accounts for the greater part of taste.
- Colour influences taste perception: strawberry ice cream coloured yellow is judged by people to be peach-flavoured.
- Alcohol suppresses the hormones that tell the kidneys to conserve water, making dehydration easy.

## Deep

- An object floats or sinks depending on whether it weighs less or more than the same volume of water.
- The water in the Dead Sea is denser (more saline), making it easier to float.
- Oceans have a high heat capacity – they cool the surroundings in summer and warm them in winter.
- Waves are a portion of wind energy transferred to water.
- When water retreats from a beach, one should run inland – a large wave, such as a tsunami after an earthquake, is approaching.
- At Fukushima, the tsunami wave destroyed the generators cooling the fuel rods.

## Sticky

- Stickiness is a property describing how a substance behaves when it comes into contact with different materials.
- Paint is coloured glue.
- The oldest known adhesive was made from birch bark.
- Resins protect plants from insects.
- Next came animal-based glues, which could be loosened with water.
- Baekeland invented plastic.

## Fantastic

- Watercolour: the water evaporates and the pigment remains.
- Oil painting: oil reacts with air and hardens into a solid (goes rancid); the drawback is a long drying time.
- Linoleum: wood coated with oil that sets and protects it.
- Liquid crystals: partly ordered, partly fluid; above 35°C they become fully liquid.
- Colours on screens: hues placed close enough together to fool the eye into perceiving continuous colour.

## Organic

- pH scale: 0 – acidic liquid, 7 – neutral (water), 14 – alkaline.
- A human produces about 1 litre of saliva per day.
- There are three types of tears: basal, reflex, and emotional.

## Refreshing

- Alkaloids include caffeine, nicotine, morphine, and cocaine.
- Espresso – made exclusively with a pressure machine.
- Americano – espresso with added boiling water.
- Cappuccino – espresso with added milk foam.
- Flat white – espresso with added milk.

## Cleansing

- Soap = wood ash + water + tallow.
- Nurse Florence Nightingale popularised the principles of cleanliness.
- Procter & Gamble sponsored radio programmes, hence the name "soap operas".
- There is no scientific evidence for the effectiveness of antibacterial soap.
- Soap production creates high demand for palm oil.

## Refreshing (cooling)

- Planes fly at high altitudes where lower air resistance reduces fuel consumption.
- The transformation of a liquid into a gas cools the surroundings.
- A compressor converts the gas back into a liquid.
- Einstein worked on a refrigerator design.
- Midgley invented Freon and also added lead to petrol.
- Planes are cooled by outside air, which is why it is hot on the runway.

## Indelible

- The Egyptians were the first to use ink.
- A non-Newtonian liquid has no single viscosity parameter – usually thin, it thickens when stirred.
- This property is exploited by the ink in the ballpoint pen invented by Biro.

## Cloud

- About 1,000 people die from lightning strikes every year.
- About 100,000 flights take place every day.
- Nearly 1 million people are airborne at any given moment.

## Solid

- Liquid metals beneath the Earth's surface keep the atmosphere and water in place (via the magnetic field).
- The Earth's crust is between 30 and 100 kilometres thick.
- Antarctica and Greenland are weighed down by an enormous layer of ice.
- Sea levels have already risen by about 20 cm.

## Renewable

- Asphalt is a liquid, but an extremely viscous one.
- 3D printing: a liquid is transformed layer by layer into a solid.
