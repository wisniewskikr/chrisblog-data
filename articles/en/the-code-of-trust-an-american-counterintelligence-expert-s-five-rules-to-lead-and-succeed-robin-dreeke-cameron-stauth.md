# Content

## Trust is practically the foundation of everything we value. It is the basis of human relations.

### Part I. The leadership power of trust

### 1. Beyond manipulation: the code of trust

- One rule: prove that you are worthy of trust.
- To build trust, put others first.
- Goals must be shared – that is what distinguishes trust from manipulation.
- Trust should be built on rationality, not emotions – building on emotions is like building sandcastles.
- Do not judge people, but accept them; treat them with humility and affirm their worth.
- Five rules of trust: (1) Suppress your ego, (2) Do not judge, (3) Validate the worth of others, (4) Be thoughtful, (5) Be generous.
- Four steps to gaining trust: (1) Align your goals, (2) Apply the power of context, (3) Shape your meetings, (4) Connect.

## 2. The five rules of engagement

- True goals should be internal, not external – that is true success.
- Internal qualities: unwavering optimism, complete self-confidence, lasting peace of mind, a deep sense of security, high self-esteem, genuine love.
- To achieve these goals, you must help others achieve their dreams.
- The secret to being liked: make others feel good about themselves.

## 3. Cracking the code

- Be humble: loved by many, liked by almost everyone.
- Do not judge: encourage people to act by asking questions so they return to their main goal on their own.
- Validate others' worth: best done by confirming you would do the same yourself; also, often bite your tongue.
- Be thoughtful: in real life, the heroes are the peacemakers.
- Be generous: sharing is in our genes, it enabled survival, and it gives far more than taking.

## 4. The chemistry of trust: it's all about us

- You need to know about every issue before it becomes a problem, and about every problem before it becomes a disaster.
- Trust is a feeling.
- Responses: fight – flight – freeze.
- In type-A people, vigor often turns into aggression.
- Strategies that support trust: healthy diet, plenty of sleep, regular exercise, minimizing toxins, daily relaxation, spending time with loved ones.
- What you hold on to stays; what you let go of leaves; what you do is entirely up to you.

## Part II. Building trust: four steps

### 5. Step one: align your goals

- Maslow's needs: physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem and recognition, self-actualization.

## 6. Step two: apply the power of context

- You must communicate with people in their context.
- Personality types: dominant A, compliant B.
- Behavioral styles: dominance, influence, stability, conscientiousness.
- Learning styles: visual, auditory, kinesthetic.

## 7. Step three: shape the meeting

- Trust is like a glass hammer – effective, but if used incorrectly, it shatters.
- How to start a meeting: (1) set a time limit, (2) ask for help, (3) offer something (a small gift or a compliment), (4) stay on topic – talk about them, not yourself, (5) empower and affirm their worth, (6) manage your expectations, (7) explain your intentions skillfully – vocabulary, tone, pace, and body language all matter.
- Prepare for various possibilities: potatoes, bullets, compresses.
- Knowledge is truth, and truth builds trust; key questions: who, what, when, where, why.
- Halo effect: people trust those who are attractive and charming.

## 8. Step four: connect

- Communication techniques: (1) guide the conversation with questions, (2) influence the outcome through active listening, (3) enrich communication with nonverbal signals.
- Expressing yourself through questions is better – questions are not judgmental.
- How to share an opinion: "What you think is very reasonable, but I believe I know another aspect of this matter" or "I generally like your idea, and here are a few suggestions that may be helpful."
- Avoid presenting opinions as facts.
- When you say "I'm sorry, but…" you signal that you have messed up.

- An effective response to a difficult comment: "That is an interesting perspective. Thank you for sharing these thoughts with me, because I had never thought about it that way before."
