# Content

## Introduction

- The principles of science: test ideas through experiments and observations.
- Follow proven ideas, reject unproven ones.
- Question everything, even authorities.

## Chapter 1.

- Proxima Centauri is the nearest neighbouring star – it lies 4 light-years away.
- The cosmos is 13.82 billion years old; translated into a human calendar: one month equals over a billion years, one day – 38 million years, one hour – almost 2 million years, one minute – 26,000 years, one second – 440 years.
- Earth formed around 31 August, life on 2 September, plants on 2 December, mammals on 26 December (small shrews with a cerebral cortex).
- Homo sapiens appeared on 31 December at around 7 p.m.; 7 million years ago 13 atoms changed in one of our ancestors – one gene responsible for the growth and folding of the cerebral cortex.
- At 23:52, Africa was home to 10,000 people; Blombos Cave and the first art in ochre.
- Domestication of plants and animals.
- The city of Çatalhöyük in present-day Turkey – one of the first cities; no doors, windows, or streets were invented there – people entered through the roof.
- Antonie van Leeuwenhoek was the first to see microorganisms through a magnifying glass – Amsterdam.
- Christiaan Huygens, Leeuwenhoek's friend, brought the stars closer to us – Amsterdam.
- Baruch Spinoza began making lenses and wrote the "Theologico-Political Treatise"; he was expelled from Amsterdam to The Hague.
- Five great extinctions: Ordovician, Devonian, Permian, Triassic, Cretaceous; the sixth is the Anthropocene.

## 2. Chapter 2

- Fire was first controlled at Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa.
- René Descartes, 1619; his maxim: "Cogito, ergo sum"; he believed that to determine whether something truly exists, it must be examined through science.
- Ashoka: a cruel ruler who became a follower of the Buddha.

## 3. Chapter 3

- In the beginning there were cyanobacteria, which shared the world with anaerobes; because cyanobacteria released oxygen, the anaerobes perished.
- Oxygen combined with methane to form CO₂, the planet cooled, and this brought about the demise of the cyanobacteria.
- Goldschmidt founded geochemistry; he was fascinated by a material resistant to fire – olivine.
- Only 3 bodies in the Solar System have category 5 probability of life: Earth, Europa (moon of Jupiter), and Enceladus (moon of Saturn).

## 4. Chapter 4

- We domesticated plants and animals 10,000 years ago.
- Gregor Mendel in Brno, Czech lands, discovered hereditary factors by studying peas: dominant and recessive.
- Key figures in the history of biology: Darwin, Mendel, and Lamarck (discovered invertebrates, but his theory of inheritance of acquired traits was wrong); Vavilov (founded a seed bank) and Bateson (coined the term "genetics").

## Chapter 5

- Hippocrates – the first Greek physician, around 400 BCE; the first to seek the cause of illness in nature rather than the gods; the first to identify the brain, not the heart, as the seat of consciousness.
- In 1861 in France, surgeon Broca performed an autopsy on a patient known as "Tan" (the only word he could say) and discovered damage in the left hemisphere – the speech centre now called "Broca's area".
- Italian scientist Mosso was the first to record brain activity.
- Berger discovered the EEG – a machine that analyses electrochemical signals in the brain.
- Flatworms are probably the first predators and the first to develop something resembling a brain.

## Chapter 6

- Gerard Kuiper, the son of a poor tailor, gained admission to the University of Leiden in the Netherlands.
- Urey also came from a poor family in the USA; he was a chemist and received the Nobel Prize for the discovery of deuterium.
- Carl Sagan was a student of both of them.

## Chapter 7

- Mycelium networks support communication throughout an entire forest.
- Acacia trees, when giraffes appear, can change the taste of their leaves and warn other trees through chemical signals.
- An oak tree can detect a caterpillar, analyse its DNA, and release defensive chemicals.
- Wasps have existed for 250 million years and are carnivorous; bees are their relatives and switched to vegetarianism.
- Bees can communicate the location of food using a symbolic language; scout bees can accurately calculate the size of a tree hollow – too small or too large and the swarm will die.

## Chapter 8

- Diamond rain may fall on Uranus.
- Uranus is tilted – it was probably struck by two large meteorites.
- Kondratyuk invented the gravitational slingshot manoeuvre.

## Chapter 9

- Newton believed light consists of particles; Young argued that light is a wave.
- Huygens invented the pendulum clock and probability theory.
- Thomson discovered electrons.
- Dimensions: zero – a point; one – a line; two – a plane (front, back, left, right); three – our world.
- When a quantum is split in half, the two halves are connected by what is called quantum entanglement: they can be billions of light-years apart and the motion of one affects the other, apparently exceeding the speed of light; the bond is broken only by observing one of them.
- Superdeterminism: everything was predetermined from the very beginning – this is one explanation for the interaction of entangled particles.

## Chapter 10

- Szilárd and Teller went to Einstein to have him sign a letter warning of the threat of an atomic bomb.
- Teller was a keen supporter of the bomb, pushed for the construction of a thermonuclear bomb, and stripped Oppenheimer of access to materials.
- A volcano erupted in Saint-Pierre, Martinique; the only survivor was a man called Samson, who was locked in a punishment cell for fighting.
- Uranium is highly unstable – eventually a particle always escapes, transforming it into another element: thorium.

## Chapter 11

- Earth moves through the Sun's habitable zone at a rate of 30 cm per year; we have already spent 70% of the time away from its centre, but we still have hundreds of millions of years to devise an escape plan.
- The Sun will first become a red giant, then a small white dwarf.
- A possible refuge will be Triton, a moon of Neptune (a day lasts 144 hours, winter – 50 years).
- Space telescope: the possibility of using the Sun's gravitational lensing of light rays as a lens.
- Alcubierre drive: compresses spacetime at the front and expands it at the back; faster than light because the ship itself does not move – space moves.

## Chapter 12

- Cattle and rice produce methane.
- Freon: ultraviolet radiation strikes freon and knocks off chlorine atoms; one chlorine atom can destroy 100,000 ozone molecules.
- Manabe, as early as the 1950s, predicted the consequences of global warming: more frequent floods, dying coral reefs, stronger storms, heatwaves, and wildfires.

## Chapter 13

- Einstein's words from the 1939 World's Fair: "If science, like art, is to perform its mission fully and properly, its achievements must enter not superficially but deeply into the minds of the people."
