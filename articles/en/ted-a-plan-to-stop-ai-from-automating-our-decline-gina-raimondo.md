# Content

- The US needs a strategy not only for AI technology, but also for society as a whole.
- Three commonly discussed responses are insufficient on their own:
  1. Stopping or heavily regulating AI — risky, as it could allow China to surge far ahead.
  2. Universal basic income — problematic, because work is more than a paycheck: it provides dignity, personal growth, and a sense of contribution.
  3. Doing nothing and hoping the market adapts.
- A better path combines education with industry: training programmes must be more effective and aligned with real-world needs.
- Lifelong learning is essential — the old model of schooling once and working for life no longer holds; continuous upskilling will be required.
- Workers should receive government income support during the period when they are retraining for new skills.
- Proposed government measures:
  1. Schools should be funded based on outcomes — receiving support only when graduates find jobs and possess useful skills.
  2. Incentives for businesses to retrain and retain workers rather than lay them off.
- The most important task for government: plan the transition from old technology to AI in a way that is as smooth and society-friendly as possible.
