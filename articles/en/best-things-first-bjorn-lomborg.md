# Content

## 1. First things first
- Instead of trying to do everything at once, we should focus on the most important matters.

## 2. Promises, promises
- The Millennium Development Goals improved the world.
- The goal of responsible consumption and production conflicts with other goals, such as health and education.

## 3. Implement the smartest solutions first
- The selection of policies is based on cost-benefit analysis.
- Only policies that return at least 15 dollars for every dollar spent are considered.
- Average costs amount to 41 billion USD per year.
- These policies save 4.2 million lives per year and generate 1.1 trillion dollars in benefits.
- Steve Jobs: innovation is saying "no" to a thousand things.

## 4. Tuberculosis
- Tuberculosis kills 1.4 million people per year in poor countries.
- The solution costs 6.2 billion USD per year.
- TB spreads through coughing and became more dangerous as populations became denser.
- The BCG vaccine (1921) is effective only in children and reduces the risk by about 40%.
- Streptomycin was discovered in 1944.
- The HIV epidemic caused TB mortality to rise again in poor countries.
- A key challenge is the need to take tablets for 4–6 months — incentives for patients help.
- Treatment is costly; better screening and genetic testing improve outcomes.

## 5. Education
- In poor countries, children attend school but do not acquire sufficient skills.
- Student strategy: group pupils by knowledge level rather than age.
- A tablet used for one hour a day, shared among students, is an effective tool.
- Teacher strategy: structured pedagogy — ready-made lesson plans.

## 6. Maternal and child health
- Increasing access to emergency medical care, e.g. manual breathing-assist devices.
- Promoting hospital births.
- Greater availability of family planning services.

## 7. Agricultural research and development: more affordable food
- The two main solutions are economic growth and agricultural innovation.
- Economic growth: once income exceeds 10,000 USD per person, hunger virtually disappears.
- Innovation: crop yields have quadrupled since 1960.
- Norman Borlaug — agronomist and Nobel Peace Prize laureate — introduced high-yield crop varieties in many countries.

## 8. Malaria
- Malaria occurs where there is standing water and population density.
- Female mosquitoes transmit a parasite that travels to the liver and multiplies.
- As early as the 17th century, the Spanish brought powder from the cinchona tree from the Americas.
- Draining swamps and improving nutrition also reduced malaria.
- The insecticide DDT was invented and artemisinin was discovered in China.
- Insecticide-treated mosquito nets are an effective protection method.
- Solution: wider use of DDT-treated mosquito nets.

## 9. E-procurement: reducing corruption
- 23% of firms report encountering corruption in public procurement.
- Cronyism is an additional problem.
- Moving procurement online reduces corruption, increases the number of bidders, and speeds up adaptation to change.
- It also lowers the costs of announcing tenders.
- The four main elements of the system: notifications, access, credentials, and submission of bids.
- Challenge: implementation costs arise upfront, while benefits materialise only after several years.

## 10. Nutrition
- The first 1,000 days of a child's life are critical for development.
- Deficiencies during this period result in stunted growth and many other negative health consequences.
- Solution: supplementation during pregnancy.
- From around 6 months of age, a child needs more nutrients than breast milk alone provides.
- SQ-LNS is a lipid-based supplement designed for young children.

## 11. Chronic diseases
- Chronic diseases include heart disease and the effects of tobacco, alcohol, and drug abuse.
- People at risk of heart disease can take aspirin as a preventive measure.
- Chronic heart failure is treated with diuretic tablets that help the kidneys remove water and salt.
- Tobacco taxes and regulations banning advertising and smoking in public places reduce smoking rates.
- The recommended daily salt intake is 5 g — less than one teaspoon.

## 12. Childhood vaccinations
- Jenner noticed that women working with cows contracted a mild form of cowpox and did not develop smallpox. He placed infected material under a boy's skin, immunising him.
- The Latin word for cow is *vacca* — the origin of the word *vaccination*.

## 13. More trade
- Free trade brings general benefits: it lowers prices, gives producers economies of scale, and increases innovation.
- The smaller and poorer the country, the greater the gains from trade.
- Downsides of free trade: downward pressure on wages, job losses, and difficulty re-entering the labour market.

## 14. High-skilled worker migration
- A 10% increase in this type of migration boosts productivity and reduces inequality.
- The place premium is enormous: McDonald's workers earn 16 times more in the USA than in India.

## 15. Land tenure security
- In poorer countries, land ownership rights are often unregulated.
- 70% of people worldwide lack access to official land registration systems.
- Insecure property rights discourage investment and lead to land conflicts.
- This problem was successfully resolved in Rwanda.
