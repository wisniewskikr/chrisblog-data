# Content
- The sense of self feels constant: it appears when you wake up and fades when you fall asleep.
- We construct and tell ourselves — and others — a story about who we are; in a sense, we *are* those stories.
- Short-term memory forms first and later consolidates into long-term memory; Alzheimer's disease typically attacks short-term memory first, disrupting this narrative self.
- Foreign limb syndrome is a condition in which a person does not recognize a part of their own body (e.g., a hand) as belonging to them, showing that the bodily self can break down.
- In schizophrenia, a person may perform an action yet feel no sense of agency — they do not experience the action as driven by their own will.
- Scientists estimate that about 5% of people will have an out-of-body experience at some point in their lives, further illustrating how the self is not fixed.
- Self-awareness is a construction built from multiple layers of "story" anchored in the body — and that construction can fracture due to conditions such as schizophrenia, Alzheimer's, and others.
