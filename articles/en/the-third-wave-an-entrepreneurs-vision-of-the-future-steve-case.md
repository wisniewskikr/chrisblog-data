# Content

## Three waves of the internet

- First wave (1985–1999): building the internet
- Second wave (2000–2015): the app economy and the mobile revolution
- Third wave (from 2016): the internet of everything

## 1. The winding path

- Case comes from Hawaii; his father was a lawyer, his mother a teacher, and his brother Dan was a year older.
- He studied at Williams College in Massachusetts.
- He worked at Procter & Gamble in the marketing department, then at Pizza Hut in Kansas.
- His first start-up was CVC – online games.
- He then struck a deal with Apple.

## 2. America Online

- Apple pulled out but paid $3 million in compensation.
- With that money, America Online (AOL) was founded.
- AOL was the first company to make the internet a way of life.

## 3. The third wave

- The third wave will likely require investors and perseverance, much like the first wave.
- It may bring breakthroughs in medicine, education, and financial institutions.

## 4. Start acceleration

- The biggest concern was Microsoft, which introduced a default modem.
- AOL managed to defend itself nonetheless.

## 5. The three Ps

- Three key elements: partnership, policy, perseverance.

### 1. Partnership

- African proverb: "If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together."
- Large partners lend credibility.

### 2. Policy

- Policy creates regulations that third-wave companies will have to follow.

### 3. Perseverance

## 6. Excuse me for disrupting

- Large companies will have a significant advantage during the third wave, but they must be willing to use it.
- They should think about both the needs of current and future customers.

## 7. The rise of the rest

- "The rest" refers to areas of the USA outside Silicon Valley.
- During the third wave, these regions will develop.
- Industry specialists will matter more than IT specialists.

## 8. Impact investing

- Social engagement will become very important.
- Companies will look not only at profit but also at social benefits.
- Both employees and customers will expect this.

## 9. The trust issue

- AOL merged with Time Warner, but the merger failed due to mutual distrust and cultural differences between the companies.
- The dot-com bubble also burst.
- After leaving AOL, Case founded an investment firm called Revolution.

## 10. The visible hand

- The government will play the role of both regulator and customer.

## 11. America at a crossroads

- Two types of companies: start-ups (which can grow quickly and disrupt the existing order) and small businesses (which grow slowly and develop over the long term).
- The presidential advisory council should be reduced from 22 to 7 members; a "third-wave czar" is needed.
- Investment in research and development – both public and private – is declining as short-term goals are prioritised over long-term ones.
- Financing for start-ups and the recruitment of specialists should be made easier.
