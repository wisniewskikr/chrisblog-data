# Content

## I. The world has changed

- Climatic, population, energy, and economic extremes are shaping today's reality.
- Until 1650 the human population remained stable at around 500 million.
- From 1650 onward rapid growth began; by 1804 the population reached 1 billion.
- The economic extreme encompasses currency reserves and debt.

## II. It is not a lack of solutions

- The problem is not a lack of solutions.

## III. The turning point

- A crisis passes through a turning point that can become either a catastrophe or a change.
- Turning points can be spontaneous or intentional.
- Useful diagnostic questions:
  - Am I happy in the relationship?
  - Is this a healthy relationship?
  - Is there a chance for improvement?

## IV. Entering the turning point

- Resilience is the ability to recover from difficult experiences and to adapt to adverse conditions.
- Optimism and hope help one survive a crisis.
- A person can operate in either a "grow/thrive" mode or a "fight/flee" mode.

## V. The next level

- A strong community is built on a shared vision and connection.
- Globalization encompasses economic, political, cultural, ecological, and ideological dimensions.
- Societal resilience is built from: reserves, flexibility, limited scope of a problem, rapid reconstruction, and continuous exchange of information.

## VI. On the transformed world — seriously

- Three types of transformation are distinguished: catastrophic transformation, planned reset, and evolutionary transformation.
- The main challenge: improving the lives of 8 or more billion people without devastating the environment.
- Key planetary systems: climate, land, fresh water, oceans, biodiversity, the phosphorus cycle, the nitrogen cycle, and the ozone layer.
- A proposal to replace uranium with thorium as an energy source.
