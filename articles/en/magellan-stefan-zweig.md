# Content

## Introduction

- Magellan set sail with 5 ships.
- 265 men departed; 18 returned.

## Navigare necesse est

- Europeans were seeking spices.
- Malacca, near present-day Singapore, was the spice trading hub.
- From there, spices were transported by ship to Arabia or Egypt, from where caravans travelled to Beirut or Cairo.
- The Venetians required the goods to be unloaded at the Rialto.
- Spices had to pass through 12 middlemen's hands.
- Christians could not travel through Arab countries.
- Ptolemy claimed it was impossible to sail around Africa.
- Portugal bordered Spain on one side and had the sea on the other — it needed room for expansion.
- As the westernmost country, Portugal could become the gateway to the Indies.
- Prince Henry the Navigator devoted his entire life to seafaring, though he himself sailed only once — to Ceuta.
- He lived at Cape Sagres, where he planned voyages; knowledge of exploration had been lost during the Middle Ages.
- Cape Sagres was later plundered by Francis Drake.
- Henry dies but opens the way for others.
- Under King John II, Bartolomeo Dias reached the Cape of Good Hope in 1486, and Vasco da Gama sailed to India.
- John II refused Columbus, who therefore sailed under the Spanish flag and reached America in 3 weeks.
- The Pope divided the world between Portugal and Spain.

## Magellan in India

- Expeditions came in waves: first exploration, then trade, finally conquest.
- Almeida received 20 ships to conquer the East; Magellan, then about 24 years old, sailed with him.
- He probably came from Porto and was of noble birth.
- He was one of 1,500 soldiers, and nothing is known about him from this period.
- On 16 March 1506, at the Battle of Cannanore, 200 Muslim ships attacked 11 Christian ones; the traveller Varthema warned the Christians.
- Magellan is wounded and returns to Lisbon.
- He then sails to Malacca under Sequeira's command, ostensibly for trade but in reality on a military reconnaissance.
- They are attacked in Malacca, losing a third of their crew and their boats, but manage to escape.
- Magellan warns the captains as a messenger and saves his friend Serrao.
- He ran a spice-laden ship onto rocks but did not flee by boat — he stayed on board with the crew.
- Magellan is promoted to officer.
- Albuquerque sails to avenge Sequeira and captures Malacca after 6 weeks.
- Serrao sails on to the Spice Islands with 3 ships, but his vessel is wrecked and he remains on the islands.
- Serrao invites Magellan to join him — this may have given Magellan the idea of travelling westward rather than eastward.
- After 7 years, Magellan returns to Lisbon, which is now completely transformed and very wealthy.

## Magellan strikes out on his own

- Magellan receives a modest noble pension at court.
- He takes part in an expedition to Morocco and is wounded in the leg, leaving him permanently lame.
- Cattle under his guard were stolen; he was accused of collusion with the enemy.
- He travelled to Portugal to seek justice but was treated as a deserter and sent back to Africa.
- There he is officially cleared of the charges and discharged from the army.
- He returns to Portugal once more.
- Magellan was an eternal loner — cold and withdrawn.
- At about 35, he asks King Manuel for: a pay rise, command of a ship, and permission to serve another court. The king agrees only to the last.
- He spends a year in Portugal and befriends the theorist Ruy Faleiro.
- He goes to the King of Spain, convinced he knows of a passage; he had found reference to it in the Portuguese king's library — from the cartographer Behaim, who had learned of it from Portuguese merchants. Those merchants had, however, discovered the River Plate, not the Strait of Magellan.

## The dream becomes reality

- Spain is interested because Magellan promises the shortest route to the Spice Islands as well as legal title to them under the papal division of the world.
- For this, Magellan is to become a traitor to Portugal.
- Magellan travels to Seville to meet Charles V.
- He gains the support of the former Portuguese merchant Barbosa and marries his daughter.
- He submits his project to the Casa de Contratación, but it is initially rejected.
- One of the three examining experts, Aranda, wants to hear more.
- A partnership is formed: Magellan — practical matters, Faleiro — theory, Aranda — connections.
- An audience with the king in Valladolid ends successfully.
- The merchant Haro supports Magellan.
- In 1518 a contract is signed.

## One will against a thousand obstacles

- Magellan prepares 5 ships.
- Portugal tries every means to sabotage the expedition.

## Departure

- The expedition departs on 20 September 1519.
- Magellan sails on the flagship "Trinidad".
- The Spanish captains are hostile towards him.
- Pigafetta — an idealist — serves as the expedition's chronicler.
- Magellan bids farewell to his wife and son.

## Futile searches

- The ships developed a communication system to stay in contact.
- Captains were required to report to Magellan daily.
- Magellan did not explain his chosen course to the Spanish captains, fearing a conspiracy.
- He has one captain imprisoned for asking why the course was changed.
- After 11 weeks they arrive at the bay of Rio de Janeiro.
- They reach the River Plate; Magellan believes it to be the Strait of Magellan.
- After 13 days it is discovered to be a river.
- Magellan presses southward in search of a passage, then decides to winter at San Julián.

## Mutiny

- Magellan invites the captains to an Easter feast; they do not come.
- The Spanish captains seize the ship commanded by Magellan's cousin and put all the Portuguese in irons.
- They do not attack Magellan directly, having sworn an oath and not wishing to be called traitors.
- They send Magellan a letter demanding explanations.
- Magellan sends a boat with 5 men; its commander kills the Spanish captain as he reads Magellan's letter.
- Magellan succeeds in imprisoning the Spanish captains.
- One captain is beheaded; two others — a captain and a priest — are put ashore on the coast.

## The great moment

- They wintered at San Julián for 4–5 months.
- They encountered a man with large feet — a Patagonian; they captured the natives by trickery, but those captives died on the voyage.
- Magellan sent one ship on reconnaissance; it was wrecked on the rocks.
- At the mouth of the Santa Cruz river they stopped for another 2 months, being only 2 days' sail from the Strait of Magellan.
- They finally found the passage and took a month to navigate it, as it is a true labyrinth.
- One ship deserted; the remaining three decide to sail on.

## Magellan discovers his kingdom

- Magellan named the new ocean "El Pacífico" (the Pacific) on account of its calm.
- They sail for 3 months and 20 days suffering terrible hunger.
- They reach the Islands of Thieves, where the natives steal from them.
- A week later they discover the Philippines.
- Under the terms of his contract, the discoverer of 6 new islands receives 2 of them — making Magellan one of the richest men in the world.
- On Good Friday they arrive at Massawa.
- Enrique, Magellan's slave, understands words spoken by the locals — making him the first person to have truly circumnavigated the Earth.

## Death before the ultimate triumph

- Magellan reaches Cebu.
- The natives ask to be baptised of their own accord.
- Magellan supports the king of Cebu in a battle against a neighbouring raja, wishing to demonstrate his power.
- He is killed in the skirmish because the boats cannot come close enough to the shore.
- The natives refuse to return his body.

## The voyage home without a guide

- The servant Enrique is offended and, together with the king of Cebu, plans revenge.
- The crew is attacked by treachery.
- The new captain, Carvalho, abandons one of the captains without aid.
- Short-handed, the Spanish scuttle the "Concepción".
- After wandering, they finally reach the Spice Islands.
- The two remaining ships part ways: "Trinidad" needs repairs, while "Victoria" cannot wait for favourable winds — she is commanded by Sebastián del Cano, who had previously conspired against Magellan.
- "Victoria" cannot use Portuguese ports, as the King of Portugal has ordered that the Spaniards be treated as pirates; they sail without stopping.
- Racked by hunger near the Cape Verde Islands, they deceive the Portuguese by claiming to have come from America.
- One of the Spaniards gives the game away; they are forced to flee with only 18 men on board.
- Pigafetta discovers they have lost one day in their calendar.
- On 6 September 1522, three years after departure, they return — only 18 men survive.
- The mutineers lost credibility by claiming that Magellan was dead and that the discovered strait was of no importance.
- Magellan's wife and children had died, so there is no one to inherit from him.
