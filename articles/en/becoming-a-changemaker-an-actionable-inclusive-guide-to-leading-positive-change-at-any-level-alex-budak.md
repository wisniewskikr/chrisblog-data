# Content

## Introduction

- 1440: Gutenberg and the invention of the printing press
- 1608: invention of the telescope
- The next 90 years: the steam engine
- The next 100 years: the light bulb and the telegraph
- Moore's Law: the number of transistors in a microprocessor doubles every two years while cost falls by half
- Martec's Law: technological transformation is exponential, but organizations change logarithmically – hence a growing gap

## I. The changemaker mindset

### 1. Shaping the changemaker mindset

- Mindset: a fixed set of attitudes and beliefs held by a person
- Two types of mindset: fixed (innate, cannot be changed) and growth (can be developed)
- Three core building blocks of the changemaker mindset: (1) there is always another option – the status quo is not predetermined; (2) existing on the margins; (3) learned hope
- Learned optimism consists of: permanence (problems are not permanent), pervasiveness (separating bad experiences from other aspects of life), and personalization (sometimes the fault is not yours)
- Three levers of change: become a changemaker, surround yourself with changemakers, help others become changemakers

## 2. Questioning the status quo

- EJI overturned 125 criminal convictions in Alabama
- Principles for questioning the status quo: curiosity (seeing the world a little differently from others), forging your own path, taking smart risks
- Hope is the enemy of injustice
- Convergent thinking: one specific answer; divergent thinking: many possible solutions
- Creative people should be like the letter T: the vertical stroke represents depth of knowledge, the horizontal stroke represents the ability to collaborate with specialists from other disciplines
- What limits curiosity: overloaded calendars, asking the wrong questions, micromanagement, hyper-specialization, lack of time for play
- Conformity experiment: 75% of participants gave the wrong answer to fit in with the group
- It is important to know when and where to question the status quo and when to take a risk versus when not to
- Risk quotient = benefits / losses; if greater than 3, it is worth taking the risk
- Idiosyncrasy credit: the degree to which a person makes a positive impression on a group – going along with the majority on matters where you are unsure, while maintaining professionalism in others

## 3. A sense of confidence and openness

- Duality: a strong personal vision combined with openness to collaboration
- Humility is not a weakness – it is linked to better financial results and greater satisfaction
- The mirror and window metaphor: a person lacking humility looks in the mirror when praised and out the window when blamed; a humble person does the opposite
- Trust has never been more needed, yet never been in shorter supply
- Trust is not binary; it has three pillars: (1) trusting yourself (e.g. making your bed – the first small completed task, habits that build trust); (2) trusting others (start with small steps, trust but verify, clearly define the "what" but stay open on the "how", guard against the negative consequences of risk); (3) earning the trust of others (extend your own trust first, be tough on ideas but gentle on people, provide transparency, show vulnerability – which we see as a weakness in ourselves but a strength in others)
- Two axes of influence: assertiveness (your needs) and cooperativeness (others' needs) – both should be at a high level
- It is better to start slowly and establish values in order to work faster later

## 4. Going beyond yourself

- Serving others (servant leadership) in the right way (ethical leadership), over the long term (long-term thinking), toward a defined goal (vision)
- Elements: (1) serving others – officers eat last; ask whether those you serve are growing as individuals, what is getting in their way, and how you can help; (2) ethics – authenticity, consistency, and inclusivity; (3) long-term thinking – a new habit takes 66 days to form, it is a long-term relay race, a game without end; (4) vision – the ability to foresee the future, seek inspiration from others, and listen to feedback on your own vision

## 5. Always students

- You need a goal and must pursue it flexibly: a clear "why", a flexible "how"
- Three types of flexibility: (1) cognitive – the ability to use different strategies and mental frameworks; (2) emotional – varied ways of handling your own emotions and those of others; (3) dispositional – maintaining optimism while remaining realistic
- Empathy spreads through a group
- Self-care is like sharpening the saw
- Categories of self-renewal: physical, social, mental, and spiritual (nature, meditation, service)

## II. Changemaker leadership

### 6. The new face of leadership

- Leadership is the ability to create meaningful things through and with other people
- Many people have learned good leadership by observing bad leadership
- Key skills: (1) you do not need to wait for permission – act, not just think, do not wait for a formal appointment; (2) leadership through networks – research shows 25% of people must change to create a tipping point; (3) purpose-driven leadership – people are willing to give up 25% of their income to have purpose, vision and values matter; (4) listening leadership – insightful and constructive questions

## 7. Micro-leadership

- Four keys: (1) believe it, be it – see yourself as a leader; (2) give yourself permission; (3) serve others; (4) take action
- Leadership is made up of many small moments and is not the same as authority
- Types of people: visionary, first follower, early adopters, the rest
- Tools: seeing the best in others, building alliances, asking for advice
- Superpowers of influence: (1) relationships – build the relationship first, then influence; (2) vision – painting a picture of the future others want to follow; (3) empathy – what is in it for the other person; (4) passion and a positive attitude; (5) providing a sense of safety

## 8. Become your dream leader

- Be the kind of leader (boss, parent, etc.) you wished you had
- Inclusive leadership: drawing richness from diversity
- Bad habits: domination, favoritism, ignoring alternative opinions
- Good habits: commitment to inclusivity, humility, curiosity about others, cultural intelligence, effective collaboration, awareness of biases
- Psychological safety: the belief of team members that they can be themselves and express their opinions without risking their reputation or well-being
- Tips: ask others before making a decision; apply cognitive flexibility; celebrate "falling forward" (failure); be alert to unanimity
- Talent is evenly distributed around the world, but opportunities are not
- Diversity motivates us to work harder
- Six leadership styles to be applied depending on circumstances: (1) authoritative – for crisis situations, but kills creativity; (2) visionary – the leader sets the larger goal, leaving details to others, problematic when inexperienced; (3) affiliative – people first, problem: tolerates ineffective work; (4) democratic – "what do you think?", problem: chaotic meetings; (5) pace-setting – "do it the way I do", problem: standards may be too high; (6) coaching – "try it this way", problem: when people are not interested in changing

## III. Changemaker action

### 9. Initiating change: from idea to action

- Action is the best antidote to despair
- (Mindset + Leadership) × Action = Impact
- Fire bullets first, then cannonballs: start with small things
- Lean Startup: Idea → Minimum Viable Product → Evaluation

## 10. Leading change when change is hard

- Three groups of people during difficult change: (1) champions – want change; (2) cynics – do not want change; (3) the undecided – engage after 42%
- Normative entrepreneurship: creating standards that others begin to follow
- Theory of change: (1) what change do you want to make? (2) what tools do you want to use? (3) how will you know you have achieved the change?
- People tend to be motivated by avoiding losses rather than seeking gains
- People dislike being told what to do – ask questions instead, e.g. offer two proposals
- Ask for less at the beginning
- Take care of yourself

## 11. The changemaker launch template

- Template segments: (1) vision, (2) opportunity, (3) four indicators of change, (4) action, (5) community, (6) approach
- Vision: why you care and what the outcome should be; the Why → How → What framework; the "why" must be compelling, authentic, personal, and generate excitement
- Opportunity: first and foremost, understanding the problem; components: problem, consequences, causes
- Four indicators of change: significant impact (how to measure it), scalability, sustainability, systemic change
- Action: minimum viable functionality; resilience plan – what obstacles may arise and how to overcome them
- Community: implementers, evangelists, community members, allies
- Approach: changemaker mindset, changemaker leadership

## 12. Catalyzing your changemaker journey

- Life shrinks or expands in proportion to one's courage
