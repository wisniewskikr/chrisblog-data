# Content

## Part I. What is glucose?

### 1. Entering the cockpit

- Main goal: flattening the glucose curve.
- Glucose is not everything – exercise, social relationships and other factors also matter.
- Context is crucial: "healthy" always depends on the point of reference.
- Conclusions backed by scientific evidence.
- The body needs about 200 grams of glucose per day.

## 2. Meet Jerry

- Van Helmont confirmed that plants do not build their mass from soil – after 5 years of growing a seedling, the weight of the soil in the pot remained unchanged.
- Glucose is produced by plants from water and carbon dioxide; it serves as an energy source or building material.
- Types of glucose-based compounds:
  - **Starch**: linked glucose molecules used to store energy.
  - **Fibre**: linked glucose molecules used for structure; the body cannot break it down into glucose, but it plays an important role in digestion.
  - **Fructose**: 2–3 times sweeter than glucose, found in fruit to attract animals; unnecessary for the body.
  - **Sucrose**: a temporary energy store in fruit; half glucose, half fructose.

## 3. Family matters

- Alpha-amylase breaks starch down into glucose; humans can carry out this process.
- Carbohydrates = starch + fibre + sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose).
- Glucose can also be produced from protein.
- The body can switch to an alternative fuel – fat.

## 4. In search of pleasure

- Glucose is best consumed from plants, because they contain fibre, which slows its absorption.
- Manufacturers remove fibre from products because it shortens their shelf life.
- Sweet taste triggers dopamine release because historically it signalled: the food is safe and provides a lot of energy.
- Modern plants have been modified to contain more sucrose and less fibre.

## 5. Under the skin

- Normal fasting glucose level: 72–85 mg/dl.
- A post-meal glucose spike should not exceed 30 mg/dl.
- Spikes, rather than the baseline level, pose the greatest risk – they accumulate and ultimately raise fasting levels.
- The same spike caused by sucrose (e.g. a muffin) is more harmful than one caused by starch (e.g. rice) because of the fructose, whose levels we cannot yet measure.

## II. Why are glucose spikes unhealthy?

### 6. Trains, toast and Tetris

- Mitochondria convert glucose and oxygen into chemical energy.
- The amount of glucose supplied should be steady and match the amount being burned.
- Supplying glucose to mitochondria too quickly produces free radicals (oxidative stress), which can damage cells and contribute to cancer. Fructose is worse than glucose in this regard.
- Glycation: damage to other molecules caused by glucose – a normal process, but one that can be accelerated or slowed; fructose accelerates it 10 times faster than glucose.
- When glucose or fructose is delivered too quickly, the pancreas releases insulin, which stores them in:
  - the liver (glucose),
  - muscles (glucose),
  - fat tissue (glucose and fructose).
- Burning reserves only begins about 2 hours after the insulin spike.

## 7. From head to toe

- Short-term effects of glucose spikes: constant hunger, food cravings (intensified when blood sugar drops), chronic fatigue, sleep disturbances, colds, migraines.
- Long-term effects of glucose spikes: acne, accelerated ageing, dementia, cancer, depression.

## Part III. How to flatten the glucose curves?

### Hack 1. Eat foods in the right order

- Recommended order of eating meal components:
  1. Fibre (e.g. broccoli)
  2. Protein (e.g. nuts, beans)
  3. Fat (e.g. oils, avocado)
  4. Starch (e.g. pasta, bread, rice, potatoes)
  5. Sugars (e.g. fruit)
- Eating fibre first: limits alpha-amylase activity, slows stomach emptying, and forms a mesh in the small intestine that slows glucose absorption.
- No breaks between the different stages of the meal are necessary.
- Key rule: **carbohydrates last**.

## Hack 2. Start every meal with a green starter

- Recommended daily fibre intake: 25 grams.
- Fibre in bread is found in sourdough rye bread or pumpernickel.
- A green starter can be any vegetables, hummus or lentils.

## Hack 3. Stop counting calories

- Calories measure heat output, not the full nutritional picture.
- Instead of changing the caloric content of meals, it is better to change the type of food consumed.
- Reactive hypoglycaemia: after some time, glucose levels drop – due to insulin – below the starting level.
- A salad with dressing eaten after a meal not only does not cause weight gain but may actually support weight loss.

## Hack 4. Flatten your breakfast glucose curve

- Packaged cereals with milk are very unhealthy.
- Breakfast sets the tone for energy levels throughout the day.
- In the morning we are particularly sensitive to glucose spikes due to an empty stomach.
- Blended fruit loses some of its fibre.
- The daily sugar limit for men is 36 grams.
- The first meal should be based on protein, fat and fibre.

## Hack 5. Eat whatever type of sugar you like – they are all the same

- The type of sugar does not matter – they all act similarly.
- Dried fruit is not healthy: it contains a little fibre but mainly sugar.
- The glycaemic index only accounts for glucose, not fructose, which is more harmful.
- Best sweeteners: allulose, monk fruit, stevia, erythritol.
- Sugar is addictive, so it is worth limiting consumption.

## Hack 6. Have dessert instead of a sweet snack

- The postprandial state lasts about 4 hours after the last bite.
- Once the postprandial state ends, the body begins to "clean up" and regenerate its systems.
- It is important to train cells to use fat as fuel.

## Hack 7. Have some vinegar before eating

- A tablespoon of vinegar diluted in a glass of water, consumed from 20 minutes before to 20 minutes after a meal, reduces the glucose spike by 8–30%.
- Vinegar works in two ways: it temporarily deactivates alpha-amylase and accelerates glucose uptake by muscles.

## Hack 8. Move after a meal

- Walking is beneficial even up to 70 minutes after a meal; the effect is noticeable after just 10 minutes of movement.
- Recommended forms of activity: 10–20 minutes of walking or 10 minutes of strength exercises.
- Exercising before a meal is also beneficial.

## Hack 9. If you must snack, choose a savoury snack

- A savoury snack is a better choice than a sweet one.
- Examples of good snacks: an apple with peanut butter, nuts with dark chocolate, coconut chips.

## Hack 10. Don't eat naked carbs

- Fat, protein and fibre should be added to starches and sugars ("clothing" for carbohydrates).
- Examples of "clothing": peanut butter, almonds, nuts, vegetables, avocado, legumes, butter, cream, cheese, eggs, fish, meat.
- Examples of naked carbohydrates: bread, corn, couscous, pasta, rice, tortilla, sweets, fruit, ice cream, chocolate.
- Good saturated fats: animal fats, butter, ghee, coconut oil.
- Good monounsaturated fats: avocado, olives, fruits and nuts.
- Sources of fibre: vegetables, nuts, seeds.
- Sources of protein: nuts, seeds, legumes.

## Cheat sheets

- When a craving strikes, wait 20 minutes – during this time the liver kicks in and releases stored glucose.
- Alcohols causing minimal blood sugar spikes: dry wines, pure spirits (gin, vodka, tequila, whisky).
- Ingredients on a product label are listed in order of quantity – if sugar appears among the first five, it is better not to buy it.
- A good carbohydrate-to-fibre ratio is 5:1.
- Do not go grocery shopping when hungry.
- Different people may react differently to the same foods, but the described hacks work universally.
